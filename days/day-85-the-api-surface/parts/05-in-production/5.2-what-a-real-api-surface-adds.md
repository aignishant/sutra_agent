# 5.2 — What a real API surface adds

## One-line answer

The factory gives you twenty-seven working routes and none of the eight things that make a service
safe to expose — and two of the eight are single lines that this day has already measured the cost of
omitting.

## The story

What the landlord fixes before the tenant moves in.

The flat is finished. Walls painted, tiles laid, water running, power on. By any reasonable test it
is a flat, and somebody could live in it tonight.

The list the landlord works through before handing over the keys is not about any of that. A lock on
the front door that the previous tenant's key does not open. A working latch on the bathroom. The
gas connection checked and signed. A name on the meter so the bill goes to the right person.

None of it is construction. All of it is the difference between a building and a home somebody else
can safely occupy, and the reason it gets skipped is that the flat looks finished without it.

## The idea in plain language

Everything this day measured came out of an application that was *already working*. Twenty-seven
routes, sessions, artifacts, streaming, a generated schema. `get_fast_api_app` is not an incomplete
library and none of the findings in sections 3 and 4 are defects in it — the help text in part
[4.1](../04-blast-radius/4.1-the-caller-says-who-they-are.md) states the boundary plainly.

So the honest question is not *what is broken* but **what has to be added before this is a service
other people may call**. Eight things, in the order they should be done.

**1. An authentication dependency.** Validate a credential, yield a subject. Part
[4.1](../04-blast-radius/4.1-the-caller-says-who-they-are.md) measured a stranger reading and
deleting another user's session in three requests. Until this exists, everything else on this list is
decoration.

**2. An authorization check on `{user_id}`.** Compare the authenticated subject to the path
parameter, applied at the router across all fifteen routes that carry it — not per handler, because a
per-handler rule is a rule somebody will forget on route sixteen.

**3. `bind_host`, passed explicitly.** Part [4.3](../04-blast-radius/4.3-the-guard-in-the-box.md):
one argument, no cost to legitimate traffic, and without it a rebound page reaches a loopback server.
This is the cheapest item on the list.

**4. Named origins instead of a wildcard.** Part
[4.2](../04-blast-radius/4.2-which-pages-may-call-it.md): `allow_origins=["https://desk.example"]`,
or a `regex:` anchored to a domain you own. Same ten seconds as the wildcard.

**5. One exception handler, producing problem documents.** Parts
[3.1](../03-when-it-refuses/3.1-one-field-two-types.md) and
[3.2](../03-when-it-refuses/3.2-the-404-that-reads-out-the-disk.md) are one fix: a constructed body
in one shape, with a stable `type`, and the server's filesystem path in the log rather than the
response. Convert the framework's validation errors in the same change or the most common error keeps
the old shape.

**6. A readiness endpoint.** Part [5.1](5.1-health-is-not-readiness.md): `/ready` that touches the
session store and returns `503` when it cannot, on a different probe from `/health`.

**7. A declared media type on `/run_sse`.** Part
[2.3](../02-exercising-it-free/2.3-the-endpoint-that-sends-a-stream.md): the schema says
`application/json` and the handler sends `text/event-stream`. A few lines, and every generated client
inherits the mistake until it is done.

**8. 🅿️ Parked — rate limiting and admission control.** Day 78 built an admission decision for the
nightly; this API has no way to express it, so every caller gets in and the refusals come from the
provider as 429s. Parked because it needs the Flash-Lite allowance that Day 83's criterion 6 still
cannot determine — you cannot admit against a budget nobody has measured.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because tomorrow puts this in a container and Day 88 puts it behind a cluster, and every step after
today increases the number of things that can reach it. Items 1 to 4 are the ones that change meaning
as that happens: "trusted network" is true today, less true in a container, and false behind a
service.

And because items 3, 4 and 7 are each roughly one line. A list where three of the eight items are one
line is not a roadmap, it is an afternoon — and the reason they are not done is that nothing fails
without them.

## The mechanism

The list with what each costs, so it can be argued with:

| # | Item | Where it lands | Roughly | Measured in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | authentication dependency | `sutra/api.py` | a day, with a token issuer decided | part 4.1 |
| 2 | authorization on `{user_id}` | one router dependency | an hour | part 4.1 |
| 3 | `bind_host` passed explicitly | one argument | a minute | part 4.3 |
| 4 | named origins | one argument | a minute | part 4.2 |
| 5 | one exception handler | `sutra/api.py` and `tests/` | half a day | parts 3.1, 3.2, 3.3 |
| 6 | readiness endpoint | one route plus a store ping | an hour | part 5.1 |
| 7 | declared media type on `/run_sse` | one decorator argument | a few minutes | part 2.3 |
| 8 | 🅿️ rate limiting and admission | not built | needs a measured allowance | day 78, day 83 |

**Items 3, 4 and 7 are minutes between them**, and they close a rebinding vector, a CORS decision and
a client-generation bug. Item 2 is the one with the largest ratio of safety to effort, and it depends
entirely on item 1.

Items 1 and 2 are one function each in the shape FastAPI expects:

```python
async def caller(request: Request) -> str:
    """The authenticated subject, or a refusal. Nothing else in the app resolves identity."""
    ...


async def owns(user_id: str, subject: str = Depends(caller)) -> None:
    """Refuse when the authenticated subject is not the user named in the path."""
    if subject != user_id:
        raise DeskError(403, FORBIDDEN, "Not your session", "...")
```

**Line by line:**

- `caller` is the only place a credential is read. One function means one place to review, one place
  to test, and one place to change when the token format does.
- `owns` takes `user_id` as a parameter and FastAPI fills it from the path, so the dependency sees the
  same value the handler will. Re-reading it from the raw URL would be a second parse and a place for
  the two to disagree.
- It returns `None` and raises on failure. A dependency that returned a boolean would leave every
  handler responsible for checking it, which is item 2's whole point undone.
- `DeskError` is part [3.3](../03-when-it-refuses/3.3-an-error-with-a-handle-on-it.md)'s type, so the
  refusal is a problem document like every other. A `403` in a different shape from the rest of the
  API is a new client branch.
- Applied at the router — `APIRouter(dependencies=[Depends(owns)])` — so route sixteen inherits it.
  The `...` bodies are `TODO(me)` and stay that way: the hub's build brief is where they get written.

## When it breaks

The list breaks by being read as a backlog, and the composite has been measured piece by piece across
this day:

```text
1. the desk is served with the factory's defaults, on a laptop        (part 1.3)
2. a front end needs CORS, so allow_origins=["*"] goes in             (part 4.2)
3. bind_host is never passed, because the factory did not ask         (part 4.3)
4. a page the user visits reaches loopback and is allowed to read it
5. it calls /apps/.../users/<any name>/sessions, which needs nothing   (part 4.1)
6. the 404s it gets while guessing name the filesystem                 (part 3.2)
7. nothing in any log distinguishes this from ordinary use
```

Seven steps, every response a valid one, no error text anywhere. Steps 2 and 3 are the two arguments
from item 3 and item 4 — the minutes-long ones.

The step that no item on this list would have caught is step 7, and it is worth naming: none of the
eight items is *detection*. Every one of them prevents something, and a service with all eight would
still have no way to notice somebody trying. Day 84's tracing and Day 22's structured logging are
where that lives, and the field to log is the one part 4.3 named.

## In production

**What a professional does.** Items 3, 4 and 7 today, because they are minutes. Then item 5, because
it is the one that makes every later failure legible. Then items 1 and 2 together, before the service
is reachable by anything the team does not control — which in this project's plan is Day 88, not some
distant milestone.

**What degrades at scale.** Every item gets more expensive to retrofit and item 5 worst of all. Once
three clients exist, changing the error shape is a coordinated release; before any client exists it
is one commit. That is the general shape of everything on this list: the cost is measured in how many
callers already depend on the current behaviour.

**The failure that only shows with real traffic.** Item 8, the parked one. A service with perfect
authentication and no admission control is a service that faithfully authenticates every caller while
spending the day's model quota in the first hour — and then returns Day 78's `429
RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` to everybody, including the callers who were behaving. Access control decides who
may ask; it does not decide how much.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"Do three, four and seven before you go home — they
are single arguments and one of them closes a rebinding hole. Then the exception handler. One and two
go on the ticket for the container work, because that is the week 'trusted network' stops being
true."*

**The interview question:** *"What do you add to a framework's default server before exposing it?"*
The answer that shows experience starts with identity and works outward — who is calling, may they do
this, who may reach the port at all — and then names the error contract, because that is the item
teams postpone and the one that gets expensive with every client added.
