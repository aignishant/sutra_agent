# 4.2 — Flaky by construction

## One-line answer

💥 A judged case is a coin by construction, so quarantining the ones that flip is correct — and a
quarantine with no expiry is a list of checks somebody switched off: five here, the oldest **203 days**
old, and nothing in the process says so.

## The story

The out-of-order sign on the lift.

It went up on a Thursday because the lift was juddering, and the caretaker was right to put it up.
Somebody came, looked, said it needed a part, and the part had to be ordered.

That was a while ago. The sign is laminated now, because paper does not survive a year on a lift door.
Everybody in the building takes the stairs and has stopped noticing that they do.

Here is the thing nobody can tell you: is the lift still broken? The sign does not say when it went up.
Nobody remembers whether the part came. It might work fine. The information the sign carries has decayed
from *"this is broken"* to *"nobody has looked at this in a long time"*, and those are completely
different statements that look identical from the corridor.

## The idea in plain language

Part [4.1](4.1-a-red-build-is-a-decision.md) established the rule for a red build. This part is about
the cases that break the rule by being unreliable rather than wrong.

The unreliability is not a defect. It is a property of the metrics Phase 12 built, and it was measured
on the day each one arrived:

- Day 81 part 1.2: a judge answering the same question five times and splitting 3–2 — the majority is
  stable, and a case near the boundary flips when the split becomes 2–3.
- Day 80 part 4.1: the same for a rubric line, with the vote's margin discarded so a 3–0 and a 2–1 are
  indistinguishable.
- Day 79 part 5.1: `AgentEvaluator.evaluate`'s `num_runs=2` exists precisely because the *agent* is
  non-deterministic too.

So a judged case is a coin whose bias is usually strong and occasionally is not, and a gate that fails
on it will be red some mornings for no reason connected to any commit.

**Quarantine** is the standard answer: mark the case as known-unreliable, exclude it from the fatal
set, keep running it. That is right, and it is right for the reason Day 81 part 1.2's finding gives —
the flip is a property of the instrument, and failing a build on it teaches people that the eval gate
is not about their code.

And then there is the problem this part is about, which is not the quarantining. It is that a
quarantine is a **deletion nobody had to defend**:

- deleting a case is a diff, reviewed, with a reason;
- quarantining one is a line in a list, and the case still appears in the suite, still runs, still
  reports — so nothing looks smaller;
- and nothing anywhere counts the days.

The list only grows, because every entry was added for a good reason on the day and nothing creates
pressure to remove one. It is the same one-way ratchet Day 79 part 5.2 measured for thresholds and Day
80 part 3.2 measured for rubric wording, arriving a third time in a third shape.

The fix is one field: **a date**, and a limit. Not to force a decision, but to make the absence of one
visible.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because Sutra is about to have judged cases in a gate, and the first time one flips on an unrelated
pull request somebody will quarantine it. That is the correct action. What decides whether the suite is
still meaningful in a year is whether anything ever takes it out again.

And because Day 83's gate asks whether the eval suite is green. A suite that is green with five cases
quarantined is green over a smaller suite, and that is the missing-denominator shape Day 78 part 3.2
measured — a rate reported without the population it was taken from.

## The mechanism

A quarantine entry is a case, a reason, and a number of days. From `lab/flake.py`:

```python
MAX_DAYS = 14  # a convention: how long a case may stay quarantined before somebody must decide


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Quarantined:
    case: str
    days: int
    why: str
```

**Line by line:**

- `days` is the field that does all the work, and it is the one a real quarantine list does not have.
  A `case: reason` mapping — which is what people write — cannot answer "how long", and "how long" is
  the only question that distinguishes a live decision from an abandoned one.
- `why` is a sentence, not a category. `"flaky"` is a category and it is what a dropdown produces;
  *"judge flipped twice this week; genuinely borderline"* is something a person can argue with in six
  months, which is the same argument Day 79 part 1.3 made about a case's `note`.
- `MAX_DAYS = 14` is labelled a convention in the source. It is not derived from anything and it should
  not pretend to be — the number's job is to exist, so that "over the limit" is a fact rather than a
  feeling.
- `frozen=True`: the list is a record of decisions, and an entry something can quietly edit is not a
  record.

And the check, which is a filter and a return code:

```python
    stale = [q for q in QUARANTINE if q.days > MAX_DAYS]
    ...
    for item in stale:
        print(f"    - {item.case}: {item.days} days, limit is {MAX_DAYS}")
    if stale:
        print("\n  a quarantine with no end date is a deletion nobody had to defend")
        return 1
```

**Line by line:**

- `q.days > MAX_DAYS` is the whole rule. Strictly greater, so a case at exactly the limit is still
  inside it and the day it crosses is unambiguous.
- The loop prints **each** stale entry with its age and the limit next to it, rather than a count.
  `3 cases over the limit` sends somebody to open a file; the three names and their ages let them
  decide in the terminal.
- `return 1` — the check is an eval that goes red, which means it can go in the pipeline next to part
  [4.1](4.1-a-red-build-is-a-decision.md)'s two. Its owner is whoever maintains the suite, not whoever
  wrote the commit.
- Nothing here un-quarantines anything. The check produces pressure, not action; a script that silently
  re-enabled a case would be making a decision that needs a person.

Run it:

```bash
cd days/day-82-regression-discipline/lab
uv run python flake.py; echo "exit: $?"
```

**Line by line:**

- No flag: the expiry is on, so entries past the limit are marked.

Measured on 2026-09-06:

```text
5 cases are quarantined
expiry: 14 days

  case                  days  why
  refund_limit             3  judge flipped twice this week; genuinely borderline
  escalation_wording       9  added last sprint, still being argued about
  order_matters           61  flaky since the prompt change in July <- over the limit
  one_lookup             148  flaky; nobody remembers
  counts                 203  quarantined during the incident, never revisited

  checks currently switched off: 5
  longest: 203 days

    - order_matters: 61 days, limit is 14
    - one_lookup: 148 days, limit is 14
    - counts: 203 days, limit is 14

  a quarantine with no end date is a deletion nobody had to defend
exit: 1
```

**Read the `why` column downwards.** The first two are live decisions — a judge that flipped this week,
a case still being argued about — and they are inside the limit. The last three are the same list six
months later: *"flaky since the prompt change in July"*, *"flaky; nobody remembers"*, *"quarantined
during the incident, never revisited"*.

Every one of those was a correct decision on the day it was made. None of them was ever a decision to
stop checking `counts` for two hundred days, and that is what happened.

Now take the expiry away:

```bash
uv run python flake.py --no-expiry; echo "exit: $?"
```

**Line by line:**

- `--no-expiry` is the quarantine everybody actually has: a list of cases and reasons, with no dates
  and no limit. Same five entries.

Measured on 2026-09-06:

```text
5 cases are quarantined
expiry: none (the ablation)

  case                  days  why
  refund_limit             3  judge flipped twice this week; genuinely borderline
  escalation_wording       9  added last sprint, still being argued about
  order_matters           61  flaky since the prompt change in July
  one_lookup             148  flaky; nobody remembers
  counts                 203  quarantined during the incident, never revisited

  checks currently switched off: 5
  longest: 203 days

  with no expiry the list only grows, and nothing on it is ever a decision
  3 of these have been off for more than 14 days and nothing says so
exit: 0
```

**The same five rows, and the run exits 0.** Nothing is hidden — the days are printed in both — and
the difference is entirely whether anything *compares* them to a limit and refuses.

That is the shape this curriculum has now met four times: Day 79's threshold, Day 80's rubric wording,
Day 81's baseline column, and here. The information is present, the report is honest, and without a
check that turns it into an exit code, it is a number on a page.

## When it breaks

Even with an expiry, the check breaks in a way worth naming, because the obvious response to a red
quarantine check is the wrong one.

A build that goes red because `counts` has been quarantined for 203 days is red for something nobody
can fix in that pull request. The person who hits it has two options that take a minute — raise
`MAX_DAYS`, or reset the entry's date — and one that takes an afternoon, which is to find out whether
`counts` still flips.

Both of the fast options are silent. `MAX_DAYS = 14` becoming `MAX_DAYS = 30` is a one-character diff
with no reason attached, and it re-greens the build for a fortnight. Resetting a date is worse: it
destroys the only evidence that the entry is old.

There is no error text and the tell is a pattern in the history: **`MAX_DAYS` going up, or dates being
touched without the case being run.** The defence is the same one Day 80 part 3.1 asked for on gate
rubric lines — make the loosening a diff somebody reviews, with a sentence — plus one structural
choice: store the **date the case was quarantined**, not the number of days, so an entry's age cannot
be reset without changing a date that has a meaning.

The lab stores `days` rather than a date, which is the wrong choice for a real system and the right one
for a fixture that must print the same numbers on every machine. That trade is worth naming rather than
inheriting.

## In production

**What a professional builds into the quarantine.** A date, an owner, and an expiry that fails the
build — and a rule that an expired entry must be either fixed, deleted with a reason, or renewed **with
a new sentence**. The renewal is allowed; the renewal without a sentence is not. That is the difference
between a decision and a ratchet.

**What degrades at scale.** The quarantine's *share* of the suite. Five of nine cases quarantined is a
suite reporting on four, and part [4.1](4.1-a-red-build-is-a-decision.md)'s green build is green over
whatever is left. Real suites report the quarantine count next to the pass count, always, for the same
reason Day 78 part 3.2 demanded a denominator: a rate over an unstated population is not a rate.

**The failure that only shows with real traffic.** Cases are quarantined non-randomly. The ones that
flip are the borderline ones, and the borderline ones are the interesting ones — the answer that is
partly right, the conversation that nearly escalated. So a quarantine drains exactly the cases the
suite exists to watch, and a suite that has been quarantining for a year is green on the easy half.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"Store the date, not the day count — a count can be
reset and a date has to be lied about. Put the quarantine size next to the pass count in the report.
And when the expiry check goes red, the fix is not `MAX_DAYS`; if we want to change that number it
comes with a sentence in the commit message."*

**The interview question:** *"A test is flaky. What do you do?"* The answer that shows experience
quarantines it — and then, unprompted, says what stops the quarantine becoming permanent: a date, a
limit, and a check that fails. A candidate who only says "quarantine it" has a list somewhere with a
two-hundred-day entry on it.
