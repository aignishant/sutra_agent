# 5.2 — What a real regression suite adds

## One-line answer

The lab compares two runs through ADK's own record types, classifies every difference and refuses to
guess — and the distance between that and a suite a team can act on is nine things, the first of which
is one line in a config file and is what makes the other eight possible.

## The story

The garage's job cards, in a box under the counter.

Every car that comes in gets one. Registration, date, what was done, what was replaced, who did it.
When the same car comes back six months later, somebody pulls the old card and reads it before opening
the bonnet — and half the time that is the whole diagnosis, because the answer is *"we changed that
part in March and it has gone again"*.

The box is not a system. It is a cardboard box. What makes it work is one rule that nobody wrote down
and everybody follows: **the card goes in the box.** Not the desk drawer, not the pocket of the
overall, not "I'll file it later". The box.

A garage with the same forms and no box has a stack of paper and no history, and it takes exactly as
long to fill in.

## The idea in plain language

The lab does four things properly. It stores results through ADK's own `LocalEvalSetResultsManager`
rather than inventing a format. It compares two runs case by case and metric by metric rather than
counting passes. It **classifies** every difference into the three causes from part
[1.2](../01-what-a-regression-is/1.2-three-things-that-move-a-number.md). And it refuses to guess:
an unattributable difference exits 1 rather than being blamed on the desk.

Here is what goes in the box.

**1. Write the history somewhere that survives.**
Part [2.2](../02-the-result-is-a-file/2.2-the-history-this-repo-throws-away.md) measured
`.gitignore:24:.adk/` catching the directory ADK writes into. Point the results manager at
`evals/history/` and commit it. This is first because every other item on this list assumes there is a
yesterday, and today there is not.

**2. The four provenance fields, written at run time.**
Part [1.3](../01-what-a-regression-is/1.3-what-has-to-be-written-down.md): the desk's version, the
judge's model and version, the evalset's content hash, and the timestamp ADK already writes. Without
the second, part [1.2](../01-what-a-regression-is/1.2-three-things-that-move-a-number.md)'s
`--unrecorded` arm is the report you get.

**3. Iterate the union of the two case sets.**
Part [2.3](../02-the-result-is-a-file/2.3-comparing-two-runs.md) named the gap: a case that vanished
between runs produces no row, no warning and no exit code. One line — `sorted(set(old) | set(new))` —
plus a third branch for removals.

**4. The free six in `./m check`.**
Part [3.1](../03-two-cadences/3.1-the-fast-half-on-every-commit.md): six checks at zero requests, four
of which test whether the suite can still measure rather than whether the desk works. They belong in
the gate that already runs on every commit.

**5. The verdict in the nightly's exit code, and two codes rather than one.**
Part [3.3](../03-two-cadences/3.3-the-suite-that-ran-and-told-nobody.md) measured a job exiting 0 on a
night with a regression in it. `1` for a regression, `2` for the job failing, `0` for clean — so an
alert can route the two to different people.

**6. An alert on the absence of a run.**
Also part [3.3](../03-two-cadences/3.3-the-suite-that-ran-and-told-nobody.md), and it is a different
alarm from either exit code: a nightly that never starts produces no code at all. Day 73 part 4.2
listed this among the four things a scheduler does not do for you.

**7. The quarantine carries a date, and expires.**
Part [4.2](../04-what-ci-must-refuse/4.2-flaky-by-construction.md) measured five quarantined cases, the
oldest 203 days. Store the **date** rather than a day count, report the quarantine size next to the
pass count, and fail the build when an entry is over the limit.

**8. 🅿️ Parked — a bisection budget and a bisection subset.**
Part [5.1](5.1-what-a-regression-report-costs.md) priced it: the paper's demo needed 34 runs to isolate
an interacting pair, and at 270 requests a run that is 9,180 on a lane whose allowance is unmeasured.
Parked because the reserve cannot be sized until Day 78's criterion 3 can answer.

**9. 🅿️ Parked — comparing against a window rather than against last night.**
Part [2.3](../02-the-result-is-a-file/2.3-comparing-two-runs.md)'s **In production** section: compare
against the median of the last five runs so that judge noise cancels and drift survives. It needs five
runs of history, which needs item 1, and five nights at 270 requests each.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because Day 83's gate is tomorrow and asks whether Phase 12's eval suite is green. Items 1, 2 and 5 are
what make that question answerable at all: without history there is nothing to compare, without
provenance the comparison cannot attribute, and without the exit code nobody finds out.

And because items 8 and 9 are both blocked on the same measurement that has blocked four days running.
`gemini-2.5-flash-lite`'s daily allowance is the number that decides whether a bisection reserve is
affordable and whether five nights of history is, and it has now been a `TODO(me)` since Day 78.

## The mechanism

The list, with what each costs, so it can be argued with rather than admired:

| # | Item | Where it lands | Roughly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | history written somewhere committed | one config line + a `.gitignore` decision | half an hour |
| 2 | the four provenance fields | the wrapper in part 1.3 | an hour |
| 3 | iterate the union | one line + a removal branch | half an hour |
| 4 | the free six in `./m check` | `m` and `tests/` | an hour |
| 5 | verdict in the exit code, two codes | the nightly | half an hour |
| 6 | alert on the absence of a run | outside the repository | needs somewhere to alert to |
| 7 | quarantine with a date and an expiry | `sutra/regress.py` | an hour |
| 8 | 🅿️ bisection budget and subset | not built | needs a measured allowance |
| 9 | 🅿️ compare against a window | not built | needs five nights of history |

**Items 1, 3 and 5 are ninety minutes between them**, and they are the three that turn the lab from a
demonstration into something that would catch a real regression. Item 2 is the one that makes the
report attributable rather than merely present.

Item 1's whole implementation, since it is the one everything else waits on:

```python
HISTORY = ROOT / "evals" / "history"
manager = LocalEvalSetResultsManager(agents_dir=str(HISTORY))
```

**Line by line:**

- One path, outside `.adk/`, and therefore outside `.gitignore` line 24 — which stays exactly as it is,
  because part [2.2](../02-the-result-is-a-file/2.2-the-history-this-repo-throws-away.md) established
  that relaxing it would commit session stores.
- `agents_dir` rather than a history dir: the manager appends `<app_name>/.adk/eval_history` itself, so
  results land in `evals/history/sutra/.adk/eval_history/`. Ugly, and it is the library's layout, and
  fighting it would mean not using the library's manager.
- Committing that directory makes `git log` over it the regression history — the same artefact, with
  authorship and dates attached, for free.

## When it breaks

The list breaks by being read as improvements, and the composite failure has been measured piece by
piece across this day:

```text
1. history goes to .adk/, which is gitignored                    (item 1, part 2.2)
2. there is no yesterday, so nothing can be compared             (part 1.1)
3. the nightly reports a pass count instead                      (part 1.1)
4. it exits 0 because the stages completed                       (item 5, part 3.3)
5. a case that flips gets quarantined, correctly                 (part 4.2)
6. the quarantine has no date, so it never empties                (item 7, part 4.2)
7. a year later the suite is green over half its cases
```

Seven steps, no error at any of them, and the end state is a suite that has been reporting green for
months about a shrinking subset of a system nobody is comparing against anything.

## In production

**What a professional does instead of building all nine.** Item 1, today, because it is thirty minutes
and everything else is worthless without it — and because history that starts accumulating now exists
by the time somebody needs it, while history that starts on the morning of the incident starts empty.
Then items 5 and 3, then 2. Items 8 and 9 are genuinely optional until the suite is big enough for
noise to matter.

**What degrades at scale.** Every item here gets more valuable and more expensive as the suite grows,
except item 1, which gets *harder* — a committed history is three megabytes a year at nine cases and a
repository problem at four hundred with per-invocation results. That is the point at which the
`EvalSetResultsManager` interface earns its keep and an object store replaces the directory, and
switching is easy only if the path was never hard-coded.

**The failure that only shows with real traffic.** Everything in this day compared two fixtures. A real
pair of runs differs in ways a fixture cannot: cases added and removed in the same night, a judge
upgraded mid-week, a run that half-completed because the window ran out. Part
[2.3](../02-the-result-is-a-file/2.3-comparing-two-runs.md)'s three lookup misses are the three that
were designed for; the fourth is a run that is **partial**, and nothing in this day handles it.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"Do item 1 this week — it is a config line and a
directory, and every other thing on this list is waiting on it. Then the exit code and the union.
And put items 8 and 9 on the same ticket as the Flash-Lite measurement; they are both blocked on the
same number and it has been blocked since day 78."*

**The interview question:** *"What would you add to a regression suite before trusting it?"* The answer
that shows experience starts with the history — where it is written and whether it survives the machine
that produced it — rather than with the comparison logic, because a perfect comparison over one run is
not a comparison.
