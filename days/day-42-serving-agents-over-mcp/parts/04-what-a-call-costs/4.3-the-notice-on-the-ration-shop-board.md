# 4.3 — The notice on the ration shop board

## One-line answer

MCP has no field for capacity, cost or rate, so the only place you can publish yours is the tool
description — and a served agent that does not publish it is a service whose callers find out by
being refused.

## The story

The ration shop gets a fixed quantity each month and the whole street depends on it.

For years there was no notice, and the way you learned there was nothing left was to walk down, stand
in line and reach the counter. People came at six in the morning to be sure. People who could not come
early stopped coming at all, not because they were refused but because they could not afford to find
out.

Now there is a board outside with a piece of chalk. It says what came in this month and what is left
today, and the man updates it when he opens and again after the morning rush.

The quantity did not change. Not one extra sack arrived. What changed is that a hundred people now
know, before they leave the house, whether the walk is worth it — and the queue at six in the morning
is gone, because nobody is queuing against uncertainty any more.

## The idea in plain language

You have a hard capacity limit. Twenty generations a day, six or ten calls depending on what you
serve. Your callers cannot see it. There is no field in the protocol that carries it.

Go and look for one. On `mcp==1.29.1`, printed on 2026-09-05, the `Tool` type carries exactly nine
fields: `name`, `title`, `description`, `inputSchema`, `outputSchema`, `icons`, `annotations`, `meta`
and `execution`. **None of them is a cost, a rate or a capacity.** Day 41's
[1.3](../../../day-41-capabilities-and-mcp-apps/parts/01-what-a-server-declares/1.3-an-extension-is-a-capability-with-a-surname.md)
established that inventing a field and putting it in `_meta` is a thing you can do and a thing no
client will read, because a key nobody has agreed on is a key nobody parses.

But two of those nine are closer than they look, and this is the part worth knowing.
`annotations` is a `ToolAnnotations` object with four boolean hints — `readOnlyHint`,
`destructiveHint`, `idempotentHint` and `openWorldHint` — and `execution` is a `ToolExecution` object
carrying `taskSupport`. `idempotentHint: false` is very nearly the sentence *"do not retry this
automatically"*, in a field a client can branch on rather than a sentence a model has to read.

And `to_mcp_server` sets none of them. Measured on 2026-09-05:

```text
annotations: None
execution  : None
meta       : None
title      : None
```

The reason is one line of the ADK source: `server.add_tool(call_agent, name=..., description=...,
structured_output=False)`. `FastMCP.add_tool` accepts `annotations`, `title`, `icons` and `meta` as
parameters; this call passes none of them. So the one machine-readable field that could have carried
half of what you need to say is left `null`, and you are back to prose.

Which leaves you exactly one channel you can use today: **prose in the description**, read by the
caller's model on every turn.

That has two consequences worth stating plainly. First, everything you want the caller to know
competes for the same few sentences that also have to explain what the tool does. Second, it is
advisory. A caller can ignore it, and a caller's *model* can misread it. Publishing your capacity does
not enforce it — it makes a well-behaved caller behave well, and it makes a badly-behaved caller
somebody you can point at.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because Day 45's audit is going to read `sutra_kb_answerer`'s description and ask whether a competent
stranger could use it correctly, and "correctly" now includes "without exhausting our day".

And because the alternative has already been measured.
[4.2](4.2-the-family-data-pack.md) showed forty calls arriving and six being answered. Thirty-four
callers get a refusal they did not expect, in the middle of work they had planned, and every one of
them raises it with you as a bug. A published limit converts all thirty-four of those conversations
into a decision the caller makes before they mount the server.

The third reason is the one that matters most on a free tier: **the callers can help.** A caller who
knows the desk answers ten questions a day will batch, cache and think before asking. A caller who
does not know will let their model call it in a loop. The information is worth more in their hands
than in yours.

## The mechanism

Four things go in the description, in this order, and each one earns its space:

| # | What it says | Why a caller cannot get it any other way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | what kind of question this answers | the input schema is one untyped string |
| 2 | one example that fits and one that does not | there is no example field |
| 3 | that a call runs a language model | nothing on the wire distinguishes this from a lookup |
| 4 | the rate, and that retries are not free | there is no rate field, and the retry hint that exists is left `null` |

Written out, as the constant [3.3](../03-tool-or-peer/3.3-the-guillotine-and-the-press.md) put in
`agent_server.py`:

```python
SERVED_DESCRIPTION = (
    "Answers one self-contained question from Sutra's internal support knowledge base and "
    "cites the article id it used. Good: 'why do users get logged out after the 2.3 update?' "
    "Not for: closing tickets, refunds, or anything about a specific customer's account. "
    "Each call runs a language model on a free-tier quota, so calls are slow, limited to a "
    "small number per day, and must not be retried automatically."
)
```

**Line by line:**

- Sentence one answers *what is this for*, in the caller's language rather than yours. It says
  "self-contained" because [2.3](../02-what-crosses-the-counter/2.3-the-table-you-may-leave-your-books-on.md)
  showed memory the caller cannot rely on, and this is the safe lie: promise nothing, so nothing
  breaks.
- *"cites the article id it used"* tells the caller what the **output** looks like, which matters
  because `outputSchema` is `null` and there is no other way to say it.
- The `Good:` and `Not for:` pair is doing the work Day 28's
  [2.4](../../../day-28-progressive-disclosure-design/parts/02-descriptions-as-routing/2.4-the-crowded-shelf.md)
  found matters most on a crowded shelf: a routing model distinguishes tools far better from a
  negative example than from an adjective.
- *"runs a language model"* is the cost sentence, phrased as a fact about mechanism rather than as a
  number. A number would be better and a number would also be wrong within a month, and a stale
  number in a public description is worse than no number.
- *"limited to a small number per day"* is deliberately vague where the previous clause is precise.
  Publishing "twenty" invites a caller to plan for exactly twenty, and the real figure moves with the
  provider's roster ([Addendum 02](../../../../docs/02_ADDENDUM_ZERO_BUDGET_MODELS.md) is why). The
  shape of the constraint is stable; the number is not.
- *"must not be retried automatically"* is the sentence [3.2](../03-tool-or-peer/3.2-a-note-handed-over-and-money-sent.md)
  earned. It is unenforceable and it is still worth writing, because the default behaviour it is
  arguing against — wrap every network call in a retry — is exactly what Day 44 teaches, correctly,
  for cheap idempotent calls.
- The whole thing is **five sentences**, which is long for a tool description and short for a
  contract. Every one of them is on the hot path of somebody else's token budget on every turn, so
  a sixth would need to displace one of these rather than be added to them.

And the enforceable half, which the description cannot be:

```python
SERVED_CALLS_PER_DAY = 10  # TODO(me): decide this, and where the counter lives

BUSY = (
    "The Sutra desk is at its daily capacity and cannot answer right now. "
    "Retry after 00:00 UTC. Do not retry this call automatically."
)
```

**Line by line:**

- `SERVED_CALLS_PER_DAY` is a `TODO(me)` on purpose. The number depends on which agent you serve and
  how much of the day's budget you keep for yourself, and that is a decision this document must not
  make for you. What is not optional is that the constant exists.
- `BUSY` is a **sentence for a model**, following Day 34's
  [4.1](../../../day-34-building-sutra-mcp-tools/parts/04-two-kinds-of-no/4.1-the-tool-said-no.md):
  it names the condition, gives the time the condition clears, and states the action. A model reading
  it can decide to stop; a model reading a raw 429 body decides to retry.
- Returning `BUSY` as an ordinary result with `isError: true` costs **zero generations**. That is the
  entire point of putting the counter in front of the runner rather than letting the provider refuse:
  the provider's refusal arrives after you have already spent something.
- `Retry after 00:00 UTC` is a real boundary rather than a duration, because a daily quota resets on a
  clock the caller can also read. Day 24's
  [2.1](../../../day-24-token-accounting-and-budgets/parts/02-two-ceilings/2.1-two-ceilings-one-clears.md)
  is the difference between the two ceilings and which one this is.

## When it breaks

The break is publishing nothing and letting the provider speak for you.

Without a description sentence and without a counter, here is what a caller receives when your day
runs out — and this is the real body, measured on 2026-09-05 by `lab/throttled.py`:

```text
isError : True
caller sees (543 characters):
  Error executing tool sutra_desk: 429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED. {'error': {'code': 429, 'message': 'You exceeded your current quota, please check your plan and billing details.', 'status': 'RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED', 'details': [{'@type': 'type.googleapis.com/google.rpc.QuotaFailure', 'violations': [{'quotaMetric': 'generativelanguage.googleapis.com/generate_content_free_tier_requests', 'quotaId': 'GenerateRequestsPerMinutePerProjectPerModel-FreeTier', 'quotaValue': '5'}]}, {'@type': 'type.googleapis.com/google.rpc.RetryInfo', 'retryDelay': '47s'}]}}
```

Five hundred and forty-three characters of somebody else's internal error format, handed to a
stranger's model as the answer to a support question. It leaks your provider, your tier, your quota
metric and your project's quota shape. It says *"check your plan and billing details"* to a caller who
has no plan and no billing details with you. And the one genuinely useful fact in it — `retryDelay:
47s` — is buried where a model is unlikely to act on it correctly.

[5.3](../05-failure-lab/5.3-the-price-sticker-underneath.md) is that failure in full, with the
guarded arm beside it.

## In production

**What a professional writes instead:** the description and the counter ship together, and neither is
allowed to change without the other. The description is tested — an assertion that it is not ADK's
fallback string, that it contains both an example that fits and one that does not, and that it
mentions the model cost. Those are three cheap string assertions and they are the only automated
defence a prose interface can have.

**What degrades at scale:** description length. Every caller's model reads your description on every
turn of their loop, so a five-sentence description across twenty mounted tools is a real fraction of
somebody's context window before any work happens. At scale the discipline inverts: the description
gets *shorter* and the operational detail moves to a document the description links to, which only a
human reads. Day 28's progressive disclosure is that idea, applied to somebody else's context instead
of your own.

**The failure mode that only appears with real traffic:** the published number becoming false. You
write "about ten a day", the provider changes its free roster, and now it is four. The description is
a promise you are no longer keeping, and unlike a stale version pin nothing fails loudly — callers
just get refused more often than you told them they would. This is why the sentence describes the
*shape* of the limit and the counter carries the number.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"the description tells a caller what the tool does
and nothing about what it costs, and we have no counter, so the first thing a caller learns about our
capacity is a five-hundred-character quota body from our provider with our project's quota metric in
it. Put the cost sentence in the description, put the counter in front of the runner, and make the
busy message a sentence written for a model — the current one is written for a Google engineer."*

**The interview question:** *"how does a caller find out your service is rate limited?"* The honest
answer: *"In MCP, they cannot, unless you tell them in prose. The `Tool` type has no rate, cost or
capacity field, and putting one in `_meta` means nothing because no client parses a key nobody agreed
on. So the description is the only channel, and it gets a sentence saying a call runs a model and
must not be retried automatically. That is advisory, so the enforceable half is a counter in front of
the runner that refuses cheaply, with a message written for a model — the condition, when it clears,
and what not to do. The alternative is what happens by default, which is the provider's raw 429 body
going to the caller as the answer, and that leaks your tier and your quota metric while telling them
to check their billing details."*
