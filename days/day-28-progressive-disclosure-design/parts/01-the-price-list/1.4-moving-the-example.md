# 1.4 — Moving the example, and moving it back

## One-line answer

Moving `ticket-triage`'s worked example into `references/` saves **181 tokens per activation** and
costs **one model round trip on most triages** — so the measurement says do it and the frequency says
do not, and the right answer is to make the change, look at both numbers, and revert it.

## The story

Somebody on the internet says you can save fuel by taking the spare tyre out of the boot.

It is true. It weighs about twelve kilos, and twelve kilos less is measurably less fuel over a year.
The number is real and you can look it up, and for a certain kind of driver the sum is genuinely
favourable.

Then, once, on a road with nowhere to stop, at night, you get a puncture — and the saving, which was
distributed thinly across three hundred journeys, is collected all at once in a form that has nothing
to do with fuel.

The arithmetic was never wrong. It was answering a different question from the one that mattered.

## The idea in plain language

[1.3](1.3-weight-flows-down.md) gave the three questions. This part runs the experiment they were
written for, on the one section of `ticket-triage` where the answers disagree.

The worked example is 181 tokens of the body's 654 — **twenty-eight per cent**. It is the single
largest movable block in either skill. And by [1.3](1.3-weight-flows-down.md)'s questions:

- **Q1, how often is it needed?** On most triages. Not all — an obvious ticket does not need it — but
  most.
- **Q2, does a step need it to be performed?** Not exactly. No step says *"consult the example"*. What
  it teaches is the **shape** of the answer, which every step then relies on
  ([27.1.5](../../../day-27-authoring-first-skills/parts/01-extraction/1.5-one-worked-example.md)).
- **Q3, will it grow?** No. It is one case and it stays one case, deliberately.

Q3 says no, which by the rule is already enough to leave it alone. But Q1 says "most, not all" and
twenty-eight per cent is a big enough number that somebody will propose the move — so the honest thing
is to make it, measure it, and find out what the proposal is actually worth.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because this is the exact change a reasonable reviewer will suggest, and *"we tried it and here are the
two numbers"* is a much better answer than *"the rule says no"*.

It also settles something about the rule itself. [1.3](1.3-weight-flows-down.md)'s three questions are
a heuristic, and a heuristic that has never been tested against a real case is a slogan. Running this
one produces two numbers that either support it or do not, and it happens that they do — which is worth
knowing rather than assuming.

## The mechanism

Make the change. Cut the `## Worked example` section into `references/worked-example.md`, and leave a
pointer:

```markdown
## Worked example

For a full worked run of ticket 4521 - both tool calls, what they returned, and the
answer - see [references/worked-example.md](references/worked-example.md).
```

**Line by line:**

- The heading **stays**. Removing it entirely would break the house shape the preflight checks
  ([27.4.3](../../../day-27-authoring-first-skills/parts/04-the-authoring-loop/4.3-sharpening-without-a-model.md)),
  and more importantly it would leave a body that never mentions the existence of an example.
- The pointer says **what is in the file**, not just that a file exists. *"See the worked example"*
  gives the model no reason to spend a round trip; *"both tool calls, what they returned, and the
  answer"* does.
- The link carries the `references/` prefix, because that is the string the model passes to
  `load_skill_resource` and a path without it is refused
  ([26.2.4](../../../day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/parts/02-the-four-tools/2.4-the-third-rung-and-its-fence.md)).

Then re-price the shelf:

```bash
uv run python days/day-28-progressive-disclosure-design/lab/price_the_shelf.py
```

**Line by line:**

- The same script as [1.2](1.2-pricing-your-own-shelf.md), unchanged. The whole value of having written
  it is that a design change is now one command away from a number.
- **Zero generations.**

Measured on 2026-09-04, before and after:

```text
before  body 654  refs {'severity-rubric.md': 142}
after   body 473  refs {'severity-rubric.md': 142, 'worked-example.md': 231}
```

Three numbers to read together, and the third is the surprise.

**The body drops 181 tokens**, from 654 to 473 — a twenty-eight per cent reduction, exactly as
predicted, and it is a real saving multiplied across every turn after activation.

**The reference is 231 tokens**, not 181. Moving a section into its own file costs about fifty tokens
of heading and framing that the section did not have when it lived inside the body. Weight does not
transfer cleanly down the ladder; it gains a little on the way.

**And the fetch is one round trip.** On a five-turn conversation the body saving is 181 × 4 = 724
tokens. The fetch, on the runs where it happens, costs one of twenty generations — five per cent of the
day — and delivers 231 tokens that are then also carried for the remaining turns.

So the honest ledger for a five-turn triage where the example **is** needed:

```text
in the body     : 654 body x 4 turns                = 2616 tokens, 0 extra generations
in references/  : 473 body x 4 + 231 ref x 3        = 2585 tokens, 1 extra generation
```

**Line by line:**

- The body multiplies over four turns in both cases, because activation is on turn two.
- The reference multiplies over three, because it is fetched on turn three.
- The token totals are **thirty-one apart** — about one per cent. The saving that looked like
  twenty-eight per cent of the body is one per cent of the conversation, because the fetched content
  comes back and rides along anyway.
- The generation is not one per cent of anything. It is five per cent of the day's entire capacity.

And on the runs where the model does **not** follow the link, the tokens are genuinely lower — and the
model is writing a triage answer without ever having seen what one looks like. That is not a saving;
it is a different skill.

**Revert it.** The verdict is [1.3](1.3-weight-flows-down.md)'s question 3 answered honestly: the
example will not grow, so it does not move. Record the experiment rather than the conclusion, because
the next person will have the same idea:

```markdown
<!-- The worked example was moved to references/ on 2026-09-04 and moved back the same day:
     body 654 -> 473, reference 231, net saving over a five-turn conversation ~31 tokens,
     cost one model round trip on most triages. It does not grow, so it stays here. -->
```

**Line by line:**

- An HTML comment, so it is invisible to a Markdown renderer and **visible to the model**, which reads
  the body as text. That is a small cost — a few dozen tokens on every activation — paid to stop the
  same experiment being run every quarter. Whether it is worth it is a real question; a note in the
  repository's `skills/README.md` costs the model nothing and is easier to miss.
- The note records **both numbers and the reason**, not the decision. A note saying *"do not move
  this"* invites somebody to disagree; a note with the arithmetic in it invites them to re-run it.
- It is dated, because the arithmetic depends on the tier and the tier will change. On a paid plan
  with no request ceiling, this decision flips.

## When it breaks

**The change that is measured on one request.** Measure the body before and after and the saving is
181 tokens, full stop, and the move looks obviously correct. The whole of the counter-argument lives
in the multiplication and in the round trip, and neither is visible from a single request.

**The reference that is never fetched.** The best case for the tokens and the worst case for the
answers. It also produces a confusing signal: the body got smaller, nothing got worse in any visible
way, and the quality loss shows up as slightly worse answers with no attributable cause.

**The pointer that gives no reason to follow it.** *"See references/worked-example.md"* is a link with
no argument attached. If you do move something down, the pointer has to say what is behind it, or you
have moved the content out and left nothing that would make anybody go and get it.

**The revert that leaves the file behind.** Move it back and delete `references/worked-example.md`, or
the preflight reports an orphan
([27.4.3](../../../day-27-authoring-first-skills/parts/04-the-authoring-loop/4.3-sharpening-without-a-model.md)):

```text
ticket-triage: reference 'worked-example.md' is never linked from the body
```

Which is the check doing exactly its job, on the exact mistake a revert produces.

## In production

**Make the change, measure it, and be willing to revert.** That is the whole method and it is cheap
here: two edits and one script, no model, no quota. A team that reasons about progressive disclosure
without ever running the experiment ends up with a folklore version of the rule that nobody can
challenge.

**Record experiments, not conclusions.** *"We moved it and moved it back, here are the numbers"* is
durable; *"the example belongs in the body"* is an assertion that decays into a rule nobody remembers
the reason for. The commit message is a good place for the first and a bad place for the second.

**Re-run the arithmetic when the tier changes.** Every conclusion in this part depends on a generation
being scarce and a token being cheap. Move to a paid plan and the round trip stops mattering, the
one-per-cent token saving becomes the only term left, and the move becomes marginally correct. The
numbers do not change; the currency does
([24.2.3](../../../day-24-token-accounting-and-budgets/parts/02-two-ceilings/2.3-denominated-in-quota-not-dollars.md)).

**What changes at scale** is that these decisions stop being made one at a time by somebody who has
read this. Forty skills and four authors, and the rule has to be in `skills/README.md` with the
arithmetic beside it, or every author invents their own. The failure is not one wrong decision; it is
twenty inconsistent ones.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"the worked example is twenty-eight per cent of this
body — move it to references. …Actually, I see the note: net saving is about one per cent of a
conversation and it costs a round trip on most triages. Withdrawn. Please put that note in
`skills/README.md` too, because I will suggest this again."*

**The interview question:** *"give me an example of an optimisation you rejected."* The answer that
shows you measure rather than follow rules: *"moving a worked example out of a skill body into a
reference file. The body dropped twenty-eight per cent, which sounds decisive. But the fetched content
comes back into the context and rides along for the rest of the conversation, so over five turns the
net saving was about one per cent — and the fetch is a whole model round trip, which on our
rate-limited tier is five per cent of the day's capacity. And on the runs where the model does not
follow the link, we would be writing triage answers having never shown it what one looks like. So we
made the change, measured it, reverted it, and left the numbers in the repository so the next person
does not have to."*

and run the preflight from
