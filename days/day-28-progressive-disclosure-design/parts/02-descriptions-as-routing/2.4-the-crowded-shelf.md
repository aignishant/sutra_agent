# 2.4 — The crowded shelf

## One-line answer

Four vague skills added beside two good ones do not compete for the requests they describe — they
**narrow every margin on the shelf**, taking five requests from margin 2 to margin 1 and one from 1 to
0, without a word of the good descriptions changing.

## The story

There are four shops on the same street and three of them are called *General Store*.

Not deliberately. Each one opened at a different time, each owner picked the most honest name for what
they sell, and each of them is right — they do sell general things. The fourth is called *Sharma
Stationery* and everybody on the street knows where to go for a notebook.

When somebody new asks where to buy rice, they get pointed at "the general store", and then a pause,
and then *"the one past the chemist, not the one on the corner"*. The directions have become longer
than the address.

Nothing about any individual shop is wrong. What went wrong is on the street, and no shopkeeper can fix
it from inside their own doorway.

## The idea in plain language

[2.3](2.3-measuring-routing-without-a-model.md) built the instrument. This part is the experiment it
was built for, and it is a deliberate failure
([27.5](../../../day-27-authoring-first-skills/parts/05-failure-lab/5.1-the-drill-at-a-locked-gate.md)
was the previous one).

Four decoy skills are added to Sutra's shelf. None of them is malicious and none of them is badly
written in any way a reviewer would object to. Each one is a plausible, well-meant description of a
support-desk capability:

- *"Helps the support desk with tickets and customer questions and related tasks."*
- *"Assists with support desk tasks including tickets, questions and replies."*
- *"Handles customer questions about their tickets and answers them."*
- *"Runs the standard support desk workflows for tickets end to end."*

They are built **in code**, not as folders, and that is a deliberate choice for two reasons. It keeps
the real `skills/` shelf clean — a decoy committed to the repository is a decoy somebody will ship —
and it is exactly the case Day 26 identified as legitimate for the in-code form: demonstrating a
difference between two shelves that differ in one thing
([26.1.3](../../../day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/parts/01-loading-a-folder/1.3-building-a-skill-in-code.md)).

What they have in common is the failure mode: every one is a **category** rather than a job
([2.2](2.2-coverage-orthogonality-specificity.md)). They match everything weakly. And weak matches on
everything is the most damaging profile available, because a skill that never wins still takes a point
away from whoever does.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because this is precisely what Day 29 will do to Sutra's shelf, and it will do it in one commit.

A sourced skill pack is written by people who have never seen your descriptions. They will describe
their skills honestly, in the vocabulary of the domain, and the vocabulary of the domain is the
vocabulary you are already using. Nothing in the pack has to be bad for your routing to get worse.

It also settles a question that would otherwise be argued rather than measured: *"is it fine to have a
general-purpose fallback skill?"* The answer is a number, and the number is in this part.

## The mechanism

Two shelves, one difference.

```python
# days/day-28-progressive-disclosure-design/lab/crowd.py
"""A crowded shelf built in code: four mushy decoys beside the two real skills."""

import pathlib

from google.adk.skills import load_skills_from_dir, models

from route import REQUESTS, report

DECOYS = [
    (
        "ticket-helper",
        "Helps the support desk with tickets and customer questions and related tasks. Use for any support work involving a ticket.",
    ),
    (
        "support-assistant",
        "Assists with support desk tasks including tickets, questions and replies. Use whenever a ticket or a customer question comes up.",
    ),
    (
        "customer-questions",
        "Handles customer questions about their tickets and answers them. Use when someone asks about a ticket.",
    ),
    (
        "desk-workflows",
        "Runs the standard support desk workflows for tickets end to end. Use for any ticket task.",
    ),
]


def decoy(name: str, description: str) -> models.Skill:
    """A skill with a card and a one-line body - enough to compete in the index."""
    return models.Skill(
        frontmatter=models.Frontmatter(name=name, description=description),
        instructions=f"# {name}\n\nDo the obvious thing.\n",
    )


real = sorted(load_skills_from_dir(pathlib.Path("skills")), key=lambda s: s.name)
scored = [r for r in REQUESTS if r != "what time do you close"]

print("--- two real skills ---")
print(f"ties: {report(real, scored)}")

crowded = sorted(real + [decoy(n, d) for n, d in DECOYS], key=lambda s: s.name)
print("\n--- six skills, four of them mush ---")
print(f"ties: {report(crowded, scored)}")
```

**Line by line:**

- `models.Skill(...)` builds each decoy with **no folder**, which is the in-code form from
  [26.1.3](../../../day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/parts/01-loading-a-folder/1.3-building-a-skill-in-code.md).
  The object is indistinguishable from a loaded one, so `report` cannot tell and neither could a model.
- The bodies are one line each. They will never be activated in this experiment, and giving them real
  bodies would suggest the problem is the bodies. **The failure is entirely in the cards.**
- `real + [decoy(...)]` then `sorted(...)` — the crowded shelf is the real one **plus** four, so the two
  runs differ in exactly one thing. Rebuilding the real skills for the second run would introduce a
  second variable.
- `scored` drops the negative request, because a margin is meaningless for something that matches
  nothing ([2.3](2.3-measuring-routing-without-a-model.md)).
- `report` returns the tie count and is called twice, so the two numbers are directly comparable and
  the tables are printed in the same format.
- **Zero model calls**, no key. The whole experiment is set arithmetic.

Run it from the repository root:

```bash
uv run python days/day-28-progressive-disclosure-design/lab/crowd.py
```

**Line by line:**

- Needs `route.py` beside it. **Zero generations.**

Measured on 2026-09-04:

```text
--- two real skills ---
how urgent is this ticket                ticket-triage         2  margin  2
what priority should this ticket get     ticket-triage         2  margin  2
triage this ticket for me                ticket-triage         2  margin  2
draft a reply to the customer on this    kb-answer-style       2  margin  1
how do we word an answer that cites a    kb-answer-style       4  margin  4
can you take a look at this ticket       ticket-triage         2  margin  2
ties: 0

--- six skills, four of them mush ---
how urgent is this ticket                ticket-triage         2  margin  1
what priority should this ticket get     ticket-triage         2  margin  1
triage this ticket for me                ticket-triage         2  margin  1
draft a reply to the customer on this    ticket-helper         2  margin  0
how do we word an answer that cites a    kb-answer-style       4  margin  4
can you take a look at this ticket       ticket-triage         2  margin  1
ties: 1
```

Four things in that comparison, and the second is the one to remember.

**No score changed.** `ticket-triage` still scores 2 on every request it should win. Its description is
byte-for-byte identical. It has not got worse at anything it does.

**Every margin dropped.** Four requests went from 2 to 1, and one from 1 to 0. The decoys never win
those four — they tie for second — and second place is what a margin is measured against.

**One request flipped outright.** *"Draft a reply to the customer on this ticket"* is now won by
`ticket-helper`, a skill whose entire body is *"Do the obvious thing."* It won because it matches
`ticket` and `customer` where `kb-answer-style` matches `reply` and `customer` — two each, and the tie
broke on sort order rather than on merit. That is what a margin of zero is: a coin flip, and this one
landed on the mush.

**The fifth request is untouched.** *"How do we word an answer that cites a KB article"* still wins by
four, because it is the only request whose vocabulary — `word`, `answer`, `cites`, `article` — none of
the decoys uses. Specificity is what survives crowding, and it is visible here as the one row that did
not move.

The fix is not a better `kb-answer-style`. Rewriting it to win against four skills that describe
nothing is work against the wrong opponent. **The fix is upstream**: do not add category skills, and
when one arrives from somewhere else, either sharpen its description into a job or do not install it —
which is Day 29's decision, and this table is the evidence that makes it a decision rather than a
preference.

## When it breaks

**The decoys committed to `skills/`.** They are convincing enough to look like real skills, and a
folder is forever. Building them in code is not fastidiousness — it is the only reason this experiment
leaves no trace.

**A conclusion drawn from the winner column.** *"Only one request changed its answer, so it is mostly
fine."* Five of six margins halved, and every one of those is a request now decided by a single word.
The winner column is the last thing to move and the first thing people look at.

**The general-purpose fallback that seems obviously useful.** *"A skill for anything ticket-related that
the specific ones do not cover"* is the fifth decoy, written in good faith, and it does exactly what
the four above do. If a fallback is genuinely wanted, it belongs in the **instruction** — which applies
unconditionally and competes with nothing
([27.2.1](../../../day-27-authoring-first-skills/parts/02-what-leaves-the-prompt/2.1-one-fact-one-home.md)).

**Fixing it by making the good descriptions longer.** Adding words to `kb-answer-style` raises its score
and its index cost, and the decoys still take second place on the domain words. Length is not the same
as distinctiveness, and it is paid for on every listing
([1.2](../01-the-price-list/1.2-pricing-your-own-shelf.md)).

## In production

**Run the routing gate with the new skill included before merging it.** The gate from
[2.3](2.3-measuring-routing-without-a-model.md) turns this whole part into one exit code. A pull request
that adds a skill and drops the worst margin is a pull request that made the existing shelf worse, and
that is a fact the author cannot see by reading their own file.

**Reject category skills at review, by name.** *"Helper"*, *"assistant"*, *"handler"*, *"workflows"*,
*"manager"* — a skill whose name is a role rather than a job is almost always a category, and the
four-word test catches it before the routing gate does
([27.1.2](../../../day-27-authoring-first-skills/parts/01-extraction/1.2-one-job-four-words.md)).

**Treat a sourced pack as one change, not several.** Installing eight skills at once and measuring
afterwards tells you the shelf got worse and not which of the eight did it. Add them one at a time
against the gate, or accept that the diagnosis will be a bisection.

**What changes at scale** is that this stops being fixable by curation. At forty skills the domain
vocabulary is genuinely exhausted, margins are one everywhere, and no amount of rewriting produces
orthogonal descriptions — because the skills really are all about tickets. The structural answers then
are hierarchy (one routing skill that leads to a family) or search instead of listing
([5.1](../05-in-production/5.1-when-the-index-outgrows-listing.md)). Both are architecture, and both are
much easier to adopt before the shelf is a mess than after.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"this adds a `support-assistant` skill. Run the
routing gate with it: I expect every margin on the shelf to drop by one, and the reply-drafting request
to tie. The skill is not wrong — it is a category, and categories take a point from everybody without
ever winning."*

**The interview question:** *"what happens to your skill routing as you add skills?"* The answer that
shows you measured rather than assumed: *"it degrades without any existing description changing. We
added four plausible, well-meant, general-purpose skills to a shelf of two and measured it: no score
moved, every margin dropped by one, and one request flipped to a skill whose body says nothing. The
damage is done by skills that never win — they take second place on the domain words, and a margin is
measured against second place. So the check runs on every change, adding a skill is treated as a change
to every skill, and category descriptions get rejected at review rather than tuned afterwards."*
