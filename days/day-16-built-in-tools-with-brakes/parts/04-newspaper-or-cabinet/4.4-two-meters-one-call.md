# 4.4 — Two meters, one call

## One-line answer

A grounded answer spends **two separate free allowances at once** — model requests, counted per day,
and search requests, counted per month — and Sutra's arithmetic says the one that runs out first is
not the one the word "grounding" makes you watch.

## The story

The flat had two meters, and only one of them was in the kitchen.

The electricity meter was above the door, with a display you walked past every day. When the number
climbed you noticed, and somebody turned the immersion heater off, and everyone agreed to boil less
water.

The water meter was in a covered box in the pavement outside, under a lid you needed a key for. It was
read twice a year by somebody from the company. Nobody in the flat had looked at it, ever, because
nobody had a reason to walk out to the street and lift a lid.

Then the bill came, and it was the water. A cistern in the bathroom had been running a little for
months, quietly, into the pan, at a rate that would never have caught anyone's attention.

The meter nobody watched was measuring the thing nobody was watching. That was the whole story: not
carelessness, just one dial in the kitchen and one under a slab.

## The idea in plain language

Sutra has had one meter since [Day 2](../../../day-02-llm-mechanics/LESSON.md): **model requests**,
free-tier Gemini, twenty per day on `gemini-3.7-flash` — a number that is not published in a table and
was read off a live 429 on 2026-08-25 (`docs/PACKAGES.md`). Every day since has counted its budget in
that currency, and today's hub does too.

Grounding introduces a second one: **search requests**. From `ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing`,
read on 2026-09-03, the free tier includes **5,000 grounding-with-Google-Search requests per month,
shared across the Gemini 3.x models**, and after that it is charged at $14 per 1,000. The Gemini 2.5
models on the same page have a different arrangement — 1,500 requests per day, shared with Flash-Lite,
then $35 per 1,000 — which is worth noticing only because it proves the allowance is a **product
decision that moves**, not a law.

Three things follow, and none of them is obvious:

- **The two meters have different periods.** One resets daily; one resets monthly. A day spent on
  experiments does not touch the monthly one much, and a month of steady use will not show up in the
  daily one at all.
- **One call spends both.** A grounded turn is one model request and, usually, one search request.
  There is no way to spend only the cheap one.
- **They are not proportional.** This is the part that changes decisions, and it is arithmetic rather
  than opinion, which is what the mechanism below computes.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because Day 24 builds Sutra's quota accounting, and the shape of that ledger is decided by whether it
has one column or several.

Addendum 02 §5 says budgets are denominated in **RPM and RPD per provider**, not dollars, and Day 70's
quota router routes by whoever has headroom. Both of those assume a per-provider counter. Today is the
first evidence that one provider can have **two different counters with two different periods**, and a
ledger designed with one number per provider will have to be rebuilt when the second one matters.

There is also a straightforward planning reason. The measurement below says how many grounded tickets
a day Sutra can serve on the free tier. It is a small number, it is not the number you would guess,
and it changes what the Phase 8 graph is allowed to do on every ticket rather than on some of them.

## The mechanism

No API, no key, just the two published numbers and the call pattern from
[2.3](../02-one-at-a-time/2.3-the-specialist-you-write-yourself.md):

```python
# days/day-16-built-in-tools-with-brakes/lab/two_meters.py
"""Which of the two free meters runs out first. Zero model calls, arithmetic only."""

MODEL_REQUESTS_PER_DAY = 20  # gemini-3.7-flash free tier, observed 2026-08-25 (PACKAGES.md)
SEARCH_REQUESTS_PER_MONTH = 5000  # grounding free allowance, read 2026-09-03 (pricing page)
DAYS = 30

MODEL_CALLS_PER_GROUNDED_TICKET = 3  # desk decides, researcher answers, desk writes (2.3)
SEARCHES_PER_GROUNDED_TICKET = 1  # what one grounded turn typically spends

tickets_per_day = MODEL_REQUESTS_PER_DAY // MODEL_CALLS_PER_GROUNDED_TICKET
searches_per_month = tickets_per_day * SEARCHES_PER_GROUNDED_TICKET * DAYS

print(f"grounded tickets per day, model meter : {tickets_per_day}")
print(
    f"searches that would spend             : {searches_per_month} of {SEARCH_REQUESTS_PER_MONTH}"
)
print(
    f"search allowance used                 : {searches_per_month / SEARCH_REQUESTS_PER_MONTH:.1%}"
)

tickets_if_only_search = SEARCH_REQUESTS_PER_MONTH // (SEARCHES_PER_GROUNDED_TICKET * DAYS)
print(f"grounded tickets per day, search meter : {tickets_if_only_search}")
print(
    f"the binding meter                      : "
    f"{'model requests per day' if tickets_per_day < tickets_if_only_search else 'search requests per month'}"
)
```

**Line by line:**

- Every constant carries **the date it was observed and where from**, in the comment beside it. That is
  Principle 7 in a lab script: these numbers move, and a reader six months from now needs to know
  whether they are looking at a fact or a fossil.
- `MODEL_CALLS_PER_GROUNDED_TICKET = 3` — the referral pattern's real cost: the desk's model call that
  decides to refer, the researcher's grounded call, and the desk's call that writes the note.
- `SEARCHES_PER_GROUNDED_TICKET = 1` — the assumption most likely to be wrong, and deliberately
  isolated on its own line so it can be corrected from a real run. One grounded turn may run several
  queries; `web_search_queries` ([3.1](../03-receipts/3.1-where-the-sources-are.md)) is where you find
  out how many.
- `//` rather than `/` — you cannot serve two thirds of a ticket, and rounding down is the honest
  direction for a budget.
- `DAYS = 30` — a month approximated, stated rather than hidden inside an expression.
- The last line names the **binding meter**: the one that runs out first, which is the only one worth
  putting on a dashboard until it stops being the answer.

```bash
cd days/day-16-built-in-tools-with-brakes/lab
uv run python two_meters.py
```

**Line by line:**

- Pure arithmetic. It costs nothing and it is the most useful thing in this section.

Measured on 2026-09-03, with the numbers as recorded above:

```text
grounded tickets per day, model meter : 6
searches that would spend             : 180 of 5000
search allowance used                 : 3.6%
grounded tickets per day, search meter : 166
the binding meter                      : model requests per day
```

**Six grounded tickets a day.** Not six hundred, and not limited by the thing with "5,000" written on
it: at Sutra's free-tier ceiling the search allowance is 3.6% used, and the meter above the door — the
daily model quota — runs out roughly twenty-eight times sooner.

The practical consequence is a design rule for Phase 8: grounding cannot be a step that runs on every
ticket. It has to be something the graph decides to do, which is exactly what
[4.2](4.2-routing-by-where-the-truth-lives.md)'s routing rule is for, and the reason to care is now a
number rather than a feeling.

## When it breaks

**The daily meter runs out, and it says nothing about search.**

```text
429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED. {'error': {'code': 429, 'message': 'You exceeded your current quota, please
check your plan and billing details. ...', 'status': 'RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED', ...}}
```

That is the model meter, reproduced in full in
[7.1](../07-failure-lab/7.1-the-spare-that-does-not-fit.md) exactly as it arrived on the day this day
was written. Note what it does **not** say: nothing about which quota, nothing about search, and
nothing about when it resets. The message is the same one you would get for a per-minute limit, which
is why Sutra's own note in `docs/PACKAGES.md` — that this ceiling is per *day* and waiting the
suggested delay does not clear it — was worth writing down.

**The monthly meter runs out, and the failure is different in kind.** This document cannot show you
that one: Sutra has not spent 5,000 search requests and, per the arithmetic above, on the free tier it
structurally cannot. What matters is that exceeding it is a **billing** event rather than a refusal —
$14 per 1,000 on the page read on 2026-09-03 — so a key attached to a billing account does not stop;
it charges. On a free-tier key with no billing account there is nothing to charge, and the behaviour
at the boundary is something to check on the pricing page rather than to assume.

## In production

**Count what the provider counts, per meter and per period, and put the binding one on the wall.**

The version a professional writes has a counter per (provider, meter, period) and an alert on the
binding one. The mistake to avoid is a single "AI usage" number, because it hides exactly the case
this part is about: two meters moving at different rates, one of them under a slab in the pavement.

**What changes at scale** is that the binding meter moves. Attach a billing account and the daily
model limit rises, at which point 5,000 searches a month becomes the constraint and every piece of
tuning you did for the old bottleneck is aimed at the wrong dial. A quota system that names its meters
survives this; one that hard-codes "requests per day" does not.

**What fails only under real traffic** is the shared-allowance clause. The 5,000 is *shared across the
Gemini 3.x models*, so a second service on the same project spends your allowance without appearing in
your logs. In an organisation, "our key" is rarely one team's key, and the first sign is a meter moving
when your service is idle.

**The review comment**: *"our budget tracks model requests. This feature also spends search requests,
on a different period, from a shared pool. Where is that counted?"*

**The interview question**: *"you are on a free tier and you have added web search. What is your
capacity?"* The answer that shows the work is a number with its arithmetic attached — calls per
answer, the two allowances, the binding one — rather than a shrug about limits being generous.
