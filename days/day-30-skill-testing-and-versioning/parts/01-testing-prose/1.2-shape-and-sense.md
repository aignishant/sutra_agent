# 1.2 — Shape and sense

## One-line answer

A test over a skill can check its **shape** — the fields are there, within limits, pointing at files
that exist — and cannot check its **sense**, so the job of today's suite is to be excellent at shape
and to say out loud, in code, that it never touched sense.

## The story

The invigilator at the door of the exam hall checks three things: you have a pen, you have your hall
ticket, and the number on the ticket matches the number on the desk.

They are strict about it. No ticket, no seat. Ticket for hall B, no seat. They will turn away someone
who has studied for a year and forgotten a piece of paper, and they are right to, because the rule is
the rule and the alternative is chaos at the door.

And every single person they let in might fail the paper. The invigilator has no opinion about that
and never claims one. Nobody walking past them thinks *"I got in, so I will pass"*, because everybody
understands what the check at the door was for.

The danger is not that the check is shallow. The check is exactly as deep as it should be. The danger
is only when somebody starts treating "got past the invigilator" as "ready for the exam".

## The idea in plain language

Two words, and the whole day sits on the difference.

**Shape** is everything about a skill you can decide by looking at it without understanding it. Is
there a `name`? Is it lowercase with single hyphens? Does it match the folder? Is the description
between one and 1024 characters? Does `references/severity-rubric.md` exist? Every one of those
questions has an answer that two different people, and a computer, would agree on.

**Sense** is everything about a skill that depends on what the words mean. Is this description the one
that will win the routing contest against the other four skills on the shelf? Are these steps the
right steps for a refund in this company this quarter? Is step 3 an instruction or an attack? Those
questions have answers too — they just do not come from the text's structure.

You have already met this line twice, from two directions:

- [Day 29, 3.3](../../../day-29-sourcing-and-auditing-skills/parts/03-the-poisoned-skill/3.3-it-validated-and-was-hostile.md)
  put a deliberately hostile skill through the spec validator and watched it come back `Valid skill`.
  Shape was perfect. Sense was an attack.
- [Day 28, 4.3](../../../day-28-progressive-disclosure-design/parts/04-the-three-axes/4.3-what-the-gate-cannot-check.md)
  showed a routing gate that measures a shelf without a model and still cannot tell you whether a
  description is *true*.

Today adds the third instance, and this time you are the one building the shape checker, so the
boundary is yours to state.

| | Shape | Sense |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decided by | structure, lengths, whether a path exists | what the words mean |
| Who can decide it | a function, deterministically, on every commit | a person, or a model, on a schedule |
| Cost to run | nothing | attention, or quota |
| Today's coverage | complete | none, deliberately |
| Where sense is handled | — | the audit ([Day 29](../../../day-29-sourcing-and-auditing-skills/parts/02-the-five-pass-audit/2.1-read-it-like-the-thing-that-will-obey-it.md)), the routing gate ([Day 28](../../../day-28-progressive-disclosure-design/parts/02-descriptions-as-routing/2.3-measuring-routing-without-a-model.md)), and the evalsets of Phase 12 |

The reason to be exact about this is not modesty. It is that a green suite is a claim, and a claim
that is wider than the thing it was measured on is how teams end up trusting nothing at all after the
first surprise. The invigilator never claims you will pass. Your test suite must be equally clear
about what it checked.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because the shelf you are protecting has a known, documented sense problem sitting on it right now,
and today's suite will not see it.

[Day 28, 2.4](../../../day-28-progressive-disclosure-design/parts/02-descriptions-as-routing/2.4-the-crowded-shelf.md)
measured what happens when skills' descriptions crowd each other, and
[Day 29, 6.1](../../../day-29-sourcing-and-auditing-skills/parts/06-in-production/6.1-run-the-pack-against-the-routing-gate.md)
watched a security-clean sourced pack take Sutra's worst routing margin from one to zero. That pack
would pass every check you write today, without a single finding. If the only thing anybody remembers
about `tests/test_skills.py` is "the skills tests are green", the pack gets merged.

So [6.2](../06-in-production/6.2-writing-down-what-green-means.md) writes the boundary into the suite
itself as a test that passes on purpose — a skill that is shaped perfectly and is routing mush — with
the reason in its docstring. A comment is forgotten. A test that runs on every commit is not.

## The mechanism

The cleanest way to hold the line is to sort every rule you are tempted to write into one of the two
columns before you write it. Here is the sort for the rules this day ends up with:

```text
SHAPE  (a function decides, today)          SENSE  (a person or an eval decides, elsewhere)
-----------------------------------          --------------------------------------------
name present, kebab-case, <= 64 chars        is this the right name for the job?
name equals the folder name                  does the name mean anything to a reader?
description present, 1..1024 chars           does the description win the routing contest?
description contains a "when" clause         is the "when" clause true?
every references/ path resolves              is the referenced file worth loading?
body under 500 lines                         are these the right steps?
metadata.version present and quoted          is the number honest about what changed?
folder digest matches the recorded one       did the change deserve a major bump?
```

**Line by line:**

- The left column is not a subset of the right one. They are different kinds of question, not easy and
  hard versions of the same question. No amount of work on the left column moves anything into it from
  the right.
- `description contains a "when" clause` sits on the left and looks like it might belong on the right.
  It does not: the check is *does the string contain one of a fixed list of phrases*, which is
  structure. Whether the clause describes the tasks that should reach this skill is the row beside it,
  and that is sense.
- `folder digest matches the recorded one` is on the left because a hash comparison is as mechanical as
  a check gets. `did the change deserve a major bump?` is on the right, and
  [5.2](../05-versioning/5.2-the-bump-rule.md) is where a human is told how to decide it.
- Nothing in the left column needs a model. That is not a coincidence and it is not thrift. A check
  that needs a generation cannot run on every commit against a twenty-requests-a-day budget, so a
  check that needs a generation is a check that will be switched off.

## When it breaks

**Somebody adds a sense rule to the shape checker.** The temptation is real and it arrives in a pull
request that says *"also flag descriptions under 40 characters, they route badly"*. That specific rule
is defensible. The next one — *"flag descriptions that overlap another skill's"* — is not, because now
your checker has an opinion about routing that is wrong in every case where two skills genuinely share
vocabulary, and it will be silenced with a per-skill exemption within a month. A shape checker earns
its trust by never being wrong; a rule that needs exemptions has left the left column.

**The green suite that answered the wrong question.** The failure text you will see is no failure text
at all, which is what makes it hard:

```text
13 passed in 0.23s
```

That is a true statement about shape and says nothing about the skill somebody shortened to *"Handles
ticket triage."* this morning. [6.1](../06-in-production/6.1-the-drill-in-the-other-building.md) is
the part where that green becomes a real incident.

**Trying to close the gap with a model call inside a test.** *"Just ask the model whether this
description is specific enough."* Now your suite is non-deterministic, spends quota on every run, and
fails in continuous integration on an HTTP 429 rather than on a real problem —
[Day 23, 5.1](../../../day-23-testing-tools-and-callbacks/parts/05-failure-lab/5.1-the-test-that-phoned-the-model.md)
is the day that failure was named. The gap is closed by a different lane, on a different schedule, not
by making the always-on lane worse.

## In production

**What a professional writes instead** is a test suite with its scope in its module docstring and one
test whose entire job is to demonstrate the scope. The second half is what survives a team change: a
docstring is read once by the person who wrote it, and a test named
`test_a_skill_can_be_shaped_right_and_still_be_wrong` shows up in every run and in every failure list
forever.

**What degrades at scale** is the memory of what green meant. In the first month, everyone who runs
the suite also wrote it. By the second quarter the suite is run by people who joined after it existed,
and "the skills tests pass" has quietly become "the skills are fine". The written boundary is the only
thing that survives that handover, and it has to live where the suite lives rather than in a
design document nobody opens.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"this check reads the description and decides
whether it is 'specific enough'. That is a judgement, and judgements in a merge gate get exemptions
until the gate means nothing. Keep the gate to things that are true or false, and put this in the
routing gate where it can be measured against the other skills."*

**The interview question:** *"how do you know your skill tests are testing anything worth testing?"*
The honest spoken answer: *"By being explicit that they test shape and not sense. Shape is structure —
fields present, within limits, pointers that resolve, a name that matches its folder — and it is
deterministic, free, and safe to run on every commit. Sense is whether the description routes, whether
the steps are right, whether a line is an attack, and none of that is decidable from the text's
structure. We handle sense somewhere else: a human audit for hostility, a routing gate for
description quality, evalsets for behaviour. And we wrote the boundary into the suite as a passing
test — a skill that is shaped perfectly and is routing mush — so that nobody ever reads 'skills tests
green' as 'skills are good'."*

## Check yourself

```bash
uv run python -c "
from google.adk.skills import Frontmatter
f = Frontmatter(name='ticket-triage', description='Handles stuff. Use when something comes up.')
print('loaded fine:', f.name, '|', f.description)
"
```

That description is a genuine routing hazard and the model accepted it without a murmur. Say which
column that rule belongs in, and name the thing in Sutra that *would* have complained.

each from a skill you have written, and say why closing the sense gap with a model call inside the
test suite makes the suite worse rather than better.
