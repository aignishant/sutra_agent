# 3.2 — 💥 Thirty-seven per cent of the way to a cache

## One-line answer

Sutra's desk cannot use ADK's context caching at all — its cacheable prefix is 1,521 estimated tokens
against a 4,096-token floor, which is 37.1% of the way there — and the correct engineering response
is to write that number down rather than to configure around it.

## The story

There is a shared taxi at the edge of the town that leaves for the district headquarters when it has
ten passengers. Not before. The driver is not being difficult; below ten the trip does not pay for the
diesel.

Four people are waiting. They have been waiting a while. One of them keeps walking to the road and
looking down it, because a person who has been waiting begins to believe that waiting is a strategy.

The useful thing anybody does that afternoon is said by the tea-stall owner, who has watched this
particular road for eleven years: *"Four. At this hour, on a Tuesday, four is what you get. The
morning taxi fills. This one does not."*

That sentence ends the waiting. It is not encouraging and it is not a solution. It is a fact with a
number in it, and the four passengers can now do something — share an auto, take the bus, go
tomorrow morning — instead of standing on a road believing that the tenth person is nearly here.

## The idea in plain language

There is a thing this curriculum is supposed to do, and it is easy to do the opposite.

The plan assigns ADK-31 to this day. The obvious way to close an ID is to make the feature work: turn
it on, show it running, tick the box. And there is always a way to make it run — pad the prompt,
duplicate the instruction, paste something big in — because the floor is a size and sizes can be
manufactured.

The honest way is to measure first and then report what the measurement says, including when it says
*this feature does not apply to you*. Principle 10 is usually read as being about errors. It is also
about results: a day that made a cache work by inflating a prompt to reach a floor has fabricated a
success in exactly the way a fabricated transcript would.

So this part measures Sutra's prefix against the floor and reports the gap. That is all it does, and
the gap is the finding.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because the alternative is a `sutra/cache.py` with a `ContextCacheConfig` in it that never once
produces a cache, sitting in the repo for forty more days, with a comment saying `# caching enabled`.

Every later day of this curriculum reads the code the earlier days left. Day 58 wires the triage graph,
Day 70 builds the Quota-Router, Day 84 puts tracing across the whole app. Each of them will look at
this configuration and reason about it. If it is a decoration, they will reason wrongly.

Writing down *"context caching is configured and does not currently fire, because the prefix is 1,521
of 4,096 tokens; here is the condition under which it would"* costs one comment and is true for as
long as it is true.

## The mechanism

The measurement is one command and it has been on this page since
[1.1](../01-two-caches-two-currencies/1.1-the-same-sentence-paid-for-twice.md). Here it is read as a
verdict rather than as a size:

```bash
cd days/day-51-caching-the-quota-lifeline/lab
uv run python prefix.py
```

**Line by line:**

- The arms are the desk without retrieval and the desk with Day 50's `TOP_K = 2` rows, because those
  are the two shapes the desk actually sends.
- `count = mgr._find_count_of_contents_to_cache(req.contents)` applies
  [2.1](../02-the-context-cache/2.1-what-adk-is-willing-to-cache.md)'s boundary, so `contents[:4]` is
  ADK's choice and not the script's.
- `prefix = mgr._estimate_request_tokens(req, count)` is ADK's estimator restricted to the prefix,
  which is precisely the number the third gate in
  [3.1](3.1-two-floors-and-a-first-turn-rule.md) compares.
- The percentage and the shortfall are printed because *"refused"* on its own is the thing that starts
  people configuring.

Measured on 2026-09-05:

```text
Gemini 3 explicit-cache floor : 4096 tokens
ADK's estimator               : 4 chars a token, floor division

no retrieval         instruction    660 + tools  4952 + contents[:4]
                     whole request  1531 tok   cacheable prefix  1521 tok  (37.1%)  refused, 2575 tokens short

retrieval, k=2       instruction   1222 + tools  4952 + contents[:4]
                     whole request  1671 tok   cacheable prefix  1661 tok  (40.6%)  refused, 2435 tokens short
```

**1,521 of 4,096. Thirty-seven point one per cent. Two thousand five hundred and seventy-five tokens
short.**

And it is worse than that, which is worth doing rather than glossing. ADK's estimator divides
characters by four. Day 24 measured Sutra's own text against the provider's real tokenizer at
**4.55 characters per token**. The cacheable prefix is 6,085 characters, so:

| Counted by | Tokens | Share of the 4,096 floor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ADK's estimator, 4 chars/token | 1,521 | 37.1% |
| Day 24's measurement, 4.55 chars/token | 1,337 | 32.6% |

The estimate is the **optimistic** one. The desk is closer to a third of the way to the floor than to
forty per cent of it.

Turning on retrieval makes the prefix bigger — 1,661 instead of 1,521 — and it is worth being clear
that this is not progress. Those extra characters are the per-question rows from
[2.3](../02-the-context-cache/2.3-the-fingerprint-is-the-key.md), which move the fingerprint on every
request. Growing the prefix with material that changes every time gets you closer to a floor you
would immediately fall back through.

So: **for Sutra's desk as Phase 7 leaves it, ADK's context caching does not fire.** Not
misconfigured. Not broken. Not applicable.

## When it breaks

The break is what happens when somebody refuses to write that sentence. There are three ways to make
the number go away, and all three are worse than the fact.

🎬 **The scene.** The number is 37.1%. The ticket says "enable context caching". Somebody has to close
the ticket.

😬 **The naive fix, first form: lower `min_tokens`.** It is the only knob with "tokens" in the name.
Setting it to `0` — or to `1000`, or to anything — changes nothing, because the refusal is the
**third** gate and `min_tokens` is the second. The log line does not even change; it still says
`Cacheable prefix below Gemini minimum cache size (1521 < 4096 tokens)`.

😬 **The naive fix, second form: pad the prompt.** Add 2,600 tokens of instruction. This works. The
cache is created and the dashboard turns green. What it has actually done is add roughly 10,000
characters to every request in order to save 6,085 of them, which is a straightforward loss — and
worse, it has added 10,000 characters of instruction that the model reads and obeys, which is a
behaviour change made for an operational reason.

😬 **The naive fix, third form: report it as done anyway.** Configure it, write "caching enabled" in
the ledger, and move on. Nothing fails. The row in `PROGRESS.md` is green. The lie has a shelf life of
about forty days, until Day 70's Quota-Router tries to reason about where the requests are going.

💥 **Why they fail.** All three treat the floor as an obstacle to the ticket rather than as
information about the system. The floor is telling you something true: *this prompt is too small for
this optimisation to be worth the provider's while.* That is not a problem to be routed around. It is
a fact that redirects the day's effort onto the response cache in
[section 5](../05-the-response-cache/5.1-the-traffic-decides-the-hit-rate.md), which is where Sutra's
requests actually get saved.

💡 **The insight.** A measurement that says "no" has done its job. The output of this part is not a
working cache; it is a number, a condition, and a comment in `sutra/cache.py` that a future day can
re-evaluate. [3.3](3.3-what-would-have-to-change.md) writes the condition.

Here is the refusal, produced deliberately, so it can be recognised in a log:

```bash
uv run python floors.py
```

**Line by line:**

- Arm 3 is the shape of the desk's problem: past your own floor, below the provider's.
- The line is `info` level, which is why an application with default logging never sees it.

```text
3. above your floor, below Gemini's
    previous prompt = 3000, min_tokens = 2048, prefix estimate = 2980
    log: Cacheable prefix below Gemini minimum cache size (2980 < 4096 tokens)
    returned: None
```

## In production

**What a professional writes instead.** A comment, in the config, with the number and the date:

```python
# Context caching is configured and does not fire for the desk today.
# Measured 2026-09-05 (lab/prefix.py): cacheable prefix 1,521 est. tokens against
# the gemini-3 floor of 4,096 - 37.1%. Re-measure when the tool schemas grow past
# ~16,000 characters or when a policy document is added to the instruction.
CACHE_MIN_TOKENS = 4096  # the provider's floor, not a preference
```

**Line by line:**

- The first line states the outcome, so a reader does not have to derive it.
- The second names the command and the date, so it is reproducible and its staleness is visible.
- The third gives the **trigger condition** rather than a promise to revisit. "Revisit later" is not a
  condition; "16,000 characters of schema" is one that a future day can check.
- `CACHE_MIN_TOKENS = 4096` matches the provider's floor deliberately, so a refusal is attributed to
  your own gate and appears in your own logs rather than as a provider-floor message.

**What degrades at scale.** This is one of the few things that gets *better* with scale. Tool schemas
grow, policy paragraphs get added, few-shot examples accumulate — the prefix creeps up, and one day it
crosses 4,096 and context caching starts working without anyone doing anything. Which is exactly why
the comment names a trigger: the day it starts firing should be a day someone noticed, not a mystery
in a token graph.

**The failure mode that only appears with real traffic.** A prefix that sits near the floor is the
worst place to be. Some requests clear it and some do not, so caches are created and abandoned in a
pattern that tracks the length of your system instruction rather than anything meaningful, and the
bookkeeping calls from [2.4](../02-the-context-cache/2.4-the-three-ways-a-cache-dies.md) arrive
unpredictably. Far below the floor, as Sutra is, is operationally simpler than just above it.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"Good — you measured it and it doesn't apply. Put
the number in the comment, not just 'too small'. And add the condition under which we'd re-check,
otherwise this comment is unfalsifiable in six months and someone will delete it or trust it, and both
are wrong."*

**The interview question:** *"Tell me about a time you turned a feature on and it did nothing."* An
honest answer: *"Context caching on my support agent. The framework supported it, the config was
three fields, and it never once fired. I measured the cacheable prefix — 1,521 estimated tokens
against the model's 4,096-token minimum, so 37% of the way there, and by my own measured
characters-per-token it was closer to 33%. Three ways to make that go away and all of them are worse
than the fact: lowering my own minimum doesn't touch the provider's, padding the prompt spends 10,000
characters to save 6,000 and changes the model's behaviour, and reporting it as done leaves a lie in
the repo. So I wrote the number and the re-check condition into the config comment and moved the
day's effort to a response cache, which is what actually saved requests — 45% of them. The feature I
was told to enable was the wrong feature for my prompt size, and the measurement is what told me."*
