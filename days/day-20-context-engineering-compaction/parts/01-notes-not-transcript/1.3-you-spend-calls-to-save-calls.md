# 1.3 — You spend calls to save characters

## One-line answer

Compaction is not free and it is not even the same currency: every summary is **an extra model
call** bought in exchange for **fewer characters** on all the calls after it, so it loses on short
conversations and wins on long ones.

## The story

There is a toll road between the two towns and there is the old road.

The toll road costs money at the barrier. It also saves fuel, because it is shorter and you are not
stopping at eleven junctions. If you are going the whole way, you come out ahead — every time, and
by a lot.

If you are going to the garden centre two junctions along, you do not. You pay at the barrier, you
drive for four minutes, and you leave having spent money to save almost nothing.

Nobody thinks the toll road is a bad road. They think about how far they are going.

## The idea in plain language

Compaction has a price and the price is a model call. Somebody has to read the old turns and write
the summary, and "somebody" is a language model, so a compaction is a request to a provider exactly
like a conversational turn is.

What you get back is smaller requests from then on. The turns that were replaced stop being re-sent,
so every later call carries the summary instead of them.

The awkward part — and this is the bit that surprises people who have only ever thought about
tokens — is that **the thing you spend and the thing you save are measured in different units**.

- You **spend requests**. One extra call per compaction, on top of the conversational calls.
- You **save characters**, on every call after the compaction, forever.

If your bill is denominated in tokens, that trade is almost always good, because a summarizer call
on a small model is cheap and the saving compounds. If your quota is denominated in *requests per
day* — which is exactly Sutra's situation on the free tier
([Addendum 02](../../../../docs/02_ADDENDUM_ZERO_BUDGET_MODELS.md)) — then compaction spends the
scarce thing to save the abundant one, and the calculation changes completely.

That is not an argument against compaction. It is the reason the summarizer is a separate model
choice: the summarizer's calls come out of the summarizer's model's quota, and on Gemini's free tier
the daily request limit is **per model**
([5.1](../05-in-production/5.1-budgeting-the-minute-taker.md) has the measurement). Point the
minute-taker at a different model and you are spending a different budget.

The measurement below is the toll-road arithmetic: at what conversation length does compaction start
sending fewer characters, and what does it cost in calls to get there.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because "turn compaction on" is not a decision — "turn it on after turn *n*" is, and Sutra has to
pick *n*.

The free tier gives 20 generate requests per day per model
([`docs/PACKAGES.md`](../../../../docs/PACKAGES.md), 2026-08-25, re-confirmed on 2026-09-03 from a
live 429). A conversation of ten turns with compaction every third turn is fourteen requests, not
ten. Get that wrong and Sutra stops answering at lunchtime, having spent a third of the day's quota
writing notes to itself.

Day 24 builds the budget accounting that makes this a running number rather than a hand calculation,
and Day 70's quota router uses it to decide which provider gets which job. Both start from the
crossover point this part computes.

## The mechanism

Simulate the same conversation twice — once sending the whole history every turn, once compacting
every third invocation — and count both currencies.

```python
# days/day-20-context-engineering-compaction/lab/break_even.py
"""Compaction costs one extra call every interval. Find the turn where it starts paying for itself."""

TURN_CHARS = 240  # one user message plus one model reply, from day 19's measurement
SUMMARY_CHARS = 150  # what the summary itself occupies in the window
INTERVAL = 3  # compact after every 3 completed invocations
KEEP = 1  # invocations left verbatim after a compaction


def plain(turns: int) -> tuple[int, int]:
    """No compaction: turn n carries n turns. Returns (chars sent, calls made)."""
    return sum(n * TURN_CHARS for n in range(1, turns + 1)), turns


def compacted(turns: int) -> tuple[int, int]:
    """Compaction: history resets to a summary plus the kept turns, and each reset costs a call."""
    chars = 0
    calls = 0
    since_compaction = 0
    carried = 0  # summary chars currently standing in for older turns
    for _ in range(1, turns + 1):
        since_compaction += 1
        chars += carried + since_compaction * TURN_CHARS
        calls += 1
        if since_compaction == INTERVAL:
            carried = SUMMARY_CHARS
            since_compaction = KEEP
            calls += 1  # the summarizer's own call
    return chars, calls


print("turns   plain chars  compacted chars   plain calls  compacted calls")
for turns in (3, 6, 10, 20, 40):
    p_chars, p_calls = plain(turns)
    c_chars, c_calls = compacted(turns)
    print(f"{turns:>5}   {p_chars:>11}  {c_chars:>15}   {p_calls:>11}  {c_calls:>15}")

print()
for turns in range(1, 200):
    if compacted(turns)[0] < plain(turns)[0]:
        print(f"compaction sends fewer characters from turn {turns} onwards")
        break
```

**Line by line:**

- `TURN_CHARS = 240` is not invented. Yesterday's `history_grows.py` measured each turn adding about
  244 characters to the request
  ([19.1.3](../../../day-19-context-engineering-selection/parts/01-the-binder/1.3-the-organ-that-grows-by-itself.md));
  240 is that number, rounded, so this simulation is anchored to something real.
- `SUMMARY_CHARS = 150` is the size of the summary in the window. It is a cost, not a saving — this
  is the term people forget, and it is why compacting a two-turn conversation makes the window
  *bigger*.
- `plain()` returns `sum(n * TURN_CHARS ...)`, which is yesterday's quadratic written out: turn *n*
  carries *n* turns, so the running total grows with the square of the length.
- `since_compaction` counts invocations since the last summary. It is the counter ADK calls
  `compaction_interval`, and [2.2](../02-the-config/2.2-the-turn-count-trigger.md) is where it
  becomes configuration.
- `carried` is the summary standing in for everything older. Note it is set to `SUMMARY_CHARS` and
  never grows: this simulation assumes each new summary *replaces* the last rather than stacking. In
  ADK that is what the overlap machinery arranges, and
  [3.2](../03-reading-the-record/3.2-what-the-model-sees-after-a-compaction.md) shows the real
  version.
- `since_compaction = KEEP` after a compaction, rather than `0`. The kept turns are still in the
  window and still being paid for, so resetting to zero would overstate the saving.
- `calls += 1` **twice** in the compacted path — once for the conversational turn, once for the
  summarizer. That second increment is the entire cost side of this part, and it is one line, which
  is roughly how easy it is to forget.
- The final loop searches for the first turn count where compaction sends fewer characters. It
  searches rather than asserting, because the answer depends on all three constants above and
  changing any of them moves it.

**Line by line:**

- Pure arithmetic: no model, no key, no quota.

Measured on 2026-09-03:

```text
turns   plain chars  compacted chars   plain calls  compacted calls
    3          1440             1440             3                4
    6          5040             3570             6                8
   10         13200             6570            10               14
   20         50400            14070            20               29
   40        196800            29070            40               59

compaction sends fewer characters from turn 4 onwards
```

Three readings, in the order that matters.

**At three turns you have paid and gained nothing.** Identical character counts, one extra call. The
compaction fired at the end of turn three and every character it saved is in a conversation that
already finished. This is the garden centre.

**The crossover is turn four**, and after that the gap opens fast because the plain column is
quadratic and the compacted column is close to linear. By turn forty it is 196,800 characters
against 29,070 — the compacted conversation sends **85% less**.

**And the calls column moves the other way the whole time.** Forty turns become fifty-nine requests.
That is 47% more requests to a provider, in exchange for 85% fewer characters. On a token bill that
is an obvious win. On a free tier that counts requests, it is a decision, and the answer depends on
which model those nineteen extra requests are pointed at.

## When it breaks

The arithmetic never raises. What raises is the provider, at the end of the day, when the extra
calls have eaten the budget you were counting on for conversation:

```text
google.genai.errors.ClientError: 429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED. {'error': {'code': 429, 'message':
'You exceeded your current quota, please check your plan and billing details. ...
* Quota exceeded for metric: generativelanguage.googleapis.com/generate_content_free_tier_requests,
limit: 20, model: gemini-3.7-flash\nPlease retry in 57.991539991s.', 'status':
'RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED'}}
```

That is the real body, captured on 2026-09-03 while writing this day. Two things in it are worth
naming, because both mislead.

**`limit: 20, model: gemini-3.7-flash`** — the quota is per *model*, and the message says so. That
is the fact [5.1](../05-in-production/5.1-budgeting-the-minute-taker.md) turns into a strategy.

**`Please retry in 57.991539991s`** is a suggestion, not a reset. The quota that was exhausted is
`GenerateRequestsPerDayPerProjectPerModel-FreeTier` — a *daily* ceiling — so waiting achieves
nothing. Day 2 established this the hard way (28 requests spread across a quarter of an hour, each
obeying the stated delay, all refused) and recorded it in
[`docs/PACKAGES.md`](../../../../docs/PACKAGES.md) and
[ADR-0007](../../../../docs/adr/ADR-0007-interactions-error-hierarchy.md). Retrying on that hint is
how you spend the rest of the day rediscovering the same fact.

## In production

**Compaction is a knob, not a switch, and the knob is the interval.** Setting it small feels
cautious and is the expensive mistake: it maximises summarizer calls and maximises the number of
times a fact can get dropped. Setting it large keeps windows big for longer. The number comes from
your own conversation-length distribution — specifically from the length your sessions actually
reach, not the length you imagine them reaching.

**What changes at scale** is that the crossover stops being a single number. Sessions that end at
two turns pay the toll and get nothing; sessions that run to fifty pay it repeatedly and save
enormously. The aggregate saving is real while a large minority of individual sessions are made
worse, and the median session is often one of the ones made worse. Reporting the mean saving hides
that entirely.

**The failure that only appears with real traffic** is the burst. Compaction runs *after* an
invocation completes, so a hundred conversations crossing their interval within the same stretch
produce a hundred summarizer calls that nothing rate-limits, on top of the conversational load that
caused them. The provider sees a spike that has no user-facing cause and is invisible in your
request logs unless you tagged summarizer calls separately.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"what's the interval based on? If it's 3 because 3
looked reasonable, measure the session-length histogram first — we may be paying the toll on 60% of
sessions that never get past turn two."*

**The interview question:** *"what does summarization cost?"* The answer that shows you have run the
numbers: *"an extra model call per compaction, and it does not break even until about turn four with
our turn sizes. It saves characters and spends requests, which matters because our quota is
denominated in requests."*
