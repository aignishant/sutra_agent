# 5.1 — Budgeting the minute-taker

## One-line answer

The free tier's daily request limit is **per model**, so pointing the summarizer at a different model
is not merely cheaper — it spends a different allowance, and it took Sutra from 15 usable turns a day
to 20.

## The story

The corner shop takes on a part-timer for Saturdays, and the only job is the stock list.

The owner could do the stock list. The owner *did* do the stock list, for two years, and every
Saturday afternoon there was an hour when nobody was serving because the owner was in the back
counting tins. The customers who came in during that hour went to the supermarket.

The part-timer costs money. What they buy is the hour, and the hour is worth more than they cost,
because the owner's hour and the part-timer's hour are not the same hour.

Nobody sensible frames this as "the owner is bad at counting tins".

## The idea in plain language

[4.1](../04-failure-lab/4.1-the-minute-taker-you-did-not-hire.md) showed the cost of leaving the
summarizer unset: your chat model does the work. This part is about why the fix is worth more on a
free tier than it would be on a paid one.

On a paid API the argument is ordinary. A smaller model costs less per token, summarizing is an easy
task, so use the smaller model and save money. Sensible, unremarkable.

On Gemini's free tier the argument is different in kind, because **the quota is per model**. That is
not a guess. It is what the 429 body says, verbatim, captured on 2026-09-03:

```text
"Quota exceeded for metric: generativelanguage.googleapis.com/generate_content_free_tier_requests,
limit: 20, model: gemini-3.7-flash"
```

and the violation detail names the quota as
`GenerateRequestsPerDayPerProjectPerModel-FreeTier` — per project, **per model**, per day.

So the summarizer is not competing with your conversation for one pool of requests. Give it a
different model and it has its own pool. The conversation gets the whole of the chat model's daily
allowance, and the summaries come out of a budget that was otherwise sitting unused.

That turns a cost optimisation into a capacity increase, and the measurement below puts a number on
it.

There is a second reason to pin explicitly, and it is the one that survives leaving the free tier.
**Two models means two lines on the usage page.** Summarizer traffic and conversational traffic are
otherwise indistinguishable — same client, same credentials, same model — and the first symptom of a
compaction interval set too small is a request count that nobody can attribute.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because 20 requests a day is the entire budget, and this decision is worth a quarter of it.

Sutra runs on the free tier by design, not by accident
([Addendum 02](../../../../docs/02_ADDENDUM_ZERO_BUDGET_MODELS.md): *quota is the currency*). Every
day from here is written against that ceiling. A configuration choice that changes how many turns a
day Sutra can hold is not a tuning detail, it is the difference between finishing a day's exercises
and not.

Day 24 turns this into running accounting — requests per provider per model per window — and Day 70's
quota router generalises it to routing each job to whichever provider has headroom. Both are this
part's arithmetic, automated.

## The mechanism

First, the model roster, because the obvious guess is wrong. Listed live on 2026-09-03 with the
project's own key:

```bash
curl -s "https://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/v1beta/models?key=$GOOGLE_API_KEY&pageSize=200" \
  | grep -o '"name": "models/[^"]*"' | sed 's/.*models\///;s/"//' | grep flash | sort
```

**Line by line:**

- `v1beta/models` is the **list** endpoint. It costs no generation quota, which is what makes it the
  right way to check a roster on a day when you need every request for the lesson.
- `$GOOGLE_API_KEY` comes from `.env`, never from the command line history, and never into git
  (Principle 9).
- `pageSize=200` because the default page is short and the roster is long; a truncated list is how you
  conclude a model does not exist when it does.
- The `grep`/`sed` pipeline reduces the JSON to bare model ids. The full record also carries
  `inputTokenLimit` and `supportedGenerationMethods`, which is where the numbers in the table below
  come from.

The part of the output that matters:

```text
gemini-3.5-flash
gemini-3.5-flash-lite
gemini-3.6-flash
gemini-3.7-flash
gemini-3.8-flash
gemini-flash-lite-latest
```

**There is no `gemini-3.7-flash-lite`, and no `gemini-3.6-flash-lite`.** The flagship flash line has
run on to 3.8 and the newest lite model is still **3.5**. Anyone writing
`gemini-3.7-flash-lite` by pattern-matching on Sutra's pinned chat model — which is the obvious,
natural thing to write — gets a model that does not exist, and finds out at the first compaction
rather than at startup.

This is Principle 7 with the teeth showing. The version you did not look up is the one that is wrong,
and *"the lite variant of the model I am already using"* is exactly the kind of fact that feels too
obvious to check.

| Model | Role in Sutra | Input limit | `generateContent`? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `gemini-3.7-flash` | the conversation, pinned since Day 2 | 1,048,576 | yes |
| `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` | the summarizer, from today | 1,048,576 | yes |

Both read from the live record on 2026-09-03. Now the arithmetic that makes the second row worth
having:

```python
# days/day-20-context-engineering-compaction/lab/quota_split.py
"""Free-tier quota is per model per day. Work out how far a conversation gets, both ways."""

RPD = 20  # generate requests per day, per model, free tier (docs/PACKAGES.md, 2026-09-03)
INTERVAL = 3  # compact after every 3 completed invocations


def turns_possible(shared: bool) -> int:
    """How many conversational turns fit in the daily quota."""
    chat = 0
    chat_used = 0
    summarizer_used = 0
    while True:
        if chat_used + 1 > RPD:
            return chat
        chat_used += 1
        chat += 1
        if chat % INTERVAL == 0:
            if shared:
                if chat_used + 1 > RPD:
                    return chat
                chat_used += 1
            else:
                if summarizer_used + 1 > RPD:
                    return chat
                summarizer_used += 1


for shared, label in ((True, "summarizer = the chat model"), (False, "summarizer = a lite model")):
    print(f"{label:<30} {turns_possible(shared):>3} turns per day")

print(f"\n(RPD={RPD} per model, compaction every {INTERVAL} invocations)")
```

**Line by line:**

- `RPD = 20` is not a guess and not a documentation figure — the public rate-limit page no longer
  publishes a free-tier table. It is the number in the 429 body above, which is the only place the
  limit is currently stated.
- `shared` is the ablation: `True` models the broken configuration from
  [4.1](../04-failure-lab/4.1-the-minute-taker-you-did-not-hire.md) where both kinds of call hit one
  model, `False` models two models with two allowances.
- **Two counters, `chat_used` and `summarizer_used`.** The whole point of the file is that these are
  separate ceilings when the models differ, and the same ceiling when they do not.
- `chat % INTERVAL == 0` fires a compaction after every third conversational turn, matching
  `compaction_interval=3`.
- The loop returns `chat` — the number of **conversational** turns achieved — because that is what the
  user gets. Summaries are overhead, and counting them as throughput is how the broken configuration
  looks fine on a request graph.
- Each branch checks its own ceiling **before** spending, so the count stops at the last turn that
  actually completed rather than the one that got a 429.

```bash
cd days/day-20-context-engineering-compaction/lab
uv run python quota_split.py
```

**Line by line:**

- Arithmetic only. No key, no network, no quota.

Measured on 2026-09-03:

```text
summarizer = the chat model     15 turns per day
summarizer = a lite model       20 turns per day

(RPD=20 per model, compaction every 3 invocations)
```

**Fifteen turns against twenty.** A third more conversation per day, from one keyword argument, at no
cost, because the second budget was there all along and nothing was using it.

Put the other way round: leaving `summarizer` unset does not cost you a few per cent of efficiency. It
costs you **a quarter of your daily capacity**, and it does so invisibly, since the requests it spends
look exactly like the requests you meant to make.

## When it breaks

**The model name that does not exist**, which is what the roster check exists to prevent:

```text
google.genai.errors.ClientError: 404 NOT_FOUND. {'error': {'code': 404, 'message': 'models/
gemini-3.7-flash-lite is not found for API version v1beta, or is not supported for generateContent.
Call ModelService.ListModels to see the list of available models and their supported methods.',
'status': 'NOT_FOUND'}}
```

That is the real body, produced on 2026-09-03 by asking for that exact model string. Note *when*
it arrives. The summarizer is not used until the first compaction, so a wrong model
string sits harmlessly through startup, through validation, and through the first few turns of every
conversation — and then fails partway through the ones that run long. It is a configuration error
with the timing of a runtime error.

**And the quota failure, when the split has not been made:**

```text
429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED ... limit: 20, model: gemini-3.7-flash. Please retry in 57.991539991s.
```

The retry hint is not a reset — the exhausted quota is daily, as
[1.3](../01-notes-not-transcript/1.3-you-spend-calls-to-save-calls.md) covers. What is worth adding
here is *which* requests spent it. On the shared configuration, five of those twenty were summaries,
and nothing in the error says so.

## In production

**Pin both models in one place and name the reason.** Two constants, `CHAT_MODEL` and
`SUMMARIZER_MODEL`, with a comment saying they are deliberately different models and why. The next
person to read it will otherwise assume the difference is an oversight and tidy it.

**What changes at scale** is that the free tier stops being the constraint and the argument survives
anyway, in a different currency. On a paid plan the summarizer is a high-volume, low-difficulty
workload, and running it on the flagship model is a straightforward waste. The reason to separate them
is the same; only the units change.

**The failure that only appears with real traffic** is the summarizer becoming the bottleneck nobody
watches. Conversational traffic is alerted on because users complain. Summarizer traffic is not, and a
summarizer that starts failing does not break any turn — compaction just silently stops happening, and
windows quietly grow again until the day the *chat* model starts returning 429s for a reason nobody can
find.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"summarizer and chat are both on flash. On the free
tier that's one 20-a-day bucket for both — split them and we get the whole conversational budget
back."*

**The interview question:** *"how do you budget for summarization?"* The answer that shows you have
hit the ceiling: *"separately from conversation, and on a different model. Gemini's free tier meters
requests per model per day, so putting the summarizer on the lite model gives it its own allowance —
that took us from 15 conversational turns a day to 20. It also makes the spend visible, because the
two workloads land on different lines of the usage page."*
