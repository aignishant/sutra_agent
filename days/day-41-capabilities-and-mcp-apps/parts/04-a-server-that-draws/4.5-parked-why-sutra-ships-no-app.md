# 4.5 — 🅿️ Why Sutra ships no App

## One-line answer

🅿️ **Parked, with three reasons and a trigger:** Sutra's one UI-shaped problem is already solved by
elicitation, an App needs a browser and a build toolchain this budget does not have, and shipping one
would mean declaring `io.modelcontextprotocol/ui` on the client side too — so it is documented,
interview-ready, and deliberately not built.

## The story

The tailor's shop on the corner has no display window.

There is a door, a signboard, and a room with two machines in it. Every other shop on that stretch has
glass across the front with something arranged behind it, and the tailor has considered it more than
once. A carpenter quoted for the frame. It would look better.

He has not done it, and if you ask him he gives you the same three sentences every time. His customers
telephone or walk in and are measured; nobody has ever chosen him from the street. The glass costs
about two months of what he clears. And the front of the shop faces west, so anything in the window
fades.

That is not a shopkeeper who has failed to modernise. That is a decision, made once, with its reasons
written down, and he can tell you what would change his mind: if the building next door becomes a
clothes shop and starts pulling people down the street, the window becomes worth it.

## The idea in plain language

**Parked** in this curriculum means: understood well enough to defend in a design review, deliberately
not built, with the reason written down and a stated condition under which the decision is revisited.
It is the opposite of *we never got to it*.

MCP Apps is parked for Sutra. Three reasons, in the order of how much they weigh:

**1. The problem it would solve is already solved.** The one place Sutra needs a human decision inside
a tool call is the approval gate: *close ticket 4521 and its three duplicates, yes or no*. Day 37 built
that with **elicitation** — the server asks a structured question mid-tool, the client renders it
however it likes, and the answer comes back as one of three typed outcomes
([3.3](../../../day-37-auth-and-elicitation/parts/03-the-question/3.3-three-answers-not-two.md)). The
verdict is a value in a schema, not a sentence, which was the actual problem. An App would make that
same decision prettier. It would not make it more correct.

**2. It cannot be built on this budget.** Not for a lack of money but for a lack of the right shape of
thing. An App needs a **browser** and a **host that implements the bridge**, and the only free local
one ships as a Node project in the extension's repository. Addendum 02's rule is that nothing in this
curriculum may require a toolchain outside `uv` and Python, and this would require a Node install, an
`npm install`, and a repository clone kept out of git. That is a real dependency added for a feature
whose benefit is cosmetic.

**3. Rendering one is a client decision with its own blast radius.** By
[1.4](../01-what-a-server-declares/1.4-the-client-declares-too.md), Sutra only ever renders an App if
Sutra's client declares `io.modelcontextprotocol/ui` in its own capabilities. That declaration is a
standing permission for **every server Sutra mounts** to put a page in front of a user, not just for
Sutra's own. Principle 13 says a new power arrives with its containment story; the containment story
for that one is [6.2](../06-in-production/6.2-before-a-stranger-draws-on-your-screen.md)'s checklist,
and Sutra does not currently need the power the checklist would be protecting.

**What would change the decision.** Any one of these, and it is worth re-opening:

- Sutra grows a case where a human must compare several things at once — four tickets side by side,
  a diff, a chart. Elicitation renders one form; it does not render a comparison.
- Sutra is deployed inside a host that already supports Apps and where the approval flow is visibly
  worse than the rest of that host's interface.
- A Python host with the bridge appears that runs on `uv` alone, removing reason 2 entirely.

## Why Sutra needs it

The knowledge, not the feature. Three concrete places:

**Day 45's audit** asks of every mounted server: does it ship a UI, and do we render it? A row that
says *no, and here is the client capability we deliberately do not declare* is a stronger audit row
than one that says nothing, because it shows the question was asked.

**Phase 9's approval gates** will re-open this decision by design. The sketch below is what that phase
starts from, so that the argument is not had from scratch by somebody who has forgotten the reasons.

**Every interview about MCP after 2026.** Apps is the first official extension and it is the most
visible change to what an MCP server can be. Not having shipped one is fine. Not being able to explain
the security model is not.

The parking decision, written as the artefact Phase 9 will pick up. Fill it in your own words — this

Now the part that **is** built, and it is small on purpose. `sutra_mcp/capabilities.py` records the
decision as data, so that the absence of an App is a stated position rather than an omission:

```python
UI_EXTENSION = "io.modelcontextprotocol/ui"

# Parked, Day 41. Sutra declares no UI extension and registers no ui:// resource. The approval
# flow is served by elicitation (Day 37). Revisit when a decision needs several things compared
# side by side; see days/day-41-capabilities-and-mcp-apps/parts/04-a-server-that-draws/.
EXTENSIONS: dict[str, dict[str, Any]] = {}
UI_RESOURCES: tuple[str, ...] = ()
```

**Line by line:**

- `UI_EXTENSION` is defined even though it is not used in `EXTENSIONS`. It is the name a reader looks
  for, and defining it beside an empty map is what turns *"we do not do Apps"* from an absence into a
  sentence.
- The comment carries the **reason and the trigger**, not just the decision. A comment saying
  "parked" ages into a mystery within a year; a comment naming the condition under which it changes
  can be checked against reality.
- `EXTENSIONS: dict[str, dict[str, Any]] = {}` — an empty map with the right type. The day Sutra does
  declare an extension, the change is one entry, not a new structure and not a new argument threaded
  through `build_server()`.
- `UI_RESOURCES: tuple[str, ...] = ()` — an empty tuple, not a list, because it is a constant and a
  tuple says so. `lab/gate.py` reads both of these and fails if one is non-empty while the other is
  empty, which is what makes the pairing a rule rather than a convention.

## When it breaks

The break is a park that is not written down, and it fails on a Tuesday eighteen months later.

Somebody adds a `ui://` resource to `sutra_mcp/resources.py` because they saw the pattern in another
server and it looked like a nice touch. Nothing declares the extension, because nobody knew there was
an extension to declare. The resource sits in `resources/list` with mime `text/html;profile=mcp-app`,
and a host that supports Apps has a decision to make about a page whose author did not know they were
publishing one.

The gate catches exactly that:

```text
- 1 ui:// resources declared but io.modelcontextprotocol/ui is not claimed
findings: 1
```

The reverse — an extension claimed with nothing behind it — is the over-declaration from
[3.1](../03-declared-versus-implemented/3.1-a-promise-not-a-proof.md) in its most public form: telling
every client you render UI when there is no UI, which is a promise you will have to withdraw and
[5.2](../05-failure-lab/5.2-the-family-that-went-away.md) is what withdrawing costs.

The smallest fix is the pairing rule, and it is one check: **the extension claim and the `ui://`
resources are non-empty together or empty together, and never one without the other.**

## In production

**What a professional writes instead:** an architecture decision record. A park with three reasons and
a revisit trigger is exactly the content of an ADR, and putting it in `docs/adr/` rather than in a
comment means it survives a refactor of the file it was commented in. The comment then points at the
ADR.

**What degrades at scale:** unwritten decisions. One parked feature with the reasons in someone's head
is fine. Thirty of them is a codebase where nobody can tell a deliberate omission from a gap, and the
consequence is not a bug — it is that every proposal has to re-litigate ground that was settled years
ago, usually badly, because the person defending the old decision has left.

**The failure mode that only appears with real traffic:** the park that quietly became wrong. Reason 2
here is *there is no Python host with the bridge*. That is a fact about the ecosystem on 2026-09-04
and it is exactly the sort of fact that stops being true without anybody telling you. A park whose
reasons are external facts needs a re-check date, or it becomes folklore.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"'we do not support MCP Apps' is in a commit message
from last year and nowhere else. Put it in an ADR with the three reasons and the revisit trigger, and
add the gate check that a `ui://` resource cannot appear without the extension claim — otherwise the
next person to add a nice touch to a resource file will ship a UI nobody reviewed."*

**The interview question:** *"why did you not build your approval UI as an MCP App?"* The answer:
*"Because the thing we actually needed was a typed verdict, not a rendered one, and elicitation already
gives us that — the server asks a structured question mid-tool and gets back accept, decline or cancel
with typed content. An App would have made the same decision prettier, and it would have cost a Node
toolchain and a browser host that our zero-budget constraint does not allow. The third reason is the
one I would lead with in a design review: rendering an App means our *client* declares the UI
extension, and that is a standing permission for every server we mount to draw pixels in front of our
users, not just for our own server. That is a much bigger decision than the feature is worth today. We
wrote it down with a trigger — if we ever need a human to compare several things side by side,
elicitation cannot render that and we re-open it — and we added a check that a `ui://` resource cannot
appear in our server without the matching extension claim, so nobody ships one by accident."*
