# 5.1 — What a skill really costs, in this client

## One-line answer

A skill costs 479 tokens of fixed preamble whether or not anyone uses it, about 83 tokens in the index
when somebody asks, and 334 tokens per activated skill on every turn afterwards — but on Sutra's free
tier the cost that actually bites is **two extra generations out of twenty**.

## The story

The phone bill arrives and the calls are the small half.

There is the monthly rental, which you pay in a month when you are away and the phone is in a drawer.
There is a line for the plan, which is the same every month by definition. Then, at the bottom,
itemised: eleven minutes to a landline, a few messages, one call to a number you do not recognise.
The itemised part is where your attention goes, because it is the part that changes and the part you
feel responsible for.

The rental is bigger than all of it. It has been bigger than all of it for two years. Nobody looks at
the rental because there is nothing to decide about it each month — and that is exactly why it is
never the thing that gets reduced.

## The idea in plain language

Day 25 ended with a cost model taken from the format: cards are always loaded, the cost is linear,
two hundred skills is about 7,600 tokens of permanent context
([25.5.2](../../../day-25-skills-the-open-spec/parts/05-in-production/5.2-what-a-skill-costs.md)).

Every measurement in today's sections 2 and 3 says that model does not describe ADK 2.7.1. Here is the
one that does, measured on 2026-09-04 against `google-adk` 2.7.1 and `gemini-3.7-flash`:

| What | Tokens | When it is paid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the toolset preamble | **479** | every request, from the moment the toolset is attached |
| four tool declarations | not separately measured | every request |
| the index envelope | **12** | once, on a `list_skills` call |
| one skill's block in the index | **83** for `ticket-triage`, **35** for a one-line description | with the envelope, per skill |
| activating a skill | **334** for `ticket-triage`, of which 184 is the body | on activation, and every turn after |
| fetching one reference | **62** for `SEVERITY.md` | when the model follows a link |

Read it as three different shapes of cost, because they behave differently:

**The rental.** 479 tokens, unconditional, independent of everything. It does not grow with skills, it
cannot be reduced, and it is the largest single number on the list. It is also the one nobody notices,
for exactly the reason nobody notices the line rental.

**The itemised part.** The index and the activation, paid only when the model asks. Two hundred skills
the size of `ticket-triage` would be `12 + 200 × 83` ≈ **16,600 tokens** in a single tool result — more
than double Day 25's projection for the same shelf, and paid in one lump on the conversations that ask
rather than in slices on all of them.

**The rent-to-own part.** An activated body stays in the context for the rest of the conversation
([24.1.2](../../../day-24-token-accounting-and-budgets/parts/01-what-a-request-costs/1.2-charged-again-every-turn.md)),
so 334 tokens on turn two is 334 tokens on turns three through ten as well. In a ten-turn conversation
that activates one skill early, the skill costs about 3,000 tokens, not 334.

And then the number that actually governs Sutra. Day 24 established that the budget is denominated in
**requests, not tokens or dollars**
([24.2.3](../../../day-24-token-accounting-and-budgets/parts/02-two-ceilings/2.3-denominated-in-quota-not-dollars.md)):
twenty generations a day on the free tier. Each rung of the ladder is a **model turn**:

| Conversation | Generations |
| --- | --- |
| a question no skill governs, and the model does not ask | 1 |
| the model calls `list_skills` and then answers | 2 |
| `list_skills`, `load_skill`, answer | 3 |
| plus a reference fetch, plus two tool calls from the procedure | 5–6 |

**A skill-using conversation costs two to five times as many requests as one without.** On twenty a
day that is the difference between six triages and three. Nothing about token counts changes that
arithmetic, and no amount of trimming a description buys back a generation.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because Day 28 designs skills, and a design built on the wrong cost model optimises the wrong thing.

The Day 25 model says: keep the number of skills down, because every card is permanent. The measured
ADK model says something different and sharper: **the number of skills barely matters, and the number
of round trips matters enormously.** Those two lead to opposite designs. The first leads to a few large
skills; the second leads to skills whose bodies are complete enough that the model does not need a
reference fetch, because the fetch is a whole extra generation.

It also gives Sutra a defensible answer to the question Day 25 left as a `TODO(me)`: *how many skills
is Sutra willing to pay for at startup?* The measured answer is that startup cost is 479 tokens
regardless, so the constraint is not startup at all. The real constraint is that a shelf big enough to
make the index expensive is a shelf where the index should be searched rather than listed — and that
is a registry, which Sutra parks until Day 29.

## The paper behind it

> **MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems** · `arXiv:2310.08560` · 2023 ·
> <https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560>

It argued that a model can manage its own limited context by calling functions to page information in
and out, trading extra model turns for a smaller resident context — which is precisely the trade this
part is pricing.

## The mechanism

One script that prices the whole ladder for whatever is on your shelf.

```python
# days/day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/lab/price_the_ladder.py
"""Price every rung of the skills ladder against the pinned model."""

import asyncio
import json
import os
import pathlib

from google import genai
from google.adk.models.llm_request import LlmRequest
from google.adk.skills import load_skills_from_dir, prompt
from google.adk.tools import skill_toolset

from sutra.config import load_env

MODEL = "gemini-3.7-flash"
SHELF = pathlib.Path("days/day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/lab/skills")


async def main() -> None:
    """Measure the preamble, the index and one activation, then project."""
    load_env()
    client = genai.Client(api_key=os.environ["GOOGLE_API_KEY"])

    def cost(text: str) -> int:
        """Tokens for one string on the pinned model. Costs 0 generations."""
        return client.models.count_tokens(model=MODEL, contents=text).total_tokens

    skills = load_skills_from_dir(SHELF)
    toolset = skill_toolset.SkillToolset(skills=skills)

    request = LlmRequest()
    await toolset.process_llm_request(tool_context=None, llm_request=request)
    print(f"rung 0  preamble            {cost(request.config.system_instruction):>6}")

    envelope = cost(prompt.format_skills_as_xml([]))
    print(f"rung 1  index envelope      {envelope:>6}")
    for skill in skills:
        block = cost(prompt.format_skills_as_xml([skill])) - envelope
        print(f"        + {skill.name:<24}{block:>6}")

    for skill in skills:
        payload = json.dumps(
            {
                "skill_name": skill.name,
                "instructions": skill.instructions,
                "frontmatter": skill.frontmatter.model_dump(),
            }
        )
        print(f"rung 2  activate {skill.name:<18}{cost(payload):>6}")

    for skill in skills:
        for name in skill.resources.list_references():
            body = skill.resources.get_reference(name)
            print(f"rung 3  {skill.name}/{name:<22}{cost(body):>6}")


asyncio.run(main())
```

**Line by line:**

- `cost()` closes over the client and records the quota fact in its docstring, the same helper as
  [2.3](../02-the-four-tools/2.3-what-activation-actually-costs.md). Repeating a three-line helper in
  two lab scripts is better than importing across scripts that are meant to be read independently.
- The **envelope is measured and subtracted**, so each skill's block is its own contribution rather
  than its contribution plus twelve tokens of tags. Without the subtraction, adding two skills looks
  like it costs twice the envelope, and the projection to two hundred skills is wrong by a couple of
  thousand tokens.
- `prompt.format_skills_as_xml` is the same function the `list_skills` tool calls, imported from
  `google.adk.skills`. Measuring the real formatter rather than a reconstruction is the difference
  between a measurement and an estimate.
- The rung-2 payload is **rebuilt** rather than obtained by calling `load_skill`, because calling the
  tool would need a context and would write to state. The three keys are copied from what
  [2.3](../02-the-four-tools/2.3-what-activation-actually-costs.md) observed the tool returning; if
  ADK changes that shape, this script drifts, and the comment in
  [5.2](5.2-experimental-means-experimental.md) about re-verifying applies here too.
- `skill.resources.list_references()` rather than iterating the dictionary directly, because the
  accessor is the public surface and the dictionary is an implementation detail
  ([1.2](../01-loading-a-folder/1.2-the-skill-object.md)).
- **Zero generations.** Every number comes from `count_tokens`.

Run it from the repository root:

```bash
uv run python days/day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/lab/price_the_ladder.py
```

**Line by line:**

- Needs `GOOGLE_API_KEY`; spends **0** of the twenty daily generations.

Measured on 2026-09-04 with `ticket-triage` and `vague-description` on the shelf:

```text
rung 0  preamble               479
rung 1  index envelope          12
        + ticket-triage               83
        + vague-description           35
rung 2  activate ticket-triage        334
rung 2  activate vague-description     98
rung 3  ticket-triage/SEVERITY.md               62
```

The useless skill costs 35 tokens in the index and would cost 98 to activate. It has never been
activated and never will be
([25.4.1](../../../day-25-skills-the-open-spec/parts/04-failure-lab/4.1-the-skill-that-never-triggered.md)),
so its real cost is 35 tokens on every conversation that lists skills, forever, in exchange for
nothing. That is the shape of the expensive skill: not the big one, the one that never fires.

One number here moves as you work through the day. That `334` was measured on the shelf copy of
`ticket-triage` **before** [3.2](../03-wiring-the-toolset/3.2-tools-that-appear-after-activation.md)
adds `adk_additional_tools` to its metadata. With that one line added, the same measurement returns
**353**: nineteen tokens, paid on every activation and every turn afterwards, for a key the model
cannot use and that exists to grant it a tool. Cheap, and worth having seen — it is the frontmatter
dump from [2.3](../02-the-four-tools/2.3-what-activation-actually-costs.md) charging you for your own
configuration.

## When it breaks

**Measuring the body and calling it the cost.** The body is 184 of the 334. A team that optimises
bodies and reports a 40 per cent reduction has reduced the total by 22 per cent, and has not touched
the 479.

**Measuring one request instead of one conversation.** The activation is a one-off; carrying it is not.
Any cost claim about skills that does not say *per conversation* is describing the cheapest possible
case.

**Counting tokens when the ceiling is requests.** This is the specifically Sutra-shaped mistake. On a
paid tier the token numbers above are the bill. On the free tier they are almost irrelevant next to the
generation count, and a change that saves 200 tokens while adding a round trip has made things worse.
Day 24 named this and it is worth naming again: **quota is the currency**
([24.2.3](../../../day-24-token-accounting-and-budgets/parts/02-two-ceilings/2.3-denominated-in-quota-not-dollars.md)).

**Projecting from one skill.** The per-skill index block was 83 tokens for one description and 35 for
another — a factor of more than two, entirely because of description length. A projection to two
hundred skills built on either number alone is wrong. Measure the shelf you have.

## In production

**Put the fixed cost in the runbook, once, with its date.** 479 tokens for the preamble, measured
against `google-adk` 2.7.1 on 2026-09-04. It is a constant until an upgrade changes it, and writing
down the version and the date is what makes it possible to notice that it has.

**Budget the ladder in round trips, not tokens, on any rate-limited tier.** The design questions that
follow are concrete: should this reference be in the body, so that the model does not spend a
generation fetching it? Should two related skills be one, so that one activation covers both? Those
are different questions from "is this body too long", and they are the ones that move the number that
is actually scarce.

**Price the skill that never fires.** Every conversation that lists skills pays for every card. A shelf
of forty skills where six are used is thirty-four cards of pure loss on every listing, and the fix is
deletion rather than optimisation. The activation counts from
[3.3](../03-wiring-the-toolset/3.3-activation-is-a-line-in-state.md) are the input; a quarterly review
that deletes anything at zero is the process.

**What changes at scale** is that the index becomes the biggest single message in the conversation.
Sixteen thousand tokens arriving as one tool result is a different failure from a gradual drift: it
lands in the middle of the context, which is where Day 19 measured attention to be weakest
([19.3.3](../../../day-19-context-engineering-selection/parts/03-selection/3.3-position-is-not-presence.md)),
and it arrives all at once. Long before that becomes a cost problem it is a **selection** problem, and
the answer is search rather than listing.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"the cost note in this design doc says a skill costs
184 tokens. That is the body. Measured, activation is 334 and the toolset adds 479 on every request
regardless. On our tier none of that matters as much as the two extra round trips per skill-using
conversation — please redo the section in requests."*

**The interview question:** *"what does adding skills to an agent cost?"* The answer that shows you
have measured rather than repeated: *"three shapes. A fixed preamble — for us 479 tokens on every
request whether skills are used or not. A conditional index, about 83 tokens per skill, paid only when
the model calls the listing tool. And an activation, 334 tokens for our smallest skill, carried on
every subsequent turn of that conversation. On a rate-limited tier the real cost is none of those: each
rung is a model round trip, so a skill-using conversation spends two to three times the requests. I
measured all of it with the token-counting endpoint, which is free."*
