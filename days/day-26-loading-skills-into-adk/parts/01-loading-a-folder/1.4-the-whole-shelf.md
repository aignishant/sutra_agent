# 1.4 — The whole shelf in one call, all or nothing

## One-line answer

`load_skills_from_dir(folder)` loads every skill under a folder in one call, `list_skills_in_dir`
loads only their cards — and the first of those two is **all or nothing**: one unloadable skill and
you get no skills at all.

## The story

The office printer is doing a forty-page job and it stops on page twelve.

Page twelve is the one somebody scanned at a different paper size. The printer will not print it, and
because the job was sent as one job, it will not print pages thirteen to forty either. The screen says
something about a size mismatch and waits. Eleven good pages are in the tray. Twenty-eight good pages
are not, and there is nothing wrong with any of them.

The person standing there did not send a broken job. They sent one broken page inside a job, and the
machine's rule is that a job either goes through or it does not.

You can argue about whether that rule is right. What you cannot do is be surprised by it twice.

## The idea in plain language

Sutra will have more than one skill. Yesterday's build brief put `ticket-triage` under `skills/` at
the repository root, and Day 27 adds more. Writing a `load_skill_from_dir` call per skill would mean
editing Python every time somebody adds a folder, which defeats the point of a format that
non-engineers can contribute to.

ADK ships two functions for the whole-folder case, and they are not the same tool:

| Call | Returns | Reads | Use it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `list_skills_in_dir(folder)` | `dict[str, Frontmatter]` | only each `SKILL.md`'s frontmatter | you want to know what is there |
| `load_skills_from_dir(folder)` | `list[Skill]` | every file in every skill folder | you are about to hand them to an agent |

`list_skills_in_dir` is the cheap one. It gives you the cards — name and description and the four
optional fields — keyed by name, with no bodies and no resources. That is the right call for a startup
log line, an admin endpoint, or a lint that wants to check descriptions without paying for the rest.

`load_skills_from_dir` is the real one, and it has one behaviour that will cost you an evening if you
meet it for the first time in production: **it is all or nothing**. It walks the subdirectories, calls
`load_skill_from_dir` on each, and lets the first exception out. Nine good skills and one with a
capital letter in its name gives you an exception, not nine skills.

Sitting beside that is a second rule that behaves in the *opposite* direction, and the pair is the
thing to remember:

- A subfolder with **no `SKILL.md`** is **silently skipped**. Not an error, not a warning.
- A subfolder with a **broken `SKILL.md`** takes **the whole call down**.

So a `README/` folder on the shelf is invisible, and a typo in one skill's name is fatal to all of
them. Neither is unreasonable on its own. Together they mean the failure you actually get is rarely
the one you expected.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because the desk agent's shelf has to be a directory, not a list in a Python file.

The whole argument of Day 25 was that a skill is a folder a non-engineer can write
([25.1.1](../../../day-25-skills-the-open-spec/parts/01-what-a-skill-is/1.1-a-skill-is-a-folder.md)).
That argument only pays off if adding a skill is *adding a folder*. The moment it also requires an
import and a line in a list, the person who knows how triage works is back to filing a ticket against
the engineering team.

The all-or-nothing rule is why Sutra needs a decision about it today rather than on the day it bites.
The choice is between **fail fast** — the shelf is broken, refuse to start, and let the deploy fail
loudly — and **skip and log**, where a bad skill is dropped and named in the log. Both are defensible.
[4.1](../04-failure-lab/4.1-one-bad-folder.md) is the failure lab that makes you choose, and Principle
10 has an opinion: whichever you choose, the broken skill must be **named somewhere a human reads**.
Silently loading nine of ten is the one option that is not available.

## The mechanism

Both calls, side by side, over a folder holding two skills.

```python
# days/day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/lab/load_shelf.py
"""Read a whole shelf two ways: the cards only, then everything."""

import pathlib

from google.adk.skills import list_skills_in_dir, load_skills_from_dir

SHELF = pathlib.Path("days/day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/lab/skills")

print("--- list_skills_in_dir: cards only ---")
for name, card in sorted(list_skills_in_dir(SHELF).items()):
    print(f"{name:20} {card.description[:60]}...")

print("\n--- load_skills_from_dir: everything ---")
for skill in sorted(load_skills_from_dir(SHELF), key=lambda s: s.name):
    print(
        f"{skill.name:20} body={len(skill.instructions):5}  "
        f"refs={list(skill.resources.references)}  "
        f"scripts={list(skill.resources.scripts)}"
    )
```

**Line by line:**

- Both functions take the **parent** folder, not a skill folder. Pointing either of them at
  `skills/ticket-triage/` finds no subdirectories with a `SKILL.md` in them and returns nothing — an
  empty result, not an error, which is the silent-skip rule biting in its mildest form.
- `list_skills_in_dir` returns a **dictionary keyed by skill name**, so `.items()` gives you name and
  card together. The key comes from the frontmatter, not from the directory, though 1.1's rule means
  they agree.
- `card` is a `Frontmatter`, the same type as `skill.frontmatter` in [1.2](1.2-the-skill-object.md).
  There is no `instructions` on it to print, because none was read.
- `load_skills_from_dir` returns a **list**, not a dictionary, and the order is the filesystem's.
  `sorted(..., key=lambda s: s.name)` makes the output stable; without it, two machines print two
  orders and you waste ten minutes wondering what changed.
- The f-string is split across three adjacent literals so the line stays inside the repository's
  width. Python joins them at parse time.
- Nothing here catches an exception. That is deliberate: this part shows the calls, and
  [4.1](../04-failure-lab/4.1-one-bad-folder.md) shows what to do about the one that raises.

Run it from the repository root, with two skills on the shelf:

```bash
uv run python days/day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/lab/load_shelf.py
```

**Line by line:**

- The shelf for this day is `days/day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/lab/skills/`, built in the hub's §3
  by copying yesterday's `ticket-triage` and `vague-description` into it. Keeping the experiments off
  the repository-root `skills/` folder means a broken specimen cannot break the real desk agent.
- **Zero model calls.**

Measured on 2026-09-04 against `google-adk` 2.7.1:


No mention of `notes/` anywhere. It was skipped, and nothing said so.

## When it breaks

**The one broken skill that returns none of them.** This is the headline, and the exception says
nothing about which folder it came from:

```text
ValidationError: 1 validation error for Frontmatter
name
  Value error, name must be lowercase kebab-case (a-z, 0-9, hyphens), with no leading, trailing, or consecutive delimiters [type=value_error, input_value='Ticket--Triage', input_type=str]
```

Read that with fifty skills on the shelf and you know the rule that was broken and not the file that
broke it. The traceback has `_load_skills_from_dir` calling `_load_skill_from_dir` and no path in
between. The fix is to loop yourself, which is what [4.1](../04-failure-lab/4.1-one-bad-folder.md)
builds.

**The empty result that looks like a bug in ADK.** Point either call at a skill folder rather than the
shelf:

```text
--- list_skills_in_dir: cards only ---

--- load_skills_from_dir: everything ---
```

No exception, no rows, nothing. Two prints and a blank. The mistake is one directory level and the
feedback is silence, which is why the first thing to check when a shelf comes back empty is the path,
and the second is whether the folders under it actually contain `SKILL.md` rather than `Skill.md` or
`SKILLS.md`.

**A hidden or tooling folder on the shelf.** `.git`, `__pycache__`, `.DS_Store` — all silently skipped
by the same rule that skips `notes/`, which is the rule working in your favour for once. Do not rely
on it for anything you care about: the guarantee is "no `SKILL.md`, no skill", not "we understand
which folders are yours".

## In production

**Load the shelf yourself, one skill at a time.** The two-line version of the fix is a loop with a
`try` around `load_skill_from_dir`, and the reason to write it is not elegance — it is that the log
line inside the `except` can name the folder. That single line turns "the service will not start" into
"the service will not start because `skills/refund-policy/` has a bad name", and those are ten minutes
apart at three in the morning.

**Decide fail-fast or skip-and-log once, at the top.** The right answer depends on what the skill is:
a desk that has lost its triage procedure and does not say so is a desk that will quietly improvise
severities. If a missing skill changes the answers, refuse to start. If it only removes an option,
skip it, name it in the log, and count it — Day 22's structured line is the place
([22.2.1](../../../day-22-structured-logging/parts/02-wiring-the-run/2.1-one-line-per-happening.md)),
and a `skills_loaded` and `skills_rejected` pair at startup is worth more than any amount of
after-the-fact archaeology.

**Do not reload the shelf per request.** The whole folder walk, every reference file, every script, on
every request. Load at startup and expose a deliberate reload if you need one; that reload is also the
natural place to validate before swapping, so a bad edit does not take the running service down with
it.

**What changes at scale** is that the shelf grows a curation problem before it grows a performance
problem. Fifty folders is still a fast load. Fifty *descriptions* is the thing section 2 is about to
charge you for, and it is also fifty chances for two skills to sound like each other. The loading is
never the bottleneck; the selecting is.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"`load_skills_from_dir` at startup means one typo in
any skill takes the service down with a traceback that does not name the file. Loop and catch, log the
folder, and decide explicitly whether we start without it."*

**The interview question:** *"what happens if one skill in your skills directory is malformed?"* The
answer that shows you have run it: *"with the built-in bulk loader, nothing loads — it is all or
nothing, and the exception names the rule rather than the folder. So we load them one at a time and
log the folder name on failure, and we made an explicit decision about whether a missing skill is a
startup failure or a logged degradation. A folder with no `SKILL.md` at all is skipped silently, which
is the opposite behaviour, so both cases had to be handled deliberately."*
