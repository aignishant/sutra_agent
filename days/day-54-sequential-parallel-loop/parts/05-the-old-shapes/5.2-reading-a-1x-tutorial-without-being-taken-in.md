# 5.2 — Reading a 1.x tutorial without being taken in

## One-line answer

Most of a 1.x example still translates line for line, and the three places it does not are the join,
the loop's cap, and event handling — so the skill is knowing which three paragraphs to distrust rather
than discarding the whole page.

## The story

You ask a neighbour how to get to the recycling centre on the far side of town and she tells you, in
detail, because she drove it every week for years.

Left at the bakery, past the school, right at the roundabout with the flowerbed, then keep
going until the road bends left and it is on your right after the second set of lights.

Nearly all of that is still true. The bakery is there, the school is there, the flowerbed is there. What she does not know is that they built the bypass two years ago, and the road that used to
bend left now feeds onto it, and if you follow her directions exactly you end up committed to a
dual carriageway going the wrong way with no turning for four miles.

She was not wrong and she was not out of date about the town. She was out of date about one junction,
and the value of her directions depends entirely on your knowing which one.

## The idea in plain language

There is a lot of ADK 1.x material in circulation, and it was written by people who knew what they
were doing. Throwing all of it away is expensive and unnecessary. Following it exactly is worse.

What you need is a short list of the junctions that moved. For the material this day covers, there
are three, and they are three because that is how many of trap #1's consequences actually change the
code you write.

**Junction one: the shape classes.** `SequentialAgent`, `ParallelAgent` and `LoopAgent` map onto edge
lists, per the table in
[5.1](5.1-the-three-classes-that-still-import.md). This translation is mechanical and safe.

**Junction two: the join.** A 1.x parallel agent has no join, because in a tree there is nowhere to
put one. The step after a parallel agent received *a* result. In 2.x the equivalent needs an explicit
`JoinNode` or the downstream node runs once per branch — measured in
[2.1](../02-at-the-same-time/2.1-three-branches-from-one-edge.md). A tutorial will not mention this,
because in its world the question does not arise.

**Junction three: the loop's cap.** A 1.x `LoopAgent` has a `max_iterations` field. The graph runtime
has no equivalent, so a translated loop that does not grow a cap of its own has quietly lost one — and
as [5.1](5.1-the-three-classes-that-still-import.md) measured, `max_iterations` defaults to `None`, so
the tutorial you are copying may not have had a cap either.

Two more junctions belong to other days and are worth naming so you recognise them when a tutorial
crosses them: **trap #2**, event field names and shapes, which changed in 2.0 — never copy event
handling from a 1.x page — and **trap #3**, custom agents that collect events into a list and return
them, where 2.x nodes **yield** events as they happen. Day 53 covers the node model and Day 7 covered
events; the rule for reading is simply that any 1.x code touching an `Event` is the paragraph to
distrust most.

## Why Sutra needs it

Ninety-six days is long enough that you will need something this curriculum has not covered, and you
will search for it. The result will be a page that works, on 2.7.1, because the deprecated classes
still import ([5.1](5.1-the-three-classes-that-still-import.md)).

Principle 8 says every ADK symbol is verified against adk.dev on the day it is used. This part is the
practical form of that rule: not "distrust everything", which nobody sustains, but a short checklist
you can run over a page in the time it takes to read it.

## The mechanism

A 1.x example, translated, with the three junctions marked. Here is the shape a tutorial would show
for a research-then-review agent:

```python
# 1.x. Runs, on google-adk 2.7.1, with a DeprecationWarning you will not see (part 5.1).
research = ParallelAgent(name="research", sub_agents=[archive, knowledge_base, open_incidents])
review = LoopAgent(name="review", sub_agents=[drafter, critic], max_iterations=3)
root = SequentialAgent(name="triage", sub_agents=[intake, classify, research, review, send])
```

**Line by line:**

- `ParallelAgent(sub_agents=[...])` — the three lookups. Note there is nothing here about what happens
  to the three results; the tree has no vocabulary for that question.
- `LoopAgent(..., max_iterations=3)` — the cap, present and set. A tutorial that sets it is being
  responsible, and it is the field that has no 2.x equivalent.
- `SequentialAgent(sub_agents=[...])` — the spine, with the two composite agents sitting in it as
  ordinary members. That nesting is the tree model: an agent whose children are agents.

And the translation:

```python
gather = JoinNode(name="gather")  # junction two: nothing to translate FROM
MAX_ROUNDS = 3  # junction three: the cap moves into your code

workflow = Workflow(
    name="triage",
    edges=[
        (
            START,
            intake,
            classify,
            (archive, knowledge_base, open_incidents),
            gather,
            drafter,
            critic,
        ),
        Edge(from_node=critic, to_node=drafter, route="revise"),
        Edge(from_node=critic, to_node=send, route="send"),
    ],
    max_concurrency=2,
)
```

**Line by line:**

- The whole tree becomes one edge list. Five agents and two composites became one tuple and two
  edges, and the nesting disappeared — a fan-out is a tuple inside a tuple rather than an object
  inside an object.
- `gather = JoinNode(name="gather")` — **junction two.** There is no line in the 1.x version that
  corresponds to this. It is not a translation of anything; it is a thing you have to know to add.
- `MAX_ROUNDS = 3` — **junction three.** The number came from `max_iterations`, and it now has to be
  read and enforced by the critic, in state, as
  [3.5](../03-around-again/3.5-the-guard-you-write-yourself.md) sets out. The constant survives the
  translation; the enforcement does not, because there is nothing left to enforce it.
- `Edge(from_node=critic, to_node=drafter, route="revise")` — the loop, now explicit. In the 1.x
  version the looping was implied by the class; here it is an edge you can see, which is the whole
  argument of [1.3](../01-one-after-another/1.3-why-calling-them-in-order-is-not-a-workflow.md).
- `max_concurrency=2` — a 2.x control with no 1.x equivalent at all, from
  [2.5](../02-at-the-same-time/2.5-fan-out-against-a-quota.md). Junctions cut both ways: some things
  the new road has that the old directions could not mention.

The checklist, for a page you are reading now:

| Look for | Because |
| --- | --- |
| `SequentialAgent` · `ParallelAgent` · `LoopAgent` | the page is 1.x; translate per [5.1](5.1-the-three-classes-that-still-import.md) |
| a step after a `ParallelAgent` | you need a `JoinNode` the page does not have |
| `max_iterations` | the cap has to be rewritten into a node; there is no field for it |
| anything reading `Event` attributes | trap #2: the fields changed; check the 2.x event reference |
| a custom agent returning a list of events | trap #3: 2.x nodes yield as they go |
| `sub_agents=` | the tree model; ask what the edge list would be |

## When it breaks

The break is the translation that looks complete. Take the 1.x example above, translate junctions one
and three carefully, and forget junction two:

```python
workflow = Workflow(
    name="triage",
    edges=[
        (START, intake, classify, (archive, knowledge_base, open_incidents), drafter, critic),
        Edge(from_node=critic, to_node=drafter, route="revise"),
        Edge(from_node=critic, to_node=send, route="send"),
    ],
)
```

**Line by line:**

- The fan-out tuple is there, the loop is routed, the cap is in the critic. Two junctions handled.
- `gather` is missing, so `drafter` sits directly downstream of three branches.

Nothing fails. `drafter` runs three times, once per branch, each time with one lookup's result —
exactly the measurement in [2.1](../02-at-the-same-time/2.1-three-branches-from-one-edge.md). Three
drafts go to the critic, the critic reviews the last one to arrive, and a reply is sent.

The reply is written from one of the three research results, and which one depends on the completion
order from [2.3](../02-at-the-same-time/2.3-the-order-you-cannot-rely-on.md). It reads fine. It is
about the right ticket. It is missing two thirds of what the desk knows, and there is no error, no
warning and no log line anywhere in the run.

That is the dual carriageway. You followed good directions and there was no sign at the junction.

The smallest fix is the one line: put a `JoinNode` between the fan-out and the node after it. The
general fix is the checklist — and specifically, the habit of asking *"how many times does the step
after this parallel section run?"* every time you translate one.

## In production

**What a professional writes instead.** When they translate a page they translate it into a **test**
first, not into the application. A ten-line script with plain functions as nodes, run once, printing
the outputs — exactly the shape of this day's lab. `drafter` running three times is instantly visible
in that output and invisible inside a real system where each run makes a model call and produces
plausible text. Translating into a throwaway script costs a couple of minutes and answers the
question the tutorial could not.

**What degrades at scale.** Half-translated codebases, as
[5.1](5.1-the-three-classes-that-still-import.md) noted. The specific version here is worse because it
is invisible: a `SequentialAgent` in the codebase is greppable, and a graph that is missing a join
looks exactly like a graph that does not need one. There is no search that finds it. The only defence
is at translation time.

**The failure mode that only appears with real traffic.** The missing join, at every scale, forever.
It produces more work rather than less — three drafts instead of one — so it does not show up as a
timeout or a failure. It shows up as three times the model spend for a worse answer, which on a free
tier means the quota goes and the replies get thinner, and neither symptom points at a graph.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"This looks like it came from a 1.x example. The
translation is good, but the `ParallelAgent` step needs a `JoinNode` — right now the drafter runs once
per branch. Also, where did `max_iterations=3` go? The graph runtime has no equivalent, so the cap has
to be in the critic."*

**The interview question:** *"Most of the ADK material online is for 1.x. How do you use it?"* An
honest answer: *"I read it for the intent and translate the structure, with a short list of what
changed. The three that matter for workflows are the shape classes becoming an edge list, the parallel
step needing an explicit join that has no 1.x equivalent, and `max_iterations` having no equivalent at
all so the cap has to be rewritten into a node. Then two more around events, which changed shape in
2.0 and which 2.x nodes yield rather than collect. The one that actually bit me is the join, because
forgetting it does not fail — the downstream node just runs once per branch and produces a plausible
answer from a third of the data. So I translate into a ten-line script with plain functions first and
read the outputs, before any of it goes near the application."*
