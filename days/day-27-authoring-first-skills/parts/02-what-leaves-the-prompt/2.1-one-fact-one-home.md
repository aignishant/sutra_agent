# 2.1 — One fact, one home: shrinking the instruction

## One-line answer

Extraction has a second half nobody does: once a rule is in the skill, it comes **out** of the
instruction — and Sutra's shrinks from 294 tokens to 157, including the deletion of one sentence that
has been false for twenty days.

## The story

There are two clocks in the house and they disagree by four minutes.

The kitchen one is on the wall above the door. The other is on the microwave, three feet away, and it
has been four minutes fast since somebody set it after a power cut. Everybody knows. Everybody has
adapted: you trust the wall for leaving the house and the microwave for cooking, and it works, in the
sense that nobody has been late.

Then the wall clock's battery dies and gets replaced, and whoever replaced it set it from the
microwave.

The house is now four minutes fast and nobody knows, because the thing that used to make the error
visible — two clocks disagreeing — has quietly resolved itself into a single consistent wrong answer.

## The idea in plain language

Extraction is a **move**, not a copy. That sentence is the whole part and it is the step everybody
skips, because copying is safe and deleting feels risky.

The result of skipping it is a rule in two places. And a rule in two places is not twice as
well-enforced; it is a rule that will eventually exist in two versions, one of which is stale, with no
way to tell which. That is the same DRY argument applied to code, arriving at prose — and prose is
worse, because nothing compiles it and nothing tests it.

So after section 1, every sentence in `sutra/desk/agent.py`'s `INSTRUCTION` gets one of three verdicts:

**Moved.** It is procedure — a step, a failure branch, a rule about the KB. It is now in the skill and
comes out of the instruction.

**Stays.** It is identity, scope or standing behaviour: who Sutra is, what it refuses, how it talks.
Those are true whether or not any skill fires, and a skill is optional
([26.4.3](../../../day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/parts/04-failure-lab/4.3-the-manual-nobody-opened.md)),
so anything that must always hold cannot depend on one.

**Deleted.** It was never true, or it stopped being true. Sutra has one of these and it is instructive.

Here is the verdict on Sutra's instruction, sentence by sentence:

| Sentence | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *"You are Sutra, the triage assistant…"* | stays | identity; true with no skill loaded |
| *"You do exactly three things: 1 explain… 2 suggest a cause… 3 recommend a priority"* | **moved** | that is the procedure, and it is now seven numbered steps |
| *"Anything else is out of scope: refunds, release dates…"* | stays | refusal; must hold when no skill fires |
| *"You cannot look anything up. You have no ticket database…"* | **deleted** | false since Day 4 |
| *"Never invent ticket contents, customer names, article titles or dates"* | stays | honesty; must hold always |
| *"Plain words, no filler, at most three sentences…"* | stays | tone; applies to every answer |
| *"Example: Engineer: Ticket 4521 came in…"* | **deleted** | it demonstrates the deleted sentence |

Four stay, two move, two go. And the two that go are the interesting ones, because they were not
wrong when they were written. Day 6's desk agent genuinely had no tools attached — the honesty block
was accurate on the day it shipped. Then Day 10 gave Sutra function tools, nobody re-read Day 6's
file, and a false statement about the system's own capabilities has been in every request since.

That is not carelessness. It is the ordinary fate of a rule written in a file that nobody has a reason
to re-open. It is also exactly why *one fact, one home* is worth the discomfort of deleting.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because Sutra is about to attach a `SkillToolset`, and that adds 479 tokens of framework preamble to
the same field
([26.2.1](../../../day-26-loading-skills-into-adk/parts/02-the-four-tools/2.1-the-preamble-you-did-not-write.md)).

Day 6 argued that every line of the handbook is a tax
([6.1.5](../../../day-06-instructions-and-personas/parts/01-writing-the-handbook/1.5-every-line-is-a-tax.md))
and the instruction was kept short on that argument. Attaching a toolset that silently adds nearly
five hundred tokens to the same field, and *then* leaving the procedure in the instruction as well, is
paying twice for the same knowledge and then adding rent.

There is a second reason and it is sharper. The framework's preamble says, in capitals, *"you MUST use
the skill tools to interact with these skills"*. Sutra's instruction currently says *"you do exactly
three things"* and lists them. Those two are not the same instruction, and Day 6 measured what a
contradiction in one field produces: not an error, but behaviour that varies run to run
([6.1.4](../../../day-06-instructions-and-personas/parts/01-writing-the-handbook/1.4-contradictions-are-randomised-behaviour.md)).
Removing the procedure removes the contradiction.

## The mechanism

The instruction after the move. Compare it against the version in `sutra/desk/agent.py`.

```python
# sutra/desk/agent.py - the handbook, after the procedure moved into skills/ (Day 27).
INSTRUCTION = """# Role
You are Sutra, the triage assistant for a software company's support desk.
You work for the support engineers, not for the customer.

# Scope
Triage support tickets. Anything else - refunds, release dates, account
changes, company policy - is out of scope. Say this and nothing more:
"That's outside triage. I'll flag it for a human colleague."

# Honesty
Never invent ticket contents, customer names, article ids or dates. If a
lookup fails, say it failed. "I don't know" is a complete answer.

# Tone
Plain words, no filler, at most three sentences unless asked for detail.
Say "probably" when you mean probably.
"""
```

**Line by line:**

- **`# Role` is unchanged.** Identity does not move into a skill, because a skill is optional and
  identity is not. An agent that forgets who it works for when no skill fires is worse than one with
  no skill at all.
- **`# Scope` lost its numbered list.** *"You do exactly three things"* was the procedure in disguise,
  and the procedure is now `skills/ticket-triage/`. What is left is the boundary — triage, and not the
  four things that are not triage — which is a refusal rule and has to hold unconditionally.
- The refusal sentence is kept **verbatim**, including its exact wording. A refusal the agent has to
  compose itself is a refusal that will be composed differently each time, and consistency here is the
  whole point of writing it down.
- **`# Honesty` lost its first two sentences and kept its third**, with one word changed: *"article
  titles"* became *"article ids"*, because Sutra now cites ids
  ([1.4](../01-extraction/1.4-steps-for-a-competent-stranger.md)) and the honesty rule should name the
  thing that can actually be invented.
- The sentence *"You cannot look anything up"* is **gone**, and it is worth pausing on the fact that
  deleting it took a whole part to justify. Twenty days of a false claim survived because deleting
  something is a decision and leaving it is not.
- *"If a lookup fails, say it failed"* is **new**, and it is the general version of the skill's step 1.
  This is the one deliberate overlap in the whole exercise: the skill says exactly what to do when
  `lookup_ticket` misses, and the instruction says the unconditional version, because it has to hold on
  the runs where no skill was loaded. The rule for a deliberate overlap is that the **instruction
  states the principle and the skill states the procedure** — never the same sentence twice.
- **`# Tone` is unchanged** and stays in the instruction rather than moving to `kb-answer-style`,
  because it governs every answer including refusals, and `kb-answer-style` only fires when a
  customer-facing reply is being written.
- **The `# Example` block is gone.** It demonstrated the deleted honesty sentence, and an example that
  teaches a rule you removed is worse than no example.

Measure both, because this is a claim with a number attached:

```python
# days/day-27-authoring-first-skills/lab/instruction_diet.py
"""Price the instruction before and after the procedure moved into a skill."""

import os

from google import genai

from sutra.config import load_env
from sutra.desk.agent import INSTRUCTION

load_env()
client = genai.Client(api_key=os.environ["GOOGLE_API_KEY"])
counted = client.models.count_tokens(model="gemini-3.7-flash", contents=INSTRUCTION)
print("instruction:", len(INSTRUCTION), "chars,", counted.total_tokens, "tokens")
```

**Line by line:**

- It imports `INSTRUCTION` from the module rather than pasting a copy, so the number is about the file
  that actually ships. A measurement of a string in a lab script is a measurement of the lab script.
- `count_tokens` again, on the separate endpoint, so this costs **0** generations
  ([24.1.1](../../../day-24-token-accounting-and-budgets/parts/01-what-a-request-costs/1.1-a-ticket-id-costs-five-tokens.md)).
- Run it once before the edit and once after. Two numbers, one edit, and a claim you can defend.

Measured on 2026-09-04:

```text
before  : 1140 chars, 294 tokens
after   :  622 chars, 157 tokens
```

**137 tokens saved on every request, forever** — and paid back immediately, because the toolset's
preamble is 479. The instruction got smaller and the request got bigger, which is the honest
accounting and the thing nobody puts in the slide.

What was actually bought is not tokens. It is that the procedure now lives in a file a support engineer
can edit, and there is one copy of it.

## When it breaks

**The copy that was never deleted.** The most common outcome of this whole day: the skill is written,
the instruction is untouched, and the system works fine. It keeps working fine for months. Then
somebody updates the rubric in the skill and the instruction still says *"recommend a priority - low,
normal or high"*, and the model has two vocabularies for the same field.

**The rule that was moved and should not have been.** Move the refusal into a skill and an agent
without that skill loaded will happily discuss refunds. The test is
[2.2](2.2-what-never-leaves-code.md)'s and it is one question: *what happens if this skill never
fires?*

**The deletion nobody could justify.** *"You cannot look anything up"* was deletable because it was
demonstrably false — `lookup_ticket` exists. Most instruction lines are not that clear-cut, and the
honest process is to move what is procedure, keep what is unconditional, and leave the genuinely
ambiguous ones alone until you have a reason. A shrinking exercise that becomes its own goal produces
an instruction with holes in it.

**The instruction and the preamble arguing.** After the edit, read Sutra's instruction and the
framework's 479 tokens side by side, as one document, because that is what the model receives. If your
`# Scope` still enumerates steps, it is competing with the preamble's *"follow the skill exactly"*, and
one of them will win at random.

## In production

**Do the move in one change.** Skill added and instruction trimmed in the same commit, so the diff
shows the transfer. Two commits means a window in which the rule is in both places, and windows like
that have a way of lasting a quarter.

**Keep a written rule for what lives where.** Sutra's is three lines and belongs in `skills/README.md`:
identity, refusal, honesty and tone live in the instruction; procedures live in skills; policy lives in
`references/`. Without it, the next person adds a rule to whichever file they happened to have open.

**Re-read the instruction every time capabilities change.** The stale sentence got there because tools
arrived and nobody re-read the handbook. A one-line checklist item on any change that adds or removes a
tool — *"does the instruction still describe what this agent can do?"* — is cheap and would have caught
it on Day 10.

**What changes at scale** is that the instruction becomes the smallest and most contested file you
have. Twenty skills, and everything specific has moved out; what is left is the constitution, and every
team wants one sentence in it. The discipline that holds is a rule about admission — a line earns a
place in the instruction only if it must be true on a request where no skill fires — and a review that
enforces it.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"the skill is good, and the instruction still
contains the same procedure. Delete it there. Right now a change to the rubric only takes effect if
the model happens to read the skill rather than the prompt, and we cannot tell which it did."*

**The interview question:** *"you moved a procedure into a skill — what did you take out of the
prompt?"* The answer that shows it was a move and not a copy: *"the numbered steps and one honesty
sentence that had been false since we added tools. What stayed is identity, the refusal, the
unconditional honesty rule and the tone — everything that has to hold on a request where no skill
fires, because a skill is optional. Our instruction went from 294 tokens to 157, and the toolset's own
preamble added 479, so the request got bigger. The saving was never the point; the point is that the
procedure now has one home and support can edit it."*
