# 5.3 — What a real eval suite adds

## One-line answer

The lab scores two surfaces with two free metrics and can be shown to fail, and the distance between
that and a suite a team would rely on is not vague hardening — it is nine specific things, each cheap
today and expensive later, and two of them are deliberately parked rather than forgotten.

## The story

What an experienced tailor measures.

A first-timer takes three numbers: chest, waist, length. Those three are genuinely most of it, and a
shirt cut from them will fit a lot of people reasonably well.

The tailor who has been doing it for thirty years takes those three and then goes on. Shoulder to
shoulder across the back, because two people with the same chest can have very different shoulders.
Sleeve from the shoulder point, not from the neck. The neck itself, with a finger inside the tape.
Whether one shoulder sits lower than the other, which is true of most people and which nobody ever
mentions. How the customer stands when they are not thinking about it.

None of the extra measurements is clever, and none of them was arrived at by reasoning. Each one is
there because a shirt came back once.

## The idea in plain language

The lab in this day does four things correctly. It records cases as data in ADK's own format, scores
two of the three surfaces from part
[1.2](../01-a-test-with-a-score/1.2-three-surfaces-you-can-assert-on.md), costs nothing to run at any
suite size, and has been demonstrated to fail — three ways in part
[3.1](../03-two-metrics-that-cost-nothing/3.1-tool-trajectory-avg-score.md), part
[3.3](../03-two-metrics-that-cost-nothing/3.3-response-match-and-the-word-not.md) and part
[2.3](../02-the-evalset-file/2.3-the-case-that-cannot-fail.md).

Here is the rest of the tailor's tape.

**1. Assert on the state.**
Part [1.2](../01-a-test-with-a-score/1.2-three-surfaces-you-can-assert-on.md) named three surfaces and
this day scores two. `EvalCase` has `session_input` and `final_session_state` and neither is used.
Day 65's gate found a run that closes a ticket twice, exits `0` and logs normally — a failure no
assertion on the answer or the path can see. This is first because it is the surface where the
expensive bugs live.

**2. Property assertions on the trajectory, not just exact match.**
Part [3.2](../03-two-metrics-that-cost-nothing/3.2-exact-match-right-default-wrong-metric.md) measured
a one-sided tool rename producing `4 of 6` — the identical headline the two real bugs produced. Three
or four property metrics (call-count bound, write-tool deny-list, an ordering pair) wired through
`EvalConfig.custom_metrics`, with exact match kept only for the two cases whose subject is the path.

**3. A judged metric for the answers.**
Part [3.3](../03-two-metrics-that-cost-nothing/3.3-response-match-and-the-word-not.md) is not a
tuning problem: 0 of 6 correct rewordings pass and 5 of 6 negations do, and no threshold separates
them. ROUGE is a wording-drift detector and the correctness question needs
`final_response_match_v2`. It costs requests — part
[4.1](../04-what-it-costs-to-run/4.1-the-metrics-that-spend-nothing.md) priced it at 60 per metric for
six cases — which is Day 81's subject.

**4. Per-case results on disk, with the model and the date.**
`AgentEvaluator.evaluate` takes an `output_file`. Without it the scores go to stdout and a run's
history is a series of pass counts. With it, a case drifting from 0.95 to 0.84 over three months is
visible before it crosses a threshold — and the row has to carry the judge model, or March's number
cannot be compared with September's.

**5. The stub check in CI, in front of the eval run.**
`always_green.py` exits `1` when any case would be passed by an agent that does nothing. One line in
the build script, placed before the suite, so *can this fail?* is answered before *did it?*. Part
[5.2](5.2-the-suite-that-only-goes-green.md) enumerates the four routes it guards.

**6. Split the evalset by capability.**
One `.evalset.json` per behaviour group rather than one file with everything. The `adk eval` CLI takes
several paths and supports `file.json:eval_1,eval_2`, so the split costs nothing at run time and buys
reviewable diffs and survivable merges. Part
[2.1](../02-the-evalset-file/2.1-the-evalset-is-a-file.md) does the arithmetic: 5,971 bytes for six
cases is about a megabyte at a thousand.

**7. Redaction before a recorded case is committed.**
The moment cases come from real sessions — part
[2.2](../02-the-evalset-file/2.2-recording-a-case-or-writing-one.md) — the evalset is a file of
customer text, in a repository Phase 14 makes public. Day 69's boundaries apply and the format
enforces nothing. It has to happen **before** the commit, because afterwards it is a history rewrite.

**8. 🅿️ Parked — the Vertex AI evaluation service.**
`google-adk[eval]` brings `google-cloud-aiplatform[evaluation]`, and three of ADK's evaluation modules
import it at load time — measured in part
[4.3](../04-what-it-costs-to-run/4.3-the-eval-extra-pulls-the-cloud-in.md). It is a real, managed
evaluation service with metrics this curriculum will never run, and it needs a project and a billing
account. Know the shape, know what it buys, do not build it here.

**9. 🅿️ Parked — human labelling.**
Every judged metric is a cheap approximation of a person reading the answer, and the only way to know
how good the approximation is, is to have some answers a person actually judged. A few dozen labelled
examples is what turns "the judge agrees with us" from an assumption into a number. It is parked
because it costs somebody's afternoon rather than somebody's money, and because Day 81 is where the
question becomes urgent.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because the next four days consume this list. Day 80 is item 2 and part of item 1 — rubric-based
trajectory evaluation, with *"escalated before any external write"* as a rubric line, which is an
assertion about state as much as about path. Day 81 is item 3 and item 9. Day 82 is items 4, 5 and 6.
Day 83's gate asks whether the whole thing is green, and part
[5.2](5.2-the-suite-that-only-goes-green.md) is why that question needs item 5 answered first.

Item 7 is the one with no day attached, and it is the one with a deadline: Phase 14 makes this
repository public.

## The mechanism

The list above is the mechanism. What this part can add is what each item costs, so it can be argued
with rather than admired:

| # | Item | Where it lands | Roughly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | assert on `final_session_state` | `sutra/evals.py` + one case | half a day |
| 2 | property metrics for the trajectory | `sutra/evals.py` + `custom_metrics` | half a day |
| 3 | `final_response_match_v2` with a pinned judge | `test_config.json` | an hour, plus a measured allowance |
| 4 | per-case results on disk with model and date | `output_file` + a small reader | a couple of hours |
| 5 | the stub check in CI | one line in the build script | ten minutes |
| 6 | split the evalset by capability | file moves + a config per folder | an hour |
| 7 | redaction before commit | a pre-commit check over `*.evalset.json` | half a day |
| 8 | 🅿️ Vertex AI evaluation service | not built | needs a billing account |
| 9 | 🅿️ human labelling | not built here | somebody's afternoon, repeatedly |

**Read the right-hand column against the left.** Item 5 is ten minutes and it is the one that keeps
every other item honest. Item 3 is an hour of config and cannot be done at all until somebody measures
Flash-Lite's allowance — Day 78 part 5.2's item 9, still open, and the dependency between the two lists
is worth noticing: **an eval suite's most important upgrade is blocked on a quota measurement nobody
has taken.**

The two parked items are parked for different reasons, and the difference matters. Item 8 costs money,
which Addendum 02 settles permanently. Item 9 costs attention, which is a decision about this week — a
real project labels fifty examples in its first month, and Day 81 is where not having them starts to
hurt.

## When it breaks

The list has its own failure mode, and it is the same one every such list has: it reads as
nice-to-haves, so none of it is done, because no single item is ever the most urgent thing.

What that looks like is a suite that works for a year and then fails in a way that touches four items
at once. A sequence, every step of which has been measured in this day:

```text
1. a case is recorded from a session rather than written        (item 7, part 2.2)
2. its reference is a copy of what the desk said, so it passes
3. a threshold is lowered when a reworded answer fails          (part 3.3, part 3.4)
4. at that threshold the negated answers pass too               (5 of 6, part 3.3)
5. nothing asserts on state, so a double close is invisible     (item 1, day 65)
6. the nightly reports: 6 of 6 cases at or above 0.35
```

Six steps, no error anywhere, and the last line is reassuring. That is why the list is a list rather
than a paragraph: it is not one gap, it is four composing.

## In production

**What a professional does instead of building all nine.** Picks two and does them properly. Almost
every real eval suite is item 5 plus one of items 1, 2 or 3, and the choice between those three is
made by asking which surface the team's last three incidents were on. A suite that does one thing and
has been seen to fail is worth more than a suite that does nine things and has not.

**What degrades at scale.** Every item on this list is cheap at six cases and painful at four hundred.
Item 6 in particular flips: at six cases nobody needs a split, and by the time anybody does, the file
has a year of history and splitting it loses the blame. Item 4 flips the other way — per-case results
are pointless at six cases and are the only usable view at four hundred.

**The failure that only shows with real traffic.** Everything in this day was measured against a
deterministic desk. A real agent is non-deterministic, so scores move between runs, borderline cases
flip, and `num_runs=2` damps that rather than removing it. The first week of running this suite against
a real model will produce failures that are neither bugs nor regressions, and a team that has not been
told to expect them will spend that week debugging the agent.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"Do item 5 this week — ten minutes, and it is what
stops the other eight from silently not mattering. Then item 1, because our last two incidents were
both state and neither would have been caught by anything we have. Item 3 goes behind the quota
measurement; put it on the same ticket, because right now two different lists are blocked on the same
missing number."*

**The interview question:** *"What is missing from an eval suite that scores answers and tool calls?"*
The answer that shows experience names the third surface first — state, side effects, what the run left
behind — and then adds the meta-item: a check that the suite can still fail. A candidate who lists more
metrics has not yet had a suite go quietly green on them.
