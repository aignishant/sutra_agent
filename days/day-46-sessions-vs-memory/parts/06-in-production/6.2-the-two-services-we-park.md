# 6.2 — 🅿️ The two services we park

## One-line answer

`VertexAiMemoryBankService` and `VertexAiRagMemoryService` are the two implementations a real
deployment would actually use, they need a Google Cloud billing account, and on this machine they
refuse at construction with `ImportError: The 'google-cloud-aiplatform' package is required` — so
Sutra reads their constructor signatures, writes down what each one would buy, and builds neither.

## The story

There is a shop on the main road that sells washing machines, and one of them is on a plinth by the
window with a card beside it.

The card lists everything: the drum size, the spin speed, the programmes, the warranty, the price. You
can open the door and turn the dial and read every word on it, and the salesman will happily talk you
through the difference between this one and the two beside it. What you cannot do is take it home.
That one is the display model; it is not for sale, and the ones for sale are in the warehouse, and
today you are buying the cheaper machine anyway because that is what the money runs to.

You would still be worse off if you had walked past the window without reading the card. Next year,
when there is more money, you will know what you are choosing between, and — more usefully — you now
know what the machine you *did* buy does not do, which is the kind of thing you otherwise find out
during a power cut with a drum full of wet clothes.

Reading the card is free. Buying is not. They are different decisions and people confuse them.

## The idea in plain language

🅿️ **Parked** in this curriculum means: understood, documented, interview-ready, and deliberately not
built. It is not "skipped". A parked topic has a reason attached and a written answer to *"what would
you do instead, and what would it cost you"*.

`google.adk.memory` exports three implementations of the one interface
([2.2](../02-the-interface/2.2-the-silence-that-lets-you-swap.md)):

| Implementation | What it stores | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `InMemoryMemoryService` | raw events, in a dict, in this process | nothing |
| `VertexAiMemoryBankService` | model-extracted "memories" derived from a conversation | a Google Cloud project and an Agent Engine |
| `VertexAiRagMemoryService` | raw transcripts, retrieved by vector similarity | a Google Cloud project and a RAG corpus |

The two Vertex rows are parked under
[Addendum 02](../../../../docs/02_ADDENDUM_ZERO_BUDGET_MODELS.md), whose rule is not a preference: no
day in this curriculum may require a billing account. Zero budget is a feature (Principle 15), and
the reason it is a feature is that a curriculum which needs a card on file is a curriculum most
people cannot finish.

But the difference between the two parked services is genuinely worth carrying, because it is the
same distinction this whole day has been drawing, one level up.

**Memory Bank stores conclusions.** At the end of a conversation you hand it the session, and a model
reads that conversation and writes down what it thinks is worth keeping — *"this customer's portal
sessions drop after SSO changes"* — and that derived sentence is what gets retrieved later. The raw
exchange is not what comes back.

**RAG Memory stores the transcript.** It keeps what was actually said, indexes it by vector
similarity, and gives you back the passages nearest to your query. Nothing is interpreted. What comes
back is what somebody wrote.

That is the same line as *the transcript is not a database*
([1.1](../01-the-line/1.1-the-conversation-that-ends.md)), drawn between two products rather than
between two services, and choosing between them is a product decision: do you want the desk to
remember what was said, or what was concluded? One of those can be wrong in ways nobody can trace.

## Why Sutra needs it

Three reasons, and the third is the one that pays off soonest.

**Because parking is a decision and decisions get written down.** An engineer who says *"we used the
in-memory one"* has described a default. An engineer who says *"we used the in-memory one, the two
managed options both need a billing account, here is what each would have given us and here is what
we lose without them"* has described a choice. Day 45's audit and Day 48's policy both assume Sutra
can produce the second kind of sentence.

**Because the interface is what makes the park cheap.**
[2.1](../02-the-interface/2.1-two-promises-and-two-refusals.md) established that every one of these
is a `BaseMemoryService` with two required methods. Everything Sutra wrote today — `build_memory_service`,
the cap wrapper, the two tools, the runner wiring — is written against that interface, so adopting a
managed service later is a change to one function. Parking costs nothing precisely because the
abstraction was taken seriously first. That is the payoff for section 2, arriving four sections later.

**Because half of what these services do is coming anyway, for free.** Day 49 builds a local
embedding index over the ticket archive: retrieval by vector similarity, on this machine, at zero
cost. That is the interesting half of `VertexAiRagMemoryService`, and building it by hand is
Principle 4 — build the mechanism, then look at the managed version, so the managed version is a
convenience and never a mystery.

## The mechanism

Read the signatures off the installed package, then try to build each one and print what happens.

```python
# days/day-46-sessions-vs-memory/lab/parked.py
BUILDS = [
    ("InMemoryMemoryService", lambda: InMemoryMemoryService()),
    ("VertexAiMemoryBankService", lambda: VertexAiMemoryBankService(agent_engine_id="456")),
    ("VertexAiRagMemoryService", lambda: VertexAiRagMemoryService(rag_corpus="tickets")),
]
```

**Line by line:**

- Each entry pairs a name with a **`lambda`** rather than with a constructed object, because building
  the object is exactly what is expected to fail. A list of already-constructed services would raise
  while the list itself was being defined, before any of the reporting code ran.
- `agent_engine_id="456"` and `rag_corpus="tickets"` are obvious nonsense values, deliberately. The
  question this script asks is *"how far do I get before something stops me"*, and the answer turns
  out to be **before the arguments are looked at**, which is a more useful finding than a validation
  error would have been.
- `InMemoryMemoryService` is in the list even though everybody knows it works. A comparison needs its
  control: the transcript below is only meaningful because one line in it says `built`.

```python
# days/day-46-sessions-vs-memory/lab/parked.py (continued)
    for name, cls in (
        ("InMemoryMemoryService", InMemoryMemoryService),
        ("VertexAiMemoryBankService", VertexAiMemoryBankService),
        ("VertexAiRagMemoryService", VertexAiRagMemoryService),
    ):
        params = list(inspect.signature(cls.__init__).parameters)[1:]
        print(f"    {name:26} ({', '.join(params) or 'no arguments'})")

    for name, build in BUILDS:
        try:
            build()
            print(f"    {name:26} built")
        except Exception as err:
            print(f"    {name:26} {type(err).__name__}: {err}")
```

**Line by line:**

- `inspect.signature(cls.__init__)` reads the constructor's real parameter list off the installed
  package. This is the whole technique of this part: **the signature is available without a billing
  account.** You cannot run these services, and you can read exactly what they ask for, which is most
  of what you need in order to talk about them honestly.
- `list(...)[1:]` drops `self`. Without it every line starts with a parameter nobody passes.
- Importing the classes at the top of the file works even though constructing them does not, because
  `google.adk.memory.__init__` resolves them **lazily** through a module-level `__getattr__`. The
  import binds a name; the dependency is only reached when the class is used. That is why this script
  can be a single file with three imports at the top.
- `except Exception as err` is deliberately broad **here and nowhere else**. This is a probe whose
  entire output is *which exception came back*, so narrowing it to a guessed type would be guessing
  at the answer the script exists to find. Principle 10 forbids swallowing an error; this does the
  opposite — it prints the type and the message and moves on to the next probe.
- `type(err).__name__` prints the class rather than just the message, because *"an ImportError"* and
  *"a PermissionDenied"* would mean completely different things here: one says the package is absent,
  the other would have said the package is present and the credentials are not.
- No model, no network, no key. **Zero model calls.**

```text
constructor parameters, read off the installed google-adk:

    InMemoryMemoryService      (no arguments)
    VertexAiMemoryBankService  (project, location, agent_engine_id, express_mode_api_key, credentials)

what happens when you try to build each one on this machine:

    InMemoryMemoryService      built
    VertexAiMemoryBankService  ImportError: The 'google-cloud-aiplatform' package is required to use this feature. Please install it by running: pip install google-adk[gcp]
    VertexAiRagMemoryService   ImportError: The 'google-cloud-aiplatform' package is required to use this feature. Please install it by running: pip install google-adk[gcp]
```

Three things in those signatures are worth reading properly.

**`InMemoryMemoryService` takes no arguments at all.** Nothing to configure, nothing to tune, no cap,
no retriever choice. Every knob this day wished for is absent, which is why
[4.1](../04-the-choice/4.1-pricing-both-in-tokens.md) had to write a wrapper to get one.

**`VertexAiRagMemoryService` takes `similarity_top_k` and `vector_distance_threshold`.** Those are the
two things [5.4](../05-failure-lab/5.4-the-store-that-outgrew-its-value.md) proved you need and the
in-memory service cannot give you: a cap, and a cut-off on a **distance** — which only exists because
a vector retriever produces a score and a keyword matcher does not
([5.1](../05-failure-lab/5.1-the-past-that-matched-on-nothing.md)). The signature is an argument for
Day 49 written by somebody else.

**`VertexAiMemoryBankService` takes an `agent_engine_id` and no retrieval parameters at all.** No cap,
no threshold. That follows from what it stores: it returns derived memories rather than passages, and
there are far fewer of them, because a model decided what was worth writing down. That is Day 48's
subject arriving as a product.

And the failure is an `ImportError`, not a permission error — the dependency is not installed, so
neither service gets as far as noticing that there are no credentials either. Two walls, and this
machine only reaches the first.

Verified on 2026-09-05 against the installed source: `.venv/Lib/site-packages/google/adk/memory/__init__.py`
declares `__all__ = ['BaseMemoryService', 'InMemoryMemoryService', 'VertexAiMemoryBankService',
'VertexAiRagMemoryService']` and resolves the last three through a lazy `__getattr__`. Confirmed
against `https://adk.dev/sessions/memory/`, read the same day, which describes Memory Bank as *"a
fully managed Google Cloud service that provides sophisticated, persistent memory capabilities for
conversational agents"* that *"intelligently processes and stores the information as 'memories'"*,
and `VertexAiRagMemoryService` as a service that *"stores conversations in Knowledge Engine and
retrieves them by vector similarity"* — *"use it when you already have RAG infrastructure or want raw
transcript retrieval rather than the LLM-extracted memories produced by Memory Bank."* Note that
`https://adk.dev/docs/sessions/memory/` returns **HTTP 404**; the live path has no `/docs` segment.

## When it breaks

**Parking breaks when the reason is not written down.** A year later the sentence in somebody's head
is *"we didn't use Memory Bank"*, and the reason — no billing account, in a curriculum that
deliberately has none — is gone, so the decision gets re-litigated from scratch by somebody with less
information. The containment is a row in a document with a date on it, which is why this part exists
at all rather than a comment in a file.

**It breaks harder when the park was really an avoidance.** The test is whether you can say what you
gave up. Sutra gave up: durable memory that survives a restart
([5.2](../05-failure-lab/5.2-the-store-that-was-never-a-store.md)), retrieval that ranks
([5.1](../05-failure-lab/5.1-the-past-that-matched-on-nothing.md)), a cap and a distance threshold as
first-class parameters, and — with Memory Bank — the extraction step that would have stopped raw
ticket text being stored at all ([5.3](../05-failure-lab/5.3-another-customers-memory.md)). If you
cannot list what you lost, you have not parked anything; you have simply not done it.

**And the `ImportError` above is not the real wall.** Install `google-adk[gcp]` and the next thing
that happens is credentials, then a project, then an Agent Engine that must be created, then a bill.
Reading the error as *"one pip install away"* is the mistake this transcript is here to prevent —
which is exactly why the script prints the exception **type** and not just the message.

One more break worth naming, because it is the one that costs money by accident: the two Vertex
services are **not** the same thing as calling the Gemini API with a key. Addendum 02's model rule
routes Sutra through `GOOGLE_API_KEY` with `GOOGLE_GENAI_USE_VERTEXAI=FALSE`, and every day in this
curriculum runs that way. A memory service whose name starts with `VertexAi` is a different product
on a different billing path, and constructing one because "we already have a Google key" is how a
zero-budget project acquires an invoice.

## In production

**What a professional writes instead of the teaching version.** They would run one of the two parked
services, and the choice between them would be written down with its reason. The decision is not
about features; it is about **who is allowed to interpret the conversation**. Memory Bank puts a
model between what was said and what is stored — cheaper at retrieval, smaller at rest, and it will
occasionally record a conclusion nobody drew, which no downstream check can catch because the
original is not kept. RAG Memory keeps what was said and so keeps every problem
[5.3](../05-failure-lab/5.3-another-customers-memory.md) raised about storing raw customer text, and
gives you something you can audit.

For a support desk under any kind of data-handling obligation, the auditable one usually wins, and the
extraction step gets built in-house so that the rule for what may be stored is one you wrote and can
show somebody.

**What degrades at scale or under concurrency.** Nothing here degrades — this is a signature probe —
but the thing being parked changes character with scale, and it is worth being precise about which
half. `InMemoryMemoryService` is not a small version of a managed service; it is a different
architecture. It has no index (linear scan under a lock), no persistence, no ranking and no cap. A
managed service fixes the first three of those and **not** the fourth, so the dilution measured in
[5.4](../05-failure-lab/5.4-the-store-that-outgrew-its-value.md) survives the upgrade unless you also
pass `similarity_top_k`. "We'll move to the managed one later" is a plan that fixes fewer things than
people expect.

**The failure mode that only appears with real traffic.** The one nobody plans for: the managed
service's own quotas and its own latency. A search that was a dictionary walk becomes a network call
on the critical path of every turn, with everything Day 44's
[client hardening](../../../day-44-client-hardening/LESSON.md) taught applying to it — a deadline, a
retry rule, and a decision about what the desk says when memory is unreachable. Sutra's rule 4 in
[4.2](../04-the-choice/4.2-sutras-memory-policy.md) already covers the answer: report the miss,
never interpret it as *"there is no past case"*.

**The review comment a senior engineer leaves:** *"`build_memory_service()` hard-codes the in-memory
implementation. Fine for now — but put the reason in a comment with a date, and shape the function so
the choice comes from configuration, because the day we get a project the change should be one line
and not a search through the codebase. And when you do write that line: pass `similarity_top_k`.
Moving to a managed store does not cap anything by itself."*

**The interview question:** *"Would you use a managed memory service?"* An honest answer: *"For
anything real, yes, and the choice is between two of them that store different things. Memory Bank
runs a model over the finished conversation and stores what it concluded, so retrieval is small and
cheap and you have lost the original. The RAG-backed one stores the transcript and retrieves passages
by vector similarity, so you can audit what it returned and you are storing raw customer text with
everything that implies. For a support desk I would take the auditable one and build the extraction
step myself, so the rule about what may be kept is one I can show a reviewer. On this project I built
neither, on purpose — both need a Google Cloud billing account and the project is deliberately
zero-budget — so I read their constructor signatures off the installed package instead, which is free
and tells you a surprising amount: the RAG service takes `similarity_top_k` and a vector distance
threshold, and the in-memory one takes no arguments at all, which is exactly the gap I had to fill
with a wrapper. The thing I would push back on is the assumption that moving to a managed service
fixes retrieval quality. It fixes persistence, indexing and ranking. It does not cap the result set
unless you ask it to, and an uncapped query against a large managed store is the same dilution
problem at a much bigger scale."*
