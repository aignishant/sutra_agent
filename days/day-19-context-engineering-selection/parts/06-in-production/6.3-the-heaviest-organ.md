# 6.3 — The heaviest organ

## One-line answer

Five organs are fixed by decisions you can make today; the sixth grows on its own and cannot be fixed
by choosing better — which is why tomorrow is a whole day about **replacing what happened with a
shorter true account of it**.

## The story

The minutes of the meeting.

Nobody keeps the recording. An hour and a half of people talking, and what survives is a page: who was
there, what was decided, who is doing what by when, and the two things that were explicitly left open.

The page is not the meeting. It is missing the argument about the deadline, the joke that made everyone
relax, and the four minutes where somebody explained the same thing twice. And it is what everybody
reads next time, because it contains the part that has consequences.

Somebody had to write it. That is the cost, and it is why minutes exist for meetings that matter and
not for every conversation in the building.

## The idea in plain language

This day has been a selection exercise. Every rule in section 3 is of the form *choose less*, and four
of the organs respond to it directly: shorten the instruction, trim the menu, template facts rather
than blobs, distil the document.

The history does not respond to choosing, because you did not choose it. It is the record of what
happened, it grows by roughly 244 characters a turn in the simplest possible conversation
([1.3](../01-the-binder/1.3-the-organ-that-grows-by-itself.md)), and by turn ten it is the largest
organ in the request and the fastest-growing one.

There are exactly three things you can do with it:

- **Send all of it.** The default. Correct, complete, and quadratic in total cost.
- **Truncate it.** Keep the last N turns. Cheap, and it loses facts silently — the very failure
  [5.1](../05-failure-lab/5.1-in-state-and-not-in-the-window.md) is about, self-inflicted.
- **Compact it.** Replace the old turns with a short summary that keeps what has consequences. This
  costs a model call to produce and it is the only option that shrinks the organ without losing what
  the conversation established.

That third one is tomorrow: **Day 20 — context engineering II: compaction and summarisation**, closing
AG-10 and ADK-22, and ADK has machinery for it, which is why it is a day of its own rather than a
paragraph here.

Two things today has already put in place for it, and it is worth seeing that they were not accidents.

**Facts do not live in the transcript any more.** Days 17 and 18 moved them: decisions to state, files
to artifacts. That is what makes compaction *safe* — summarising the conversation cannot lose the
severity, because the severity is a state key, not a sentence.

**And the trigger is a budget.** [6.2](6.2-a-budget-per-organ.md)'s `history: 20_000` is not a limit to
refuse at; it is the line where compaction should run. Sutra now has a measurement, a threshold and a
place to put the summary before it has the summariser.

## Why Sutra needs it

Because Sutra's conversations are exactly the ones where truncation would be wrong.

A support thread's early turns contain the ticket, the symptom, the version and what has already been
tried. Truncating to the last three turns throws that away and produces an agent that asks a question
the engineer answered ten minutes ago. Compaction keeps it — *"the engineer reported logins failing
since 09:10; tried a browser restart; tenant northwind-prod"* — in a fraction of the characters, and in
a strong position at the front of the window
([3.3](../03-selection/3.3-position-is-not-presence.md)).

## The mechanism

There is no new code in this part — the mechanism is tomorrow. What today can do is show the arithmetic
that decides *when* it should run, using measurements this day already produced:

```python
# days/day-19-context-engineering-selection/lab/when_to_compact.py
"""When the history organ crosses its budget. Zero model calls."""

from sutra.context import BUDGET

PER_TURN = 244  # measured in 1.3: characters added to the request by one plain turn
SUMMARY = 400  # a plausible compacted summary of a long stretch of conversation


def crossing_turn(per_turn: int, limit: int) -> int:
    """The turn at which the history organ first exceeds its budget."""
    turn = 1
    while turn * per_turn <= limit:
        turn += 1
    return turn


limit = BUDGET["history"]
crosses = crossing_turn(PER_TURN, limit)

print(f"history budget         : {limit:,} characters")
print(f"a plain turn adds      : {PER_TURN} characters")
print(f"budget is crossed at   : turn {crosses}")
print()
total_at_crossing = sum(turn * PER_TURN for turn in range(1, crosses + 1))
after_compaction = SUMMARY + PER_TURN
print(f"cumulative cost by then: {total_at_crossing:,} characters")
print(f"one compacted request  : {after_compaction:,} characters")
print(f"the compaction pays for itself if the conversation continues past turn {crosses + 1}")
```

**Line by line:**

- `PER_TURN = 244` is **measured**, not assumed: it is the increment from
  [1.3](../01-the-binder/1.3-the-organ-that-grows-by-itself.md)'s table, on the simplest possible
  conversation. A real turn with a tool call is larger, so this is the optimistic case.
- `SUMMARY = 400` is a **guess**, and labelled as one. Tomorrow replaces it with a measurement.
- `crossing_turn(...)` is a loop rather than a division, because the interesting output is the turn
  number and the arithmetic is meant to be readable rather than clever.
- `sum(turn * PER_TURN ...)` — the cumulative cost, which is the quadratic quantity from
  [1.3](../01-the-binder/1.3-the-organ-that-grows-by-itself.md), and the number that makes the case.
- The last line states the break-even in the honest form: compaction costs a model call, so it pays off
  only if the conversation keeps going. On a two-turn conversation it is pure loss.

## When it breaks

**You truncate instead of compacting, and the agent forgets.**

No error. Keeping the last three turns is a two-line change and it works beautifully in testing, where
conversations are short and everything relevant is recent. In use it produces the agent that asks for
the ticket number again — and because nothing failed, the diagnosis is "the model is bad at long
conversations" rather than "we deleted the answer".

**You compact too early.** Compaction is a model call: on a free tier that is one of twenty
([Addendum 02](../../../../docs/02_ADDENDUM_ZERO_BUDGET_MODELS.md)). Running it on every turn spends
more than it saves and adds latency to conversations that never needed it. The threshold exists for
that reason.

**You compact and lose something the summary did not keep.** Tomorrow's real risk, and today's
groundwork is the mitigation: facts live in state and files live in artifacts, so a summary that drops
a detail drops *narrative*, not data. The failure mode of a system that never moved its facts out of
the transcript is much worse.

## In production

**Move the facts out first, then shorten the story.**

That ordering is the whole reason Days 17 and 18 came before this one. A system that keeps its
decisions in prose cannot compact safely, so it either pays the quadratic cost for ever or truncates
and forgets. Sutra can do neither, because the severity is a state key and the note is an artifact.

**What changes at scale** is that compaction becomes a policy with numbers: when it runs, what it keeps,
how much it costs, and how often the summary turns out to have dropped something that mattered. That
last one is measurable only by looking, which is why compaction and evals arrive in the same phase of a
real project.

**What fails only under real traffic** is the conversation that resumes. A thread picked up the next
morning has a history from yesterday, and whether that history is the raw transcript or last night's
summary is a decision somebody has to make — and it interacts with Day 47's persistence, because a
summary is a thing that has to survive a restart too.

**The review comment**: *"this trims the history to the last five turns. What was in turn one, and how
would we know it is gone?"*

**The interview question**: *"how do you stop an agent's context from growing without bound?"* The
answer that shows the ordering: *"first move the facts out of the transcript into state, so the
transcript is only narrative. Then compact the narrative when it crosses a measured threshold. Trimming
without doing the first part is just forgetting."*
