## 1 · 🎬 Where we are — the story so far

Yesterday you weighed binders and learned the craft's first rule: only what earns a place.
You applied it to five of the six organs — instruction, tools, state facts, documents, the
question. But the sixth organ laughed at you. **History grows by itself.** Every turn

You cannot delete history — Principle 12 says everything is a trace, and Day 17 built state
on top of the event log precisely because it's complete. But *what the model sees* and
*what the record keeps* were never the same thing. The session history is the archive; the
context window is the briefing. Today we finally split them: old events get **summarized**
into minutes, recent events stay **verbatim**, and the archive underneath stays whole.
ADK ships this as **context compaction**, configured in one place. That's today.

## 3 · 📚 Concepts

### AG-10 — Summarization: the minute-taker (and the price of minutes)

**The story.** A standing meeting runs for months. Nobody re-reads the full transcript
before each session — someone keeps **minutes**: decisions, open items, who owns what. The
last meeting or two you remember verbatim; everything older lives as minutes; and the full
transcript still exists in the archive if a dispute needs it.

That's compaction, organ by organ:

| Meeting | Our system | Technical name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full transcript in the archive | Session event log — never destroyed | the session history (Day 17's audit trail) |
| The minutes | Model-written summary of older events | the **compaction summary** |
| "Last meeting, verbatim, still fresh" | Recent events kept as-is | the **overlap / retention window** |
| The minute-taker | A model doing the summarizing | the **summarizer** (an explicit model choice!) |

💡 **Mental model: the archive keeps everything; the briefing keeps minutes plus the last
page.** Compaction changes what the *model sees*, never what the *system remembers*.

Now the honest half — minutes have costs, and naming them is the concept:

1. **Summaries are lossy on purpose.** The minute-taker decides what mattered. A detail
   that looked irrelevant at turn 3 ("user mentioned they're on Safari") may be the key at
   turn 30 — and it's now one casualty of a sentence like "user described login issues."
   Mitigations: keep *facts* in **state** (Day 17 — state is never compacted away; that's
   why structured facts don't live in prose!), keep documents in **artifacts** (Day 18),
   and size the verbatim window so anything "in play" stays verbatim.
2. **Summarization is itself a model call.** You spend tokens to save tokens. The trade
   only wins when the conversation is long enough — and when the minute-taker is *cheap*.
   Hence the lite lane: a small fast model summarizes; the big model reasons. (Zero-budget
   rule made architecture: route each job to the cheapest model that does it well.)
3. **Compaction has timing hazards.** Summarize in the middle of a multi-step exchange —
   half a question-and-answer, a tool call whose result hasn't landed — and the model's next
   view of the world is *incoherent*, not just lossy. §5 shows the sharpest real version.

### ADK-22 — Context ops in ADK: one config, on the App

ADK puts the whole mechanism behind one configuration object, attached not to the agent but
to the **`App`** — the wrapper that owns app-wide concerns (the same object Phase 8's graph
work will lean on). The verified idiom:

```python
from google.adk.apps.app import App, EventsCompactionConfig
from google.adk.apps.llm_event_summarizer import LlmEventSummarizer
from google.adk.models import Gemini

app = App(
    name="sutra",
    root_agent=my_agent,
    events_compaction_config=EventsCompactionConfig(
        compaction_interval=3,      # after every 3 completed invocations…
        overlap_size=1,             # …compact, keeping the last 1 verbatim
        summarizer=LlmEventSummarizer(          # the minute-taker,
            llm=Gemini(model="gemini-3.5-flash-lite")   # chosen EXPLICITLY
        ),
    ),
)
```

Read the three fields as policy, not plumbing:

- **`compaction_interval`** — *how often the minute-taker works.* Small = light windows,
  more summarizer calls, more loss risk. Large = the opposite. It counts **invocations**
  (doorbell-presses, Day 17's word), not raw events.
- **`overlap_size`** — *how much recent past stays verbatim.* Your safety margin against
  the timing hazard: anything still "in play" should fit inside it.
- **`summarizer`** — *who writes the minutes.* Explicit, because it's quota and quality
  policy. We pin the lite model — never inherit a default (ADK-73 reflex).

**The token-based variant:** for unpredictable loads (a user pastes a huge log), interval
counting misfires — three tiny invocations don't need minutes; one giant one does. The
config's alternative trigger fires on size instead: `token_threshold` (compact when history
exceeds N tokens) with `event_retention_size` (how much stays). Interval = predictable
chats; threshold = safety net. Knowing *which trigger matches which workload* is the ADK-22
skill.

```python
# --- Day 20 additions: compaction ------------------------------------------------
import asyncio                                       # (top of file, with the imports)

from google.adk.agents import Agent
from google.adk.apps.app import App, EventsCompactionConfig
from google.adk.apps.llm_event_summarizer import LlmEventSummarizer
from google.adk.models import Gemini
from google.adk.runners import Runner
from google.adk.sessions import InMemorySessionService
from google.genai import types

chat_agent = Agent(
    name="sutra_chat",
    model="gemini-3.5-flash",                        # the conversation brain (pinned)
    description="Small-talk triage assistant for the compaction demo.",
    instruction=("Answer support questions in ONE short sentence. "
                 "No lists, no elaboration — this demo is about history size."),
)

# The whole day in one object: minutes every 3 invocations, last 1 verbatim,
# minutes written by the CHEAP model (lite lane = summarizer lane).
sutra_app = App(
    name="sutra",
    root_agent=chat_agent,
    events_compaction_config=EventsCompactionConfig(
        compaction_interval=3,
        overlap_size=1,
        summarizer=LlmEventSummarizer(llm=Gemini(model="gemini-3.5-flash-lite")),
    ),
)


def demo_compact() -> None:
    """Four tiny invocations, then read the session's events: find the minutes.

    Usage:  python -m sutra.context compact     (~4 chat + ~1-2 summarizer calls)
    """
    service = InMemorySessionService()
    # TODO(on the day): mirror the compaction page's quickstart for this wiring —
    # this doc assumes Runner accepts the App in place of a bare agent:
    runner = Runner(app=sutra_app, session_service=service)

    asyncio.run(service.create_session(app_name="sutra", user_id="priya",
                                       session_id="long1"))

    questions = [                                    # 4 invocations > interval of 3
        "Ticket 4521: users keep getting logged out. Likely cause?",
        "Is that related to the SameSite cookie change in KB-104?",
        "What severity would you assign it?",
        "Draft a one-line status update for the team.",
    ]
    for q in questions:                              # one doorbell-press per question
        message = types.Content(role="user", parts=[types.Part(text=q)])

        async def run_one(msg: types.Content) -> None:
            """Run a single invocation and print only the final reply."""
            async for event in runner.run_async(user_id="priya",
                                                session_id="long1",
                                                new_message=msg):
                if event.is_final_response() and event.content:
                    print("reply:", event.content.parts[0].text.strip())

        asyncio.run(run_one(message))

    # Now open the archive and LOOK for the minutes:
    session = asyncio.run(service.get_session(app_name="sutra", user_id="priya",
                                              session_id="long1"))
    print(f"\n--- {len(session.events)} events in the session archive ---")
    for i, event in enumerate(session.events):       # walk the full record
        kind = "compaction/summary" if _is_summary(event) else "regular"
        text = ""
        if event.content and event.content.parts and event.content.parts[0].text:
            text = event.content.parts[0].text.strip().replace("\n", " ")[:80]
        print(f"[{i:2}] {event.author:12} {kind:18} {text}")


def _is_summary(event) -> bool:
    """Best-effort detector for the compaction summary event.

    The exact marker is version-specific — inspect your own event list the
    first time and adjust. Candidates seen in the wild: a dedicated author,
    an actions flag, or a distinctive summary payload.
    """
    author = (getattr(event, "author", "") or "").lower()
    return "compact" in author or "summar" in author   # tune after first run
```

## 5 · 💥 Failure lab — the minute-taker interrupts an approval

> 🎬 **The scene:** months from now (Phase 9) Sutra asks humans for approval before writes:
> the agent proposes `close_ticket(4521)`, a pending confirmation goes out, the human will
> answer when they're back from lunch. Meanwhile you, proud of today, have tuned compaction
> aggressive: small interval, minimal overlap — light windows, low bills.
> 😬 **The naive fix:** nothing feels broken. Compaction is "just an optimization."
> 💥 **Why it fails:** the human returns and clicks approve — and the agent has no idea what
> they're approving. The minute-taker ran *while the confirmation was pending* and folded
> the proposal event into a sentence like "assistant offered to close the ticket." The
> pending function call — the precise, structured request the approval must attach to — is
> no longer in the model's view. This is not hypothetical: it's a reported ADK issue
> (compaction removing a pending function-call event breaks the confirmation flow —
> github.com/google/adk-python/issues/4740, seen 2026-08-18). The general law: **anything
> still "in play" must stay verbatim**, and an aggressive interval + tiny overlap violates
> that law on schedule.
> 💡 **The insight:** compaction is not an optimization knob — it's a **policy about what
> the model may forget**, and pending work may never be forgotten. Size `overlap_size`
> to cover your longest in-flight exchange; prefer the token-threshold trigger when single
> invocations can balloon; and keep hard facts in state, where the minute-taker can't
> touch them. (File this scene — Day 64's approval gates will test you on it.)

## 6 · 🎤 Interview corner

1. **"Your agent's conversations run long. What do you do?"** — Split what the system
   remembers from what the model sees. The event log stays complete (audit, replay); the
   model's view gets compacted: older invocations summarized by a cheap model, a verbatim
   tail kept for coherence. In ADK that's one config on the App — interval or
   token-threshold trigger, an overlap window, and an explicitly pinned summarizer. I run
   the summarizer on the lite lane; spending big-model tokens to save big-model tokens is
   losing on purpose.
2. **"What breaks when you add summarization?"** — Three things I plan for: lossiness
   (a detail that mattered later got minuted away — so facts live in state, not prose),
   cost inversion (short chats pay for summaries they didn't need — trigger tuning), and
   the timing hazard: compacting mid-exchange, worst case folding away a pending tool call
   an approval needs to reference — a real reported failure mode, which is why my verbatim
   window is sized to the longest in-flight exchange.
3. **"Interval-based or token-based compaction?"** — Interval for predictable chat-shaped
   traffic: regular turns, regular minutes. Token-threshold as the safety net for
   unpredictable payloads — one pasted log can outweigh thirty turns, and only a size
   trigger sees that. Production wants the safety net regardless; the interesting tuning is
   the retention window, and that's a *correctness* parameter, not a cost one.
