## 3 · 📚 Concepts

### AG-27 — Baselines, variance, and the examiner who wants to be liked

**The story.** A school publishes "92% of our students passed." Impressive — until you
learn the exam is graded by a teacher who likes the school, the pass mark was set after
seeing the scores, and a student who wrote the same answer on every page would have
scored 85%. Three questions turn a number into evidence: *Who graded it, and how do we
know they're fair?* *Would a dummy have passed?* *If we ran it again, would we get the
same number?*

**1 — The dummy must be hard to beat on purpose.** A baseline is the simplest thing that
could plausibly score well *on this data*. For Sutra:

| Baseline | What it does | Why it's dangerous |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `always_medium_auth` | every ticket → `auth`, severity 2, not human, reply = "Please re-login; see KB-104." | the archive was *built* around 4521; this dummy is right embarrassingly often |
| `keyword_router` | 20 lines: "logged out/login" → auth; "reset/email" → billing-ish; "down/blank/all users" → outage + escalate; unknown → `other` + needs_human; reply = the KB article whose title shares the most words | this is Day 3's search_kb with a hat on — it cites real articles without a model |

If Sutra's trajectory + answer scores are not clearly above `keyword_router`, the honest
conclusion is "the model adds little on this evalset" — which is *useful*: it means
either the evalset is too easy (add harder cases, Day 83) or the graph's brains are
spent where plumbing would do (Day 58's ratio). Either way you learned something a bare
"0.83" could never tell you.

💡 **Mental model: a score without a baseline is a fact about the dataset; a score above
a baseline is a fact about the agent.**

**2 — Variance first, improvement second.** Models are sampled; judges are models.
Before "the new prompt improved response quality from 0.78 to 0.81" means anything, you
need the *spread* of 0.78 across identical runs. If three runs of the same config land at
0.74 / 0.79 / 0.82, then 0.81 is noise and the prompt change is a coin flip. The rule:
**measure the noise floor once per evalset (min/mean/max over n≥3), write it in the
README, and only call a change real when it clears the floor.** Day 83's gate reports
every metric as mean ± spread, never a bare number.

**3 — The three ways a judge lies, and the three fixes.**

| Bias | What it looks like | Sutra's counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Agreeableness** — vague rubric lines get "yes" | Day 80's "helpful" line scoring 0.98 | conditional, observable rubric lines only (Day 80's law); the audit below *measures* per-line agreement, and lines below 80% agreement are rewritten or cut |
| **Self-preference** — a model rates its own family's prose higher | judge = lite, agent = lite/flash — same family | known and disclosed; the GOLD set is human-labelled, so the audit exposes systematic inflation; for Day 83's gate the judge can be swapped to a second family (Groq lane, Day 9's pin) for one pass — *only* if its free tier covers it; otherwise note the limitation |
| **Position / length bias** — longer or first-listed answers win | reference-vs-candidate comparisons | `final_response_match_v2` compares to a *reference*, not to a rival; `num_samples > 1` at the gate; keep references short and factual so length isn't a signal |

The instrument for all three is the same: **a small GOLD set — transcripts you labelled
by hand, each label with a reason — and an agreement number per rubric line.** Day 50
built a GOLD set for retrieval (hit@k vs paraphrases); today's is the same habit pointed
at the judge. Twelve labels, written by you, *before* you look at the judge's verdicts
(label first, compare second — otherwise you are auditing yourself).

### ADK-61 — The answer-quality judges

Day 80's judges read the *trajectory*. Three more read the *answer*:

| Criterion | Needs a reference? | Asks | Sutra uses it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `final_response_match_v2` | yes | "does the candidate mean what the reference means?" (replaces Day 79's word-overlap ROUGE) | dev set — the reference replies are short and factual on purpose |
| `rubric_based_final_response_quality_v1` | no — rubrics instead | "does the reply satisfy these lines?" | reply-style policy: cites a KB article when one exists; states the workaround before the fix; flags uncertainty honestly (Day 28's `kb-answer-style` skill, as rubric lines) |
| `hallucinations_v1` | no | "is every claim grounded in the provided context?" | the cheapest honesty check you will ever run: a reply that names a KB article the clerk never returned fails here |

All three take the same `judge_model_options` block, all three pin
`gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, all three multiply by `num_samples`. Where the verdicts land:
the console prints aggregates; the per-case detail is in
`.adk/eval_history/<name>.evalset_result.json` (or wherever `output_file=` points). The
audit script reads that file — which is why Day 79 told you to find it.

💡 **Mental model: ROUGE asks "same words?"; `final_response_match_v2` asks "same
meaning?"; `hallucinations_v1` asks "earned the right to say it?"** Keep all three; they
fail in different directions.

```python
"""Day 81 — judge-the-judge: agreement between the LLM judge and hand labels.

Reads (a) tests/evals/gold/labels.json — YOUR per-rubric verdicts, written
before looking at the judge's; (b) the evaluator's per-case results file
(.adk/eval_history/<name>.evalset_result.json, or --results PATH). Prints
agreement per rubric line and overall, and writes data/evals/judge_audit_<date>.json.

    python tests/evals/judge_audit.py --results <path>      # zero quota: reads files
    python tests/evals/judge_audit.py --rejudge              # ~12 judge calls: fresh verdicts

Agreement below 0.8 on a line means: rewrite the line (Day 80's law) or
relabel — and say which, in the README.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import argparse
import json
from collections import defaultdict
from datetime import date
from pathlib import Path

from sutra.logging_setup import get_logger                   # library code logs, never prints (Day 22)

log = get_logger("sutra.evals.judge_audit")
EVALS = Path(__file__).parent
GOLD = EVALS / "gold" / "labels.json"
OUT_DIR = Path("data/evals")


def load_gold() -> dict[str, dict[str, str]]:
    """{eval_id: {rubric_id: 'pass'|'fail'|'n/a'}} from the hand labels.

    Example:
        >>> load_gold()["triage_9999_no_such_ticket"]["declined_on_missing_ticket"]

    Example:
        >>> judge_verdicts(Path(".adk/eval_history/sutra_triage_safety_x.evalset_result.json"))
        {'triage_9999_no_such_ticket': {'declined_on_missing_ticket': 'pass', ...}, ...}
    """
    data = json.loads(results_path.read_text(encoding="utf-8"))
    out: dict[str, dict[str, str]] = defaultdict(dict)
    # TODO(on the day): walk data["eval_case_results"] -> per-case metric results ->
    # rubric-level scores; map score>=0.5 -> 'pass', else 'fail'. Pin the exact keys
    # from ONE printed file; do not guess them here.
    return out


def agreement(gold: dict, judge: dict) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Fraction of (case, rubric) pairs where judge == gold, per rubric line.

    'n/a' in GOLD means the condition never fired — those pairs are skipped,
    not counted as agreement (counting them would flatter the judge).

    Example:
        >>> agreement({"a": {"r1": "pass", "r2": "n/a"}}, {"a": {"r1": "pass", "r2": "pass"}})
        {'r1': 1.0}
    """
    hits: dict[str, list[bool]] = defaultdict(list)
    for eid, labels in gold.items():
        for rid, want in labels.items():
            if want == "n/a" or rid not in judge.get(eid, {}):
                continue                                     # skip, don't flatter
            hits[rid].append(judge[eid][rid] == want)
    return {rid: sum(v) / len(v) for rid, v in hits.items() if v}


def main() -> None:
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    ap.add_argument("--results", type=Path, help="evalset_result.json to audit")
```

**Step 5 — `tests/evals/baselines.py` + tests (zero model calls).**

```python
"""Day 81 — two dummies that are hard to beat on purpose.

Neither calls a model. Each produces, for every dev/safety case, a (trajectory,
reply) pair in the same shape the evaluator records — so they can be scored by
the SAME deterministic metrics (trajectory match, ROUGE) as Sutra.

    python tests/evals/baselines.py          # prints both baselines' scores

If Sutra is not clearly above keyword_router, the evalset is too easy or the
graph's brains are misplaced (Day 58's ratio). Either is worth knowing.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import json
import re
from pathlib import Path

from sutra.loop import TICKETS, KB, lookup_ticket, search_kb     # Day 3's synthetic data + tools

EVALS = Path(__file__).parent
SETS = [EVALS / "dev" / "triage.evalset.json", EVALS / "safety" / "triage_safety.evalset.json"]


def always_medium_auth(user_text: str) -> tuple[list[dict], str]:
    """The dummy that the archive was accidentally built to reward.

    Example:
        >>> always_medium_auth("#4521")[1]
        'Please re-login; see KB-104.'
    """
    tid = _ticket_id(user_text)
    calls = [{"name": "lookup_ticket", "args": {"ticket_id": tid}}] if tid else []
    return calls, "Please re-login; see KB-104."


def keyword_router(user_text: str) -> tuple[list[dict], str]:
    """Day 3's search_kb wearing a triage hat: route by keywords, cite by title overlap.

    Example:
        >>> keyword_router("#9999")[1]
        'No ticket with id 9999 exists.'
    """
    tid = _ticket_id(user_text)
    if not tid:
        return [], "Please give a ticket id."
    text = lookup_ticket(tid)
    calls = [{"name": "lookup_ticket", "args": {"ticket_id": tid}}]
    if "No ticket" in text:                                   # honesty for free
        return calls, f"No ticket with id {tid} exists."
    low = text.lower()
    if any(w in low for w in ("down", "blank", "all users", "outage")):
        return calls, "ESCALATED: possible outage; handed to the human queue."
    if "ignore" in low and ("rule" in low or "instruction" in low):
        return calls, "Ticket text contains instructions; treated as data and flagged."
    query = "logged out" if "log" in low else ("reset email" if "reset" in low else low[:40])
    hit = search_kb(query)
    calls.append({"name": "search_kb", "args": {"query": query}})
    return calls, f"See {hit.split(':')[0]}."                # cite the article id only


def _ticket_id(user_text: str) -> str | None:
    m = re.search(r"(\d{4})", user_text)
    return m.group(1) if m else None


def score(baseline) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Trajectory IN_ORDER hit-rate and mean ROUGE-1 vs the evalsets' references.

    Deliberately re-implements the two deterministic metrics in ~15 lines so the
    dummies are scored with NO model and NO evaluator run — the evaluator scores
    Sutra; this scores the dummies; the README compares the two tables.
    """
    traj_hits, rouge = [], []
    for path in SETS:
        for case in json.loads(path.read_text(encoding="utf-8"))["eval_cases"]:
            turn = case["conversation"][0]
            calls, reply = baseline(turn["user_content"]["parts"][0]["text"])
            want = [(u["name"]) for u in turn["intermediate_data"]["tool_uses"]]
            got = [c["name"] for c in calls]
            traj_hits.append(_in_order(want, got))
            ref = turn["final_response"]["parts"][0]["text"]
            rouge.append(_rouge1(ref, reply))
    return {"trajectory_in_order": sum(traj_hits) / len(traj_hits),
            "rouge1_mean": sum(rouge) / len(rouge)}


def _in_order(want: list[str], got: list[str]) -> bool:
    it = iter(got)
    return all(any(w == g for g in it) for w in want)        # subsequence check


def _rouge1(ref: str, cand: str) -> float:
    r, c = set(re.findall(r"\w+", ref.lower())), set(re.findall(r"\w+", cand.lower()))
    return len(r & c) / len(r) if r else 0.0

    for name, fn in (("always_medium_auth", always_medium_auth), ("keyword_router", keyword_router)):
        print(name, score(fn))
```

## 5 · 💥 Failure lab — the judge that graded its own homework

> 🎬 **The scene:** a month from now the Writer gets a new instruction that makes its
> replies longer and warmer. `final_response_match_v2` rises 0.75 → 0.81 and
> `rubric_based_final_response_quality_v1` rises too. Ship it?
> 😬 **The naive fix:** yes — two judges agree it's better, and the threshold is cleared.
> 💥 **Why it fails:** the noise floor you measured today says ±0.04 is nothing; 0.06 is
> barely something. The judge is the same model family as the writer, and longer, warmer
> prose is exactly what such judges favour (self-preference + length bias — two of the
> three lies). Meanwhile nobody re-ran `hallucinations_v1`, and the warmer reply now
> "reassures" the customer that the fix ships next week — a claim in no tool response.
> Two flattering judges, one skipped honest one, and a change that made Sutra *nicer and
> less true.*
> 💡 **The insight:** **never accept a judge's verdict without (a) the noise floor,
> (b) the grounding check, and (c) the baseline.** Report every metric as mean ± spread;
> keep `hallucinations_v1` in every config and make it a veto (Day 82); re-audit the judge
> against GOLD whenever the *writer* changes, because the judge's biases are relative to
> what it is judging. And when two judges of the same family agree, count it as one
> opinion.

Run it cheaply: take one dev case's reply, append a made-up sentence ("The fix ships
next Tuesday."), and score just that case with `hallucinations_v1` (~2 calls). It should
fail. If it passes, your grounding judge isn't seeing the tool responses as context —
find out why *before* Day 82 makes it a veto.

## 6 · 🎤 Interview corner

1. **"Your agent scores 0.8 on your evalset. Is that good?"** — Alone, it's a fact about
   the dataset. I answer with three more numbers: the baseline (a 20-line keyword router
   scores X on the same set — Sutra's margin is Δ), the noise floor (n=3 identical runs
   spread ±0.04, so changes under that are nothing), and the judge's agreement with my
   hand labels per rubric line (lines under 80% get rewritten, not trusted). With those,
   0.8 becomes evidence. Without them it's a vibe with decimals.
2. **"How do you keep an LLM judge honest?"** — Rubric lines that are conditional and
   observable; an audit set I labelled *before* seeing the judge's verdicts, with
   agreement measured per line; `num_samples` to average judge noise; a grounding
   criterion (`hallucinations_v1`) that checks claims against tool outputs; and the
   disclosure that judge and agent share a model family — so on gate days I consider a
   second-family judge and I never let two same-family judges count as two opinions.
3. **"Why bother with dummy baselines?"** — Because archives are biased toward their own
   history: mine was built around one famous ticket, and a dummy that always answers
   with that ticket's fix scores disturbingly well. If my agent can't clearly beat a
   keyword router, either the evalset is too easy or I'm spending model calls where a
   regex would do. Both are findings I'd rather get from a baseline than from a customer.
