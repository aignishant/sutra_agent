## 1 · 🎬 Where we are — the story so far

Yesterday ended with a magic trick and a confession. The trick: "sign-in trouble" found
"keeps getting logged out" — geometry beat spelling. The confession: point the same
machinery at a refund question and it returns the nearest garbage with a straight face,
because **top-k always returns k**.

Two questions were left deliberately unexamined, and they are precisely where RAG
systems die in production:

1. **What exactly did we embed?** Yesterday: whole rows — one vector per ticket. Fine
   for five-line tickets. But real documents are long, and a 3,000-word incident report
   squashed into one vector is an *average of all its meanings* — the login paragraph
   diluted by twenty paragraphs of timeline. How you cut text before embedding —
   **chunking** — quietly decides what can ever be found.
2. **How many neighbors should we take?** `TOP_K = 3` was a guess with a constant's
   confidence. Too few: the right answer sits at rank 4, unfound. Too many: the window
   fills with near-misses that Day 19 taught us cost money and attention.

And behind both hides the bigger question a senior engineer asks *first*: **should this
be retrieval at all?** Today is tuning day *and* honesty day.

## 3 · 📚 Concepts

### AG-14a — Chunking: the resolution of memory

**The photograph story.** Imagine photographing a page of a book to find one sentence
later. One photo of the *whole page* from across the room: every sentence is in it, none
is readable. A photo of *each word*: perfectly sharp, and utterly useless — no word
means anything alone. The useful photo frames a **paragraph**: sharp enough to read,
wide enough to mean something.

Embedding has the same lens problem. A vector is a fixed-size summary (768 numbers, no
matter the input length):

| Photo | Chunking choice | What goes wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole page from afar | embed whole documents | **dilution** — one vector averages many meanings; the login paragraph drowns in the timeline |
| One word per photo | embed sentences/lines | **fragmentation** — "it fails after that" embeds beautifully and means nothing without the previous sentence |
| The paragraph shot | embed ~paragraph-sized chunks | the working default — one coherent *idea* per vector |
| Overlapping shots | chunks share an overlap margin | ideas that straddle a cut exist whole in at least one chunk |

💡 **Mental model: a chunk is the unit of findability — you can only ever retrieve what
you decided to make retrievable.** No ranking cleverness recovers a meaning that was
averaged away at indexing time. (Note the rhyme with Day 48: memory quality is decided
at *write* time. Same law, one layer down.)

Overlap deserves its one honest sentence: it costs index size (same text embedded
twice at the margins) to buy insurance against ideas that straddle a cut — Day 20's
compaction `overlap_size` was the same trade in the session world.

### AG-14b — Top-k and the threshold: recall vs noise, measured

Raising k raises the chance the right chunk is *somewhere* in the results (recall) —
and simultaneously stuffs the context with near-misses (Day 19's budget, Day 49's
polite-garbage problem). The only honest way to set k is to measure both on **your**
corpus with **your** queries. So today builds the smallest real evaluation harness:

- a **gold set** — a handful of (query → ref-that-should-be-found) pairs, written by
  hand, including paraphrases that share no keywords with their targets *and* two
  "nothing should be found" queries;
- **hit@k** — for each strategy and k: how often the gold ref appears in the top k;
- the **threshold** — read the score gap between genuine hits and the best
  nothing-query score; the honest cutoff lives between them.

This is a five-minute, zero-cost version of what Phase 12 industrializes — evals as
tests (Principle 11 warming up). The numbers will be small and slightly noisy; the
*ritual* — change a knob, re-run, compare — is the thing being installed today.

### AG-14c — When RAG is the wrong tool

The most senior sentence in retrieval is "we don't need retrieval here." The decision
table (learn it well enough to reproduce on a whiteboard):

| The situation | The right tool | Why RAG loses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corpus fits in the window (with room to think) | **just include it** (Day 19 curation) | retrieval adds a miss-probability to data you could have simply handed over |
| Structured/aggregate question ("how many P1s last week?") | **SQL** (Day 39 tools) | vectors can't count, join, or group — nearest-neighbor ≠ WHERE clause |
| Exact-match lookup ("show ticket 4633") | **keyed lookup** | embedding an ID to find an ID is a Rube Goldberg lookup table |
| Freshness-critical ("is the vendor down NOW?") | **live tools** (Day 15/16) | the index serves yesterday, confidently |
| Fuzzy meaning-match over a large, growing corpus | **RAG** ✅ | this — and only this — is the home game |

Sutra genuinely needs RAG for exactly one job: "has anything *like* this happened
before?" over an unbounded archive. That's why AG-33 got one honest day, not a phase —
and why the researcher node in Phase 8 will hold retrieval *and* SQL *and* live tools,
choosing per question. The tool-design lesson of Day 11 (sharp tools, clear
descriptions) is what makes that choice learnable by the model.

Add to `sutra/retrieval.py` (new functions only — Day 49's code untouched):

```python
# --- Day 50 additions: chunking + tuning harness ---------------------------

LONG_DOC_REF = "kb:KB-900"                               # today's lab prop (long doc)
LONG_DOC = (
    "Incident review, March. Timeline: 09:02 monitoring alert fired; 09:10 "
    "on-call paged; 09:40 rollback attempted; 10:15 vendor contacted; 11:00 "
    "traffic normalized. Costs were reviewed and postmortem scheduled. "
    # …imagine ~15 more sentences of timeline/process filler…
    "Root cause analysis: the session cookie was issued without the SameSite "
    "and Secure attributes, so cross-site redirects silently dropped it and "
    "users were returned to the login page in a loop. Mitigation: KB-104."
)


def chunk(text: str, size: int = 280, overlap: int = 60) -> list[str]:
    """Cut text into overlapping, roughly-paragraph-sized pieces.

    Character-based on purpose: dumb, dependency-free, and good enough
    to MEASURE. (Fancier splitters exist; buy them only if the numbers
    say so — that's today's whole ethos.)

    Args:
        text: the document.
        size: target chunk length in characters (~2-4 sentences).
        overlap: how many characters neighboring chunks share.

    Returns:
        List of chunks covering the text, each ≤ size.

    Example:
        >>> parts = chunk("abcdefghij", size=4, overlap=2)
        >>> parts[0], parts[1]
        ('abcd', 'cdef')
    """
    if size <= overlap:                                  # would loop forever
        raise ValueError("size must exceed overlap")
    chunks, start = [], 0
    while start < len(text):
        chunks.append(text[start:start + size])          # one windowful…
        start += size - overlap                          # …then slide, keeping the margin
    return chunks


def demo_dilution() -> None:
    """Whole-doc vector vs its own best chunk, on one probing query. $0."""
    query = "users stuck in a loop back to the login page"
    qvec = embed([query])[0]
    whole = embed([LONG_DOC])[0]                         # the from-across-the-room photo
    parts = chunk(LONG_DOC)
    part_vecs = embed(parts)                             # the paragraph shots
    best_i, best_sim = max(
        enumerate(cosine(qvec, v) for v in part_vecs), key=lambda t: t[1]
    )
    print(f"whole-doc similarity : {cosine(qvec, whole):.3f}")
    print(f"best chunk similarity: {best_sim:.3f}  (chunk {best_i}: "
          f"{parts[best_i][:60]}…)")
```

**Step 3 — the gold set and the tuning table.** Add:

```python
# Hand-written gold set: paraphrases that share NO useful keywords with
# their targets, plus two nothing-queries for the threshold reading.
GOLD = [
    ("stuck in a loop back to the login page", "ticket:4610"),
    ("browser keeps signing the user out",     "ticket:4521"),
    ("password reset message never arrives",   "ticket:4633"),
    ("dashboard shows an empty white screen",  "ticket:4701"),
    ("cross-site redirect drops the cookie",   "kb:KB-900"),   # lives in ONE chunk
]
NOTHING = ["refund for double billing", "printer is on fire"]  # correct answer: absence


def hit_at_k(index_path: Path, k: int) -> float:
    """Fraction of GOLD queries whose target ref is in the top-k.

    Chunk refs count for their parent: 'ticket:4610#2' hits 'ticket:4610'.

    Example:
        >>> hit_at_k(INDEX_PATH, 3)
        0.8                                              # your numbers will vary
    """
    hits = 0
    for query, want in GOLD:
        got = search_in(index_path, query, k)             # like search(), parameterized path
        if any(ref.split("#")[0] == want for _, ref, _ in got):
            hits += 1
    return hits / len(GOLD)


def demo_tune() -> None:
    """The whole day in one table: strategy × k, plus the threshold reading."""
    print("hit@k        flat   chunked")
    for k in (1, 3, 5):
        flat = hit_at_k(INDEX_PATH, k)
        chka = hit_at_k(CHUNKED_INDEX_PATH, k)
        print(f"  k={k}      {flat:.2f}    {chka:.2f}")
    # Threshold reading: best score any nothing-query achieves…
    worst_noise = max(search_in(CHUNKED_INDEX_PATH, q, 1)[0][0] for q in NOTHING)
    # …vs the weakest score a genuine gold hit needed:
    print(f"best NOTHING-query score : {worst_noise:.3f}   <- garbage ceiling")
    print("→ set SIM_THRESHOLD comfortably above the ceiling, below real hits")
```

## 5 · 💥 Failure lab — the incident report that couldn't be found

> 🎬 **The scene:** the March incident review — the *one* document that explains the
> login-loop disease completely — is in the index. A perfect query arrives: "cross-site
> redirect drops the cookie." Rank 7. Below two pleasantry-dense tickets. The one
> document written to answer this exact question is effectively invisible.
> 😬 **The naive fix:** raise k to 10. The report now *appears* — along with six
> irrelevant neighbors that bloat every prompt (Day 19's tax, paid on every single
> query, forever) — and rank 7 of 10 still loses most of the model's attention.
> 💥 **Why it fails:** the problem was never the ranking — it was the *photograph*. One
> vector for a 20-paragraph document is an average; the root-cause paragraph
> contributes 5% of the direction, timeline filler contributes the rest. The query is
> asked to match a smoothie and identify one ingredient. Raising k treats an indexing
> disease with a query-time bandage and charges the bandage to your token budget.
> 💡 **The insight:** **retrievability is decided at indexing time; k only decides how
> much noise you'll pay to paper over it.** Chunk so each vector holds one idea, then
> *measure* — today's table showed the same document jumping to rank 1 when its
> paragraph got its own vector, at k=1, with zero extra tokens. Fix the lens, not the
> crop.

## 6 · 🎤 Interview corner

1. **"How do you choose a chunking strategy?"** — Start from what a vector is: a
   fixed-size average of the input's meaning. Whole documents dilute (the key paragraph
   drowns); single sentences fragment (pronouns lose their referents); so the default
   is paragraph-sized chunks with a modest overlap for ideas that straddle cuts. Then —
   the part that separates practice from blog posts — measure on your own corpus: I
   keep a hand-written gold set of paraphrase queries and compare hit@k across
   strategies; chunking won on mine specifically because one long incident report was
   unfindable as a single vector and trivially findable as paragraphs.
2. **"How did you pick k? And what happens below your similarity threshold?"** — k came
   from the same gold-set table: I take the smallest k where hit-rate plateaus, because
   every extra neighbor is context-budget spent on near-misses. The threshold came from
   the gap between genuine hits and the best score a deliberately-unanswerable query
   achieves — results under it get explicitly marked, and the agent's instruction
   treats marked results as absence. Cosine ranking always returns k rows; without a
   threshold, an empty corpus still "finds" things, and that's how fabricated citations
   happen.
3. **"Your PM wants 'RAG on everything.' Push back."** — Retrieval earns its place for
   fuzzy meaning-matching over corpora too big for the window — that's one workload,
   not all of them. Aggregations belong to SQL (vectors can't count), exact lookups to
   keys, freshness to live tools, and small corpora belong *in the prompt* — retrieval
   there just adds a way to miss. My triage system's researcher holds all four tools
   and picks per question; the design review question isn't "where do we add RAG?" but
   "which questions are actually similarity searches?"
