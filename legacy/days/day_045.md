something real that you care about: your own repo.

## 3 · 📚 Concepts

### MCP-24 — The audit method: trust is a table, not a feeling

**The restaurant-inspector story.** A health inspector doesn't ask the chef "is the
kitchen clean?" — chefs always say yes, sincerely. The inspector carries a **fixed list**
(fridge temperature, cutting boards, dates on containers), *measures* each item, and
writes down what the thermometer said — not what anyone felt. Three properties make the
list work, and they transfer exactly:

1. **Fixed in advance** — the list doesn't bend to what's convenient to check today.
2. **Probe per row** — wherever possible, a *command* whose output is the evidence, not an
   opinion. (You built these probes all phase without calling them that: the two-instance
   rig, the policy tests, the raw `tools/list`.)
3. **Dated findings, binary disposition** — every issue is *fixed* or *filed with an
   owner-note*. "We should look into that sometime" is the third state; it is forbidden.

💡 **Mental model: an audit converts "I'm sure it's fine" into rows you can re-run.** The
deliverable isn't confidence — it's a *repeatable procedure* plus today's results.

The domains an MCP audit must cover (this becomes your checklist's spine):

| Domain | The question | Built on day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inventory | is the policy REGISTRY the complete truth of what we mount/serve? | 40, 42 |
| Transport & revision | Streamable HTTP/stdio only? 2026-07-28 on the wire? SSE nowhere? | 33, 43 |
| Statelessness | does the two-instance probe still pass, tasks included? | 43 |
| Tool surface | filters least-privilege? descriptions re-read? pins current? | 39, 40 |
| Auth posture | per server: what protects it, or an explicit "local-only, N/A — revisit at deploy"? | 37 |
| State & long jobs | handles explicit? backing store shared? writes idempotent-or-gated? | 36, 43, 44 |
| Client resilience | deadlines, retry line, catalog cache — tests green? chaos replay passes? | 44 |
| Deprecated features | any Roots/Sampling/Logging in servers we touch? migration noted? | 38 |
| Provenance | every third-party thing: source, version, date recorded? | 39, 40 |

### MCP-25 — Executing without flinching

Method is cheap; execution is where audits die. The three failure modes to guard against
in yourself today: **auditor's optimism** ("that row was fine last month" — run the probe
anyway), **scope mercy** ("the rogue server is just a prop" — props get a row too; their
job description is *being dangerous*, so verify they're contained), and **finding
shame** (a finding is the audit *working* — a clean sheet with zero findings on a
six-server estate is evidence of a bad audit, not a good estate).

### OPS-09 — The gate ritual

A phase gate (master plan §15) is the audit plus the *ledger* half: every day rowed in
PROGRESS, trace showing zero open IDs through Phase 6, `make check` green repo-wide, the
freshness check run against the live world, and any deviation amended into the plan **before**
Phase 7 begins. The gate's product is the right to start Phase 7 without a backlog of
quiet lies.

## 5 · 💥 Failure lab — audit theater

> 🎬 **The scene:** a future gate day, month six. Time is short. The checklist is long.
> You remember Day 43's rig passing vividly. The row says "Stateless: two-instance suite
> passes." You write ✅ from memory and move to the next row. Every row, same story:
> checked-by-recollection. Twenty minutes, all green, commit, done.
> 😬 **The naive fix:** "the checklist was completed" — and formally, it was. Every box
> has a mark in it.
> 💥 **Why it fails:** somewhere in Phase 7, persistent sessions (Day 47) added a database
> the task registry doesn't share, or a new server got mounted in a hurry with
> `tool_filter=None` "temporarily." The probes would have caught both in seconds — but no
> probe ran. The marks in the boxes measured your *memory*, which was accurate about the
> past and silent about the present. This is audit theater: the form of inspection
> without the thermometer, and it is *worse* than no audit, because the green sheet now
> actively vouches for the drift.
> 💡 **The insight:** **an audit row is a probe you run, or it is a lie you sign.** The
> defense is structural, not moral: keep every probe one command long (you built them
> that way all phase), wire the mechanical ones into `make check` so they run without
> being remembered, and let the audit doc's job shrink to what only a human can do —
> reading descriptions, challenging the idempotency set, dating the freshness answers.
> Cheap probes get run; expensive probes get remembered. Keep them cheap.

## 6 · 🎤 Interview corner

1. **"How would you audit an agent system's MCP integrations?"** — With a fixed-in-advance
   checklist walking nine domains — inventory against a single policy source-of-truth,
   transport and spec revision on the wire, statelessness under a two-instance chaos
   probe, least-privilege tool surface with pinned descriptions, per-server auth posture,
   explicit-handle state in shared storage, client deadlines and the read/write retry
   line, deprecated-feature scan, and provenance rows. Every row has an executable probe
   where possible; findings are dated and binary — fixed or filed. I ran exactly this
   against my own six-asset estate and it surfaced real findings on the first pass.
2. **"What's a phase gate and why bother on a solo project?"** — A gate converts 'each
   piece worked when I built it' into 'the system works now, verified against a written
   standard' — plus a freshness check against the live world, because specs and free
   tiers move under you (my plan's flagship protocol rewrote itself two weeks before I
   started). Solo is where gates matter *most*: there's no colleague to catch your drift,
   so the checklist plays the colleague.
3. **"Your audit came back clean — good news?"** — Suspicious news. On a first pass over
   a multi-server estate, zero findings usually measures the auditor, not the estate:
   optimism, scope mercy, or checked-by-recollection. My first audit filed several
   findings by design — TODOs I'd deliberately carried were dispositioned, not
   forgotten. A finding is the audit working.
