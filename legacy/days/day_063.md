## 3 · 📚 Concepts

### SEC-05 — The gate policy: deciding *what*, *who*, and *what remains*

**The story.** Two banks. Bank A's vault rule lives in the tellers' heads: "big
withdrawals should probably get a manager." Every teller draws "big" somewhere else;
the manager's approval is a nod; afterwards, nobody can say who nodded at what. Bank B
has a laminated card at every counter: *these four operations, above these thresholds,
require a countersignature; the signature is a form; the forms are filed.* Bank B is not
slower — tellers stopped guessing. And when something goes wrong, Bank B can answer the
only question that matters afterwards: *who decided, knowing what?*

Sutra has been Bank A about approvals. Becoming Bank B takes three decisions:

**Decision 1 — the inventory (what can even write?).** Walk every tool the agents can
reach and sort by effect. Do it tonight in one table — this doc seeds it; your repo may
have grown extras, add rows, never "misc":

| Tool | Effect | Blast radius | Reversible? | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `lookup_ticket`, `search_kb`, retrieval, resources | read | none | — | free |
| `record_triage` / state writes | internal note | just us | yes (overwrite) | free, review-after via logs |
| `reindex_start` | rebuild an index | just us | yes (re-run) | free — but rate-limited (quota, not safety) |
| `escalate_to_engineer` (Day 62) | pages a person | one colleague | socially awkward, not harmful | **threshold gate**: P1 auto-allowed at night, else approve-before |
| `close_ticket` | customer-visible state change | a customer | reopen exists, trust doesn't | **approve-before, always** |
| `post_reply` (future, Phase 11+) | words leave the building | a customer, in writing | no | **approve-before, always** — the rule exists *before* the tool does |

That last row is the security posture in miniature: the gate is declared before the
capability ships (Principle 13 — blast radius before capability).

**Decision 2 — who may approve, and how their answer is bound.** An approval is four
facts welded together, or it is nothing: *who* decided (an operator identity, even if
today it's just a name in an env var), *what exactly* they saw (the full payload —
ticket id, the drafted reply, the duplicates list — hashed, so later nobody can claim
the payload changed after approval), *what they answered* (the typed verdict + optional
amendments, Day 62's `approved_days` lesson), and *when*. Day 41's sketch already
demanded this — its audit row said "who clicked, what exact payload, which template
version rendered." Today that row becomes columns.

**Decision 3 — what remains (the audit trail outlives everything).** Sessions get
compacted (Day 20), logs rotate (Day 22), processes die (Day 61). The approval record
is the one thing that must survive them all — it answers for you months later. Hence
today's central design move: **approvals get their own store**, not a corner of
somebody else's.

💡 **Mental model: an approval is a *record*, not a *moment*.** Day 62 built the moment
(the pause, the verdict). Today builds the record. The moment can ride any mechanism
and be replaced next release; the record's schema is a promise you keep for years.

### ADK-47 — The mechanism map: one gate, three doors, chosen per situation

ADK hands you two pause mechanisms; MCP hands a third; Day 41 sketched a fourth *skin*.
Bad design picks a favorite. Good design assigns each to the situation it fits — and
routes them all into the same record:

| Door | Verified | Survives process death? | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tool confirmation (`require_confirmation`, `adk_request_confirmation`) | Day 62 | **No** (in-memory sessions only) | a live operator watching the run — the fast path |
| Graph `RequestInput` (node yields; workflow pauses durably; resume by `invocation_id`) | Days 61–62 | **Yes** — ordinary persisted events | the overnight gate: Sutra's default, built Day 64 |
| MCP `input_required` retry-with-answers (Day 37's `close_ticket` dance; elicitation) | Day 37 | Yes (stateless protocol — state in payload) | callers *outside* our runtime hitting `sutra-mcp` directly |
| MCP Apps panel (Day 41's sketch) | Day 41 (design) | n/a — it's a *skin* over a door | rendering the question as buttons where the host supports it; text elicitation elsewhere |

The unifying rule: **every door deposits into `data/approvals.db`.** However the
question was asked and answered, one table can replay history. Tomorrow's build wires
the second door end-to-end; the first stays available for live runs; the third already
half-exists in `close_ticket` and gains the store tomorrow; the fourth waits for an
Apps-capable host and loses nothing by waiting.

Why a hand-rolled store instead of leaning harder on ADK? Three honest reasons, in
order of weight: (1) the *record* must outlive sessions and survive compaction —
storing it inside session state couples audit lifetime to conversation lifetime, which
Decision 3 forbids; (2) the Day 62 limitation means the one ADK feature shaped like
approvals can't reach our durable sessions anyway; (3) the store is ~80 lines of
stdlib on a pattern you've built twice (tasks rack Day 36→43, processed_writes Day 60)
— this is not an abstraction adventure, it's a third table in a WAL file.

**Step 2 — the policy table, as a table in code.** Start `sutra/approvals.py` with the
policy — data first, mechanics second:

```python
"""Day 63 - approval gates: the policy and the durable record.

One module answers three questions for the whole repo:
  1. Does this tool need a human?          -> POLICY / policy_for()
  2. Where does a pending question live?   -> data/approvals.db (WAL)
  3. What remains afterwards?              -> the same row, decided + audited

The MOMENT of asking (tool confirmation, graph RequestInput, MCP
input_required) is chosen per-situation - Day 63 doc section 3 has the map.
Every door deposits here. No door bypasses here.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import hashlib
import json
import sqlite3
import uuid
from dataclasses import dataclass
from pathlib import Path

from sutra.logging_setup import get_logger          # house rule since Day 22

log = get_logger(__name__)

_DB = Path("data/approvals.db")                     # its OWN file: audit outlives
                                                    # sessions, tasks, and logs


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class GatePolicy:
    """One laminated-card row: how a single tool is gated.

    Args:
        tool: the tool name this row governs.
        gate: "never" (free), "always", or "threshold" (ask the predicate).
        reason: one sentence a reviewer reads - why this verdict (SEC-05).

    Example:
        >>> POLICY["close_ticket"].gate
        'always'
    """
    tool: str
    gate: str                                       # "never" | "always" | "threshold"
    reason: str


POLICY: dict[str, GatePolicy] = {                   # THE table from doc section 3.
    "lookup_ticket":  GatePolicy("lookup_ticket", "never",
                                 "read-only; no blast radius"),
    "search_kb":      GatePolicy("search_kb", "never",
                                 "read-only; no blast radius"),
    "reindex_start":  GatePolicy("reindex_start", "never",
                                 "internal + re-runnable; quota-limited, not gated"),
    "escalate_to_engineer": GatePolicy("escalate_to_engineer", "threshold",
                                 "pages a human; P1 flows, lower asks first"),
    "close_ticket":   GatePolicy("close_ticket", "always",
                                 "customer-visible; reopen exists, trust doesn't"),
    "post_reply":     GatePolicy("post_reply", "always",
                                 "words leave the building; gate predates the tool"),
}


def policy_for(tool: str) -> GatePolicy:
    """Look up a tool's gate row - unknown tools are GATED, not free.

    Example:
        >>> policy_for("some_new_write_tool").gate
        'always'
    """
    return POLICY.get(tool,                         # fail CLOSED: a tool nobody
                      GatePolicy(tool, "always",    # classified is a tool nobody
                                 "unclassified tool: fail closed"))  # may run alone
```

✅ *Verify:* `python -c "from sutra.approvals import policy_for; print(policy_for('close_ticket').gate, policy_for('brand_new').gate)"`
prints `always always` — the second one is the fail-closed default earning its keep.

**Step 3 — the durable store.** Same file — the record and its three verbs:

```python
_SCHEMA = """CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS approvals (
  approval_id  TEXT PRIMARY KEY,                    -- our claim ticket
  tool         TEXT NOT NULL,                       -- what wants to run
  payload      TEXT NOT NULL,                       -- EXACTLY what the human saw (JSON)
  payload_sha  TEXT NOT NULL,                       -- ...pinned, so it can't drift
  invocation_id TEXT,                               -- which paused run waits on this
  status       TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'pending',     -- pending|approved|rejected
  verdict_by   TEXT,                                -- WHO decided (operator name)
  verdict_payload TEXT,                             -- amendments (Day 62: 3 of 5 days)
  requested_at TEXT DEFAULT (datetime('now')),
  decided_at   TEXT
)"""


def _conn() -> sqlite3.Connection:
    """WAL connection to the approvals store (creates schema on first use)."""
    conn = sqlite3.connect(_DB)
    conn.execute("PRAGMA journal_mode=WAL")         # Day 43's lesson, third outing
    conn.execute(_SCHEMA)
    return conn


def request_approval(tool: str, payload: dict, invocation_id: str | None = None) -> str:
    """File the question. Returns an approval_id the paused run keeps.

    Example:
        >>> aid = request_approval("close_ticket",
        ...     {"ticket_id": "4610", "draft_reply": "...", "duplicates": []})
        >>> len(aid) > 0
        True
    """
    aid = f"appr_{uuid.uuid4().hex[:12]}"           # unguessable, greppable
    blob = json.dumps(payload, sort_keys=True)      # canonical form -> stable hash
    conn = _conn()
    conn.execute(
        "INSERT INTO approvals (approval_id, tool, payload, payload_sha, invocation_id)"
        " VALUES (?,?,?,?,?)",
        (aid, tool, blob, hashlib.sha256(blob.encode()).hexdigest(), invocation_id),
    )
    conn.commit(); conn.close()
    log.info("approval_requested", extra={"approval_id": aid, "tool": tool})
    return aid


def decide(aid: str, approved: bool, by: str, amendments: dict | None = None) -> None:
    """Record the human's verdict - once. A second decision is an error, loudly.

    Example:
        >>> decide(aid, True, by="nisha", amendments={"close_duplicates": False})
    """
    conn = _conn()
    row = conn.execute("SELECT status FROM approvals WHERE approval_id=?", (aid,)).fetchone()
    if row is None:
        conn.close(); raise KeyError(f"no such approval {aid!r}")
    if row[0] != "pending":                         # decided is decided - the audit
        conn.close(); raise ValueError(f"{aid} already {row[0]}")  # trail never flips
    conn.execute(
        "UPDATE approvals SET status=?, verdict_by=?, verdict_payload=?,"
        " decided_at=datetime('now') WHERE approval_id=?",
        ("approved" if approved else "rejected", by,
         json.dumps(amendments or {}), aid),
    )
    conn.commit(); conn.close()
    log.info("approval_decided", extra={"approval_id": aid, "approved": approved, "by": by})


def status(aid: str) -> dict:
    """Read one approval back, verdict and all (the paused run's poll)."""
    conn = _conn()
    row = conn.execute("SELECT tool,payload,status,verdict_by,verdict_payload"
                       " FROM approvals WHERE approval_id=?", (aid,)).fetchone()
    conn.close()
    if row is None:
        raise KeyError(f"no such approval {aid!r}")
    return {"tool": row[0], "payload": json.loads(row[1]), "status": row[2],
            "verdict_by": row[3],
            "verdict": json.loads(row[4]) if row[4] else None}
```

## 5 · 💥 Failure lab — the approval that approved something else

> 🎬 **The scene:** months from now, an angry customer thread: "why was my ticket closed
> with this dismissive reply?" You pull the audit trail like a pro: approval `appr_9f…`,
> status approved, verdict_by: the on-call lead. Case closed — the human signed it.
> Except the lead swears the draft they approved was *polite*. And they're right.
> 😬 **The naive fix:** the original gate stored `{"ticket_id": "4610"}` and nothing
> else — "the payload's in the session anyway, why copy it?" Between the approval and
> the send, the graph *re-ran the drafting node* (an at-least-once resume — Day 61
> taught you those are normal), producing a new, worse draft. The human approved draft
> A; the system sent draft B; the audit row, pointing at a session that has since been
> compacted (Day 20), can no longer say which was which.
> 💥 **Why it fails:** the approval was bound to a *reference*, and the referent moved.
> Any gate that stores less than what the human actually saw is approving a pointer,
> not a decision — and pointers dangle.
> 💡 **The insight:** **an approval must pin the exact bytes it blesses.** That's why
> the store keeps the full payload *and* its hash, and why tomorrow's executing gate
> re-hashes what it's about to do and refuses on mismatch — a re-drafted reply after
> approval isn't an optimization, it's a *new question*. (Day 40 pinned tool
> descriptions against rug-pulls; same immune response, one layer up.)

Tested tonight in miniature: the payload-hash test in step 6 is this scene, 20 lines
tall.

## 6 · 🎤 Interview corner

1. **"How do you decide which agent actions need human approval?"** — Two axes: blast
   radius (whose day changes if it's wrong) and reversibility (does undo exist). I keep
   the verdicts in a policy table in code — read-only tools free; internal reversible
   writes free but audited; customer-visible or irreversible actions always gated; and
   a threshold class where a predicate decides (severity-based paging). Two design
   details I'd defend hard: unknown tools *fail closed* — an unclassified tool is
   gated by default — and the gate for `post_reply` existed before the tool did,
   because declaring the brake before shipping the capability is the whole game.
2. **"What's actually *in* an approval record?"** — Four welded facts: who decided, the
   exact payload they saw (stored in full and hashed, so post-approval drift is
   detectable and the executing side can refuse on mismatch), the typed verdict with
   any amendments, and timestamps. Stored in its own database with append-only
   semantics — a decided row can never flip — because sessions get compacted and logs
   rotate, but the audit trail answers for you months later.
3. **"Why didn't you just use your framework's built-in confirmations?"** — I do use
   them — as the *moment*, for live operators. But the record can't live there: the
   feature is experimental and doesn't ride database-backed sessions, and more
   fundamentally, coupling audit lifetime to conversation lifetime is wrong even when
   the feature isn't. So the architecture is: many doors (tool confirmation, the graph
   runtime's durable request-input, the MCP input_required dance), one deposit box.
   Mechanisms rotate with framework versions; the record's schema is the promise.
