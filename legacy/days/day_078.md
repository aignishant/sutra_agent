## 3 · 📚 Concepts

### OPS-14 — Quota-aware scheduling: the night is when budgets are won or lost

**The story.** A bakery's ovens are cheapest to run at night — if the baker remembers
that the morning rush needs the same gas line. Schedule the heavy bake at 02:00 and
the croissants at 06:00 and both fit; run them together and the pressure drops on
both. Free tiers are gas lines: RPM is pressure per minute, RPD is the tank for the
day, and **the tank refills on a clock you don't control** (midnight Pacific for
Gemini — `TODO(confirm the reset hour in the AI Studio view; record it)`). Quota-aware
scheduling is simply: put the spend that doesn't need a human where it can't collide
with the spend that does.

Phase 11's concrete version, which the gate audits line by line:

| Spend | When | Lane | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| re-index | night | local Ollama embeddings | zero quota — the index is free to rebuild nightly |
| evals (Day 82 fills) | night, right after the daily reset | `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` via the Quota-Router | the full set is the day's biggest spender; run it when the tank is full and nobody is waiting |
| digest | night | none | rows → Markdown, no model |
| standup | morning | text lane (SSE) by default; Live briefly | a human is present; the voice is the value; Live sessions are counted and capped |
| triage (Phase 8–9) | day | Flash + Groq + OpenRouter `:free` routed by headroom (Day 70) | the interactive work gets the daytime pressure |

And the honesty rule that makes it *scheduling* rather than hoping: **every job that
spends asks the ledger first** (`RunContext.ledger.remaining`), the nightly **records
what it spent** (the digest's quota section), and the gate **reads the numbers back**
(step 4). A budget nobody reconciles is a wish.

💡 **Mental model: quota-aware scheduling is putting the unattended spend where the
tank is full and the attended spend where the human is — and reconciling both against
the ledger.**

## 5 · 💥 Failure lab — the gate you can pass by hand

> 🎬 **The scene:** Day 78 morning. You check `data\nightly\` — empty. The laptop was
> closed at 02:30. You have a full day planned; Phase 12 is waiting. You run
> `python -m sutra.nightly run` yourself, the ledger fills, the digest appears, every
> row in §7 can be ticked. Technically.
> 😬 **The naive fix:** tick them. The code is the same code; the job *would* have run.
> 💥 **Why it fails:** the gate's claim is not "the code works" — Day 73's tests proved
> that. The claim is "**the desk works without you**," and the only evidence for that
> is a run you didn't start. Passing it by hand certifies the wrong property, and the
> first time it matters (Day 82: the full evalset rides the nightly; Day 83's gate
> expects a week of eval history) you discover a scheduling environment that never
> worked — at the worst time, with a longer history to redo. Unattended properties need
> unattended evidence; that's the whole reason Day 65 made drills out of gates.
> 💡 **The insight:** **a gate certifies a property, not a checklist — if the evidence
> could only come from the system acting alone, wait for the system to act alone.**
> Fix the environment (wake-to-run, a waking hour), let a night pass, then close the
> gate with a timestamp you didn't type.
