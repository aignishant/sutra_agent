## 3 · 📚 Concepts

### AG-28 — A suite is green when it is honest, not when it is passing

**The story.** A driving school boasts a 100% pass rate. The examiner is the school's
own instructor; the route is the one the students practised; the test is the same every
week. Is the school good? You cannot tell — the number has stopped carrying information.
Now a different school: independent examiner, a route drawn fresh each time from a
large pool, results published with the spread across examiners, and the occasional
honest "we failed three this month, here's why." *That* 87% means something.

Day 65 taught the first half of this (rotate the drill or it measures the path it
rehearses). Evals add the second half: **a benchmark you never rotate is a benchmark you
are overfitting** — by accident, just by fixing whatever made it red. Four properties
make a suite *green in the honest sense*:

| Property | How the gate checks it | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scale** | ≥60 cases, ≥15 fear cases, ≥1 from every ticket family in the archive | seven cases can be memorised by a prompt edit; sixty cannot |
| **Rotation** | ≥10 cases written *today*, never run before; fear cases include one new injection wording | the suite grades the system, not last week's exam |
| **Spread** | every metric reported mean ± spread over `num_samples: 3`; deltas judged against Day 81's floor | a bare number is a single sample pretending to be a fact |
| **Honesty** | vetoes = 0 is *required*; flakes are *listed and explained*, never retried to green; the baseline row sits under Sutra's row | green that can't be red is decoration |

💡 **Mental model: the gate asks "is the *instrument* trustworthy?" before it asks "did
the agent pass?"** An untrustworthy green is worse than an honest red — it is a claim
Phase 13 will build on (tracing, deploy) and Phase 14 will publish.

Two more things the gate must leave behind, because later phases depend on them:

- **The score table** — in `docs/runbooks/phase12_gate.md`, copied from the record:
  per metric, mean ± spread, threshold, baseline. Day 92's security review and Day 96's
  retrospective both quote it.
- **The rotation pool** — `tests/evals/full/` is allowed to grow; cases are never
  *deleted* to improve a score (rename the folder `full/` to make that rule visible? no —
  write it in `tests/evals/README.md`: "cases are added or fixed-with-a-reason, never
  removed for score").

## 5 · 💥 Failure lab — the suite that got better by getting smaller

> 🎬 **The scene:** Phase 13 adds tracing; Phase 14 hardens. Each change re-runs the full
> set. A few cases are perennially borderline — the polite-injection fixture, the
> two-ids-in-one-message case, a multi-turn contradiction. Someone "cleans up the suite":
> those cases are "unrealistic," the references "ambiguous." Six cases are deleted. The
> score rises 0.81 → 0.88. The retrospective on Day 96 quotes 0.88.
> 😬 **The naive fix:** keep a changelog of deleted cases so it's "transparent."
> 💥 **Why it fails:** transparency about shrinking the exam does not make the grade
> comparable. The six hardest cases were the suite's *information* — the places Sutra
> is weakest are exactly where regressions will land first. Removing them did not make
> Sutra better; it made the instrument blind where it was most needed, and the 0.88 is
> now a number about a different, easier test. Day 81's baseline would show it: the
> keyword router's score rose too.
> 💡 **The insight:** **the suite only grows. Cases are fixed with a written reason or
> left red; they are never removed for score.** A case that is genuinely wrong (the
> reference asserts something false) gets *corrected* — and the correction is a diff
> anyone can read. A case that is merely hard stays, red, as a known weakness in the
> runbook. Rotation adds; it never subtracts. And every score table carries its case
> count next to it, so 0.88 on 54 cases can never be mistaken for 0.81 on 60.

Write the rule into `tests/evals/README.md` now; add a zero-quota guard test that the
full set's case count never decreases (pin today's count in the test; raising the pin is
a deliberate commit).

## 6 · 🎤 Interview corner

1. **"How do you know your eval suite is trustworthy, not just passing?"** — Four
   properties I check at the gate: scale (60+ cases, a quarter of them adversarial),
   rotation (at least ten cases written fresh for the gate run, so the suite isn't
   grading last week's tuning), spread (every metric as mean ± spread across three
   judge samples, compared against a measured noise floor), and honesty (safety lines are
   vetoes, flakes are listed and explained — never retried to green — and a dummy
   baseline sits under the agent's row so the margin is visible). The suite only grows;
   cases are corrected with a reason, never deleted for score.
2. **"Walk me through the numbers from your last full eval run."** — [Your score table,
   from the runbook: case count, the six metrics as mean ± spread, the baseline row, the
   call cost and lane, the vetoes (zero), the flaky cases and what you concluded about
   each.] Then: what changed since the previous full run and which deltas were real
   versus inside the floor. If you can't say this from memory, you can say it from
   `data/evals/` in thirty seconds — that was the point of the record.
3. **"Running 780 model calls for an eval — how do you afford that on free tiers?"** —
   By treating quota as the budget it is: the whole suite runs on the cheapest lane,
   judges are sampled three times only at gates (once nightly), the run is split by case
   id across two daily quota windows, and the call estimate is computed from the config
   *before* the first call and checked against the quota ledger *after*. The nightly
   owns trend; gates own depth; CI owns merges. None of them is allowed to cost what the
   others cost.
