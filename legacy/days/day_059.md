## 3 · 📚 Concepts

### AG-21 — The runaway taxonomy: three shapes, three brakes

**The thermostat story.** A furnace with a broken thermostat does not *decide* to burn
the house down. It just never receives "stop." Every runaway you will ever debug is a
missing or unreachable "stop" — the interesting part is *which* stop was missing. Three
shapes cover nearly everything multi-agent:

| Shape | What it looks like | Why it happens | The brake that catches it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Never-satisfied** | one loop, condition never true (critic demands what writer can't give) | the exit condition depends on a model's judgment you don't control | the **loop counter** (Day 54: patience before quality) |
| **Ping-pong** | A delegates to B, B delegates back to A, forever | each agent's instruction makes the other look like the right owner; neither owns "done" | the **runtime fuse** (`max_llm_calls`) — counters can't see across agents, the fuse counts EVERYTHING |
| **Fan-out bomb** | one step spawns N sub-tasks, each spawns N more | recursion with model-decided branching; nobody bounded the tree | the **budget** (quota ledger / circuit breaker) — the only brake denominated in the thing you actually run out of |

Notice the escalation: the counter guards ONE loop; the fuse guards ONE run; the budget
guards THE DAY. They fail independently, which is why Sutra keeps all three (defense in
depth — Phase 10 will formalize the phrase; you are living it first).

💡 **Mental model: every brake answers a different question.** Counter: "has THIS loop
gone around too often?" Fuse: "has THIS run thought too much?" Budget: "has TODAY cost
too much?" A system missing any one of the three has a failure mode the other two cannot
see.

### SEC-04 — Containment as a security property

Why does the *security* curriculum care about runaways? Because "agent burns quota
forever" and "attacker makes agent burn quota forever" are the same event with different
authors. A runaway is a denial-of-service you built yourself; unbounded loops are an
attack surface *waiting for a prompt injection to pull the trigger* (Day 66 will pull it,
in the lab). Containment properties Sutra claims from today:

1. **Every loop has a counter, checked before the quality condition.** (Days 54/57 —
   already true; today you try to break it.)
2. **Every run has a fuse.** `max_llm_calls` set deliberately per entry point — small for
   demos, sized-with-reasons for production runs — never left to "whatever the default
   is."
3. **Every day has a budget with a breaker.** New today: a plugin whose `before_model`
   hook consults Day 24's `QuotaLedger` and *refuses the call* when headroom is gone —
   loudly, with an honest error, per Principle 10. Refusing to run IS an answer;
   inventing one is not.
4. **A kill story you can say out loud.** For every entry point: how do I stop it right
   now (Ctrl-C; the runner dies with the process), what survives (the session DB — Day 47;
   the task rack — Day 43), what resumes and what must not (Phase 9's whole subject —
   today you only have to *know the answers*, not automate them).

💡 **Mental model: capability and containment ship together, or the capability waits**
(Principle 13, graduating from principle to checklist). Phase 8 granted Sutra its biggest
capability yet — autonomous multi-station runs. The gate below does not open until the
containment story matches it.

## 5 · 💥 Failure lab — the brake that was "temporarily" loosened

> 🎬 **The scene:** a month from now. A hard ticket keeps shipping-with-flag after 2
> revisions; drafts genuinely improve on round 3 when you test by hand. Annoyed, you bump
> `MAX_REVISIONS` to 8 "for now" and — since the demos feel slow — raise the lab's
> `max_llm_calls` habit to "just use the default" (500). Both changes are one-line, both
> feel local, both ship in the same commit titled "tuning".
> 😬 **The naive fix:** that WAS the naive fix. Two of them, holding hands.
> 💥 **Why it fails:** brakes trade against each other invisibly. At `MAX_REVISIONS=2`,
> the fuse never mattered for the box (worst case ≈ 6 calls); at 8, one never-satisfied
> ticket burns ~18 calls — and with the fuse at its 500 default, NOTHING between 18 and
> your daily quota is watching. The nightly batch (Phase 11 is coming) hits three such
> tickets and Sutra spends its entire Gemini day before 1 a.m., failing honestly but
> *totally* — every OTHER ticket unserved. No single line was wrong; the SYSTEM of limits
> was, and no test failed because you tested tickets, not budgets.
> 💡 **The insight:** **brakes are a system with an invariant — every altitude bounded,
> and each bound sized against the one above it.** Loosen one, re-derive the others in
> the same commit: `MAX_REVISIONS` up? Then set the run fuse to worst-case-per-run, and
> check worst-case × nightly-batch-size against the ledger floor. Sutra's rule from today:
> any PR touching a brake constant must show the arithmetic in its description. One line
> of math beats one night of quota.

## 6 · 🎤 Interview corner

1. **"How do runaway multi-agent systems actually happen, and how do you stop them?"** —
   Three shapes: a never-satisfied loop (exit condition delegated to a model), ping-pong
   delegation (two individually-reasonable instructions that each name the other as
   owner), and fan-out bombs (model-decided recursion). Each is caught at a different
   altitude: per-loop counters checked before quality, a per-run LLM-call fuse, and a
   daily budget with a circuit breaker that refuses calls when headroom is gone. I've
   deliberately built all three runaways under a 4-call cap and watched each brake catch
   its own shape — including the rally no loop counter can see, because no single loop
   exists.
2. **"Why file runaways under security rather than reliability?"** — Because an unbounded
   loop is a self-inflicted denial-of-service today and an attacker-triggered one
   tomorrow — prompt injection just needs a loop to feed. Containment is a property of
   the system, not of good intentions: budgets as blast-radius control, refusal as a
   first-class honest outcome, and a kill story per entry point — what stops it, what
   state survives, what resumes. Capability and containment ship together or the
   capability waits.
3. **"What does a phase gate buy you?"** — A moment where 'works on my ticket' must
   become 'holds as a system': whole-repo checks green, zero open concept IDs, a
   freshness audit against the ecosystem (framework releases, protocol revisions,
   free-tier rosters — any of which can invalidate a pin overnight), and a re-run of the
   security audit against the NEW surface this phase added. The gate is also where
   brake-arithmetic gets re-derived — limits are a system, and gates are when the system
   gets re-proved.
